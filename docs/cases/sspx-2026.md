# Case: schism and the Society of St Pius X (1988–2026)

*Part `sspx` (with `lattice`). Tests SX1–SX19. Evaluated as of October 2026;
the Society's recourse against the 2 July 2026 decree had no reported outcome
in the sources consulted.*

"Is the SSPX in schism?" is not one question. It is several, of different
types, and most public argument fails because it answers one of them with
evidence for another. The checker separates them. Each one then has a
definite status, and the one that was really open between 2009 and 2026 is
easy to see.

## The questions, separated

1. **Was the 1988 act schismatic?** A juridical question. *Ecclesia Dei*
   settles it; the "state of necessity" is a tier-5 school (SX1).
2. **Was the 1988 excommunication incurred, and is it in force?** Settled in
   the external forum; remitted in 2009 (SX2, SX3). The remission was
   disciplinary and did not touch the classification of the act.
3. **What could the Society's priests do in 2018?** Absolve (by papal grant),
   assist at marriages (by delegation the papal-mandate letter allowed),
   nothing else legitimately; no canonical status (SX4).
4. **Was the Society, as such, in schism in 2018?** *Open.* Every "no" on
   record is an interview, which weighs nothing and cannot cleave. The
   preference therefore tips to "yes" on a single tier-5 thesis (SX6). This is
   the honest state of the question before 2026: it was asked constantly and
   never answered by an act that could answer it.
5. **Was the 2026 act schismatic?** Settled by the decree, the note and
   *Ecclesia Dei*'s typing of the act (SX10). The objection that c. 751
   requires a state of total repudiation, not an act, survives as a live
   shadow. It returns only if *Ecclesia Dei* and the 2026 acts are relaxed.
6. **Is the Society now in schism?** Settled by the 2026 acts (SX10).

## Incurred, presumed, declared, suspended

This is where the published commentary talks past itself, and where the
question of the missing process belongs. The checker keeps four things
apart:

- **Incurred.** A latae sententiae penalty is incurred by the act itself
  (c. 1314). Whether it *binds* a given person depends on imputability
  (cc. 1321–1324), including the necessity defence, and under c. 1324 §3 a
  latae sententiae penalty does not bind someone in those circumstances.
  The checker never computes this. It is the internal forum.
- **Presumed incurred.** In the external forum, once an external violation is
  established, imputability is presumed unless it appears otherwise
  (c. 1321 §4). The consecration is public and undisputed, so the six named
  bishops are presumed to have incurred (SX11).
- **Declared.** A declaration is a singular act about a named person, after a
  hearing (cc. 1342, 1720). It adds legal certainty and the further effects of
  c. 1331 §2. The decree names six. Whether its recital of prior warnings
  satisfied the hearing is disputed. The checker treats the act as valid
  (c. 124 §2) and keeps "hearing missing" as a live shadow of the decree (SX12).
- **Suspended.** A recourse against a decree that declares a penalty
  suspends the decree (c. 1353), not the penalty incurred by law. While the
  recourse is pending, the declaration's effects are suspended and the
  presumption stands (SX11). Once the recourse is decided they apply (SX12).
  Had the pope approved the decree in forma specifica, there would be no
  recourse at all (c. 333 §3; SX13).

**On "the process was superfluous because the facts were obvious."** The
nearest rule in the law is *Normae de gravioribus delictis* (2010) art. 21 §2
n. 2. It lets the most grave cases go straight to the pope when the
commission of the delict is manifest, but it keeps the right of defence, and
it concerns dismissal from the clerical state, not the declaration of a
censure. We know of no rule in the current Code that lets notoriety stand in
for the hearing when a penalty is declared. The checker states the reason as
a type rule: **notoriety proves the act; the hearing exists for the agent.**
The defence the hearing would test, necessity or a culpably mistaken belief in
it, is about the person, not the public fact. So reading "it was public" as
"no hearing was needed" is a violation (SX18).

## What follows for priests and laity

The note's statements about "the clergy" and "formal adherents" are
statements about classes. A class statement is not a declaration about any
person (SX16). Undeclared, the prohibitions of c. 1331 §1 yield to a
member of the faithful who asks for a sacrament for a just cause
(c. 1335 §2). Reading the note as a collective declaration is a violation
(SX17), and it is the only route on which "every priest's confessions are
invalid *because he is excommunicated*" goes through.

Adherence is never inferred from attendance (SX9). The Dicastery's own return
procedure says the same thing: lay imputability "must be assessed case by
case". The checker had this rule (non-diagnosis) before the procedure was
published; it was derived from the remarriage case, not from this one.

## Confessions and marriages: a hidden premise

The note says confessions and marriages before the Society's priests are
invalid. It does not say how. The checker finds exactly two routes:

1. **The censure route:** a priest's declared excommunication invalidates
   acts of governance (c. 1331 §2). No priest has been declared, so this
   route is closed (SX16).
2. **The lapse route:** the 2016 and 2017 grants lapse with the 2026 act. This
   works only if the grants are read as *conditioned* on the Society's standing
   when they were made. Read *unconditionally*, a dicastery's note contradicts
   a papal grant, which it cannot revoke (c. 135 §2; c. 21), and the checker
   flags the note as an invalid lower act (SX15).

Neither grant says which reading is right, so the faculty question is
**open**, and it turns on that one reading. The checker prefers the
conditioned reading, because it is the one on which the Dicastery's text is
well-typed. But a preference is not a resolution. Only a papal act settles it.
Supplied jurisdiction (c. 144) is not yet encoded.

## Is schism the right type?

The worry before 2026 was that "schism" would be applied on the basis of the
Society's rejection of *Dignitatis humanae* and other conciliar teaching.
That would be a category error: dissent from tier-3 teaching falls under
c. 752 and c. 1371, not c. 751 (SX8). The 2026 classification does not do
this. It rests on the act, consecrating bishops against the pope's explicit
will, which *Ecclesia Dei* 3 types as a practical rejection of the primacy.
That is a schism-type basis, and the checker reports no category error
(SX10).

In the communion lattice, 2026 removed exactly the bonds that 2016–17 had
added (the faculties). The other bonds are unchanged, so the Society is
still above the Orthodox in shared bonds. Nor did the decree change what
*kind* of body the Society is. It is an internal society, not a separated
Church, so *Dominus Iesus*'s Church/community classification does not apply
(SX19). If the Society were typed as a separated body, DI 17's criterion
(apostolic succession and a valid Eucharist) would call it a "true particular
Church", which nobody, the Society included, claims it is.

## What would change these results

- The recourse succeeding (part `recourse_upheld`). The declaration falls,
  "hearing missing" returns, and the presumption remains.
- The pope approving the decree (`decree_papal_approval`). The suspension ends.
- A papal act on the faculties. The reading of the grants is fixed, and the
  invalidity question closes one way or the other.
