# Case: schism and the Society of St Pius X (1988–2026)

*Part `sspx` (with `lattice`). Tests SX1–SX23. Evaluated as of 10 October
2026. The Society's recourse against the 2 July 2026 decree had no reported
outcome in the sources consulted.*

> **Revised.** The first version of this case ran the juridical questions
> through the authority graph, with tiers, weights and shadows. That was a
> type error. Canon law is not weighed. Given the facts and the norm in force,
> an effect follows or it does not, and canonists' opinion fills only a gap
> in the law, never in a penal case (c. 19). The juridical side now runs on
> the binary [canon-law layer](../canon-law-layer.md). Several results
> sharpened, and one reversed: the faculty question is no longer "open,
> turning on a reading", but follows from who may revoke a papal grant.

"Is the SSPX in schism?" is not one question. It is several, of two
different types, and most public argument fails because it answers one with
evidence for another:

- **How the acts are classified, and where the Society stands.** This is the
  authority side: what the acts of the Holy See say, at what tier, and how
  they bear on the communion lattice.
- **Who has incurred what, what is declared, and which sacraments are
  valid.** This is the juridical side: yes/no rules from the facts and the
  canons in force.

## The authority side

1. **Was the 1988 act schismatic?** *Ecclesia Dei* settles it; the "state of
   necessity" remains a tier-5 school (SX1). The excommunication was in force
   in 1990 and remitted in 2009 (SX2, SX3).
2. **Was the Society, as such, in schism in 2018?** *Open.* Every "no" on
   record is an interview, which is not a magisterial act. The preference
   therefore tips to "yes" on a single tier-5 thesis (SX6). This is the honest
   state of the question before 2026: it was asked constantly and never
   answered by an act that could answer it.
3. **The 2026 act and the Society now.** Both are settled by the decree, the
   note and *Ecclesia Dei*'s typing of the act (SX10).
4. **Is "schism" the right type?** Classifying the Society as schismatic for
   rejecting *Dignitatis humanae* would be a category error. Dissent from
   tier-3 teaching falls under c. 752 and c. 1365, not c. 751 and c. 1364
   (SX8). The 2026 classification does not do that. It rests on the act,
   consecrating bishops against the pope's explicit will, which *Ecclesia Dei*
   3 types as a practical rejection of the primacy (SX10).
5. **Adherence is never inferred from attendance** (SX9). The Dicastery's own
   return procedure says the same.

## The juridical side, computed

**Who has incurred what.** All six bishops of 2026 have committed the delict
of c. 1387, Bp Fellay included: a co-consecrator truly consecrates (Pius XII,
*Episcopalis consecrationis*, 1944), so "a Bishop who consecrates" reaches
him on the plain meaning. On *Ecclesia Dei*'s reading of c. 751 they have also
committed schism (c. 1364 §1). In the external forum, imputability is
presumed once the external violation is established (c. 1321 §4), so the
penalties are **presumed incurred** (SX11). Whether they *bind* depends on
imputability, including the necessity defence the Society claims (cc. 1323
n. 4, 1324 §1 n. 8 and §3). That is the internal forum, and the checker never
computes it. The 2009 remission does not reach 2026: it remitted a censure
for an earlier act (SX14).

If c. 751 is read as requiring a lasting state rather than an act, the
decree's title for Bp Fellay (c. 1364) is not derived, but c. 1387 reaches him
anyway (SX22).

**What is declared.** The decree names six persons and declares their
penalties. Declaration matters because it brings the further effects of
c. 1331 §2, among them that the person's acts of governance become invalid.
Three things bear on it:

- **The hearing.** A penalty declared by extrajudicial decree requires that
  the accused be informed of the accusation and proofs and allowed to defend
  himself, unless duly summoned and absent (c. 1342 §1; c. 1720 n. 1). The
  published decree recites prior warnings but does not show this. That is a
  **disputed fact**, and the checker computes it both ways. Where the hearing
  is missing, that is a **ground for recourse**. It is not a void, because a
  decree is presumed valid (c. 124 §2) until annulled (SX12).
- **"The process was superfluous because the facts were obvious."** The rule
  this recalls is c. 1717 §1: the *preliminary investigation* may be omitted
  if it seems entirely superfluous. Here it can be, because the consecration
  was a public liturgical act. But c. 1717 dispenses the investigation, not
  the hearing of c. 1720 n. 1. **Notoriety proves the act; the hearing exists
  for the agent**, whose defence (necessity) is about his mind, not the
  public fact. Reading "it was public" as "no hearing was needed" is a
  violation (SX19).
- **The recourse.** A recourse against a decree declaring a penalty suspends
  the decree (c. 1353), not the penalty incurred by law. While it is pending,
  the declaration's effects are suspended (SX11). Once it is decided they
  apply (SX12). Had the pope approved the decree in forma specifica, there
  would be no recourse at all (c. 333 §3; SX13).

**Confessions.** The 2016 grant lasts "until further provisions are made",
that is, until a competent authority revokes it. The note does not purport
to revoke it, and a dicastery could not revoke a papal grant if it did
(c. 135 §2; SX17). So, on the facts of record:

- An absolution by an undeclared priest of the Society after July 2026 is
  **valid**. It is also **licit** when the penitent asks for a just cause,
  because the prohibition of an undeclared censure yields to that request
  (c. 1335 §2). The note's "invalid" is contradicted (SX15).
- An absolution by one of the six bishops, while the declaration is
  suspended, is **valid but illicit**, unless the penitent asked for a just
  cause (SX15). Once the declaration takes effect it is **invalid**
  (c. 1331 §2 n. 2), and attempting it is itself a delict, with a latae
  sententiae interdict (c. 1379 §1 n. 2; SX20).
- **If the pope revoked the grant**, or made the note his own, the note would
  be right for every later absolution. Each attempt would then be a c. 1379
  delict, while nothing earlier is touched, the dying are always absolved
  (c. 976), and common error supplies the faculty (c. 144 §2; SX16).
- The only other route to "every confession is invalid" is to read the note
  as declaring the whole clergy excommunicated. A statement about a class is
  not a declaration about any person, so that reading is a **collective
  declaration** violation (SX18).

**Marriages.** The 2017 letter *permits* ordinaries to delegate. It delegates
no one. A marriage before a priest of the Society with no delegation lacks
the form (c. 1108 §1; c. 1111), and the note is right about it. A marriage
where an ordinary did delegate has the form, and the note is wrong about that
one (SX21).

**The lattice.** On the record, the faculties stand. 2026 changed the
persons' penal status, not the Society's juridical bonds or its sort (SX23).
It is still an internal society, not a separated Church, so *Dominus Iesus*'s
classification does not apply.

## What would change these results

| Event | Effect | Switch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The recourse is decided | The declaration takes effect: the six bishops' acts of governance become invalid | `recourse_decided_2026` |
| The recourse succeeds | The declaration is annulled; the presumption of incurring remains | `recourse_upheld_2026` |
| The pope approves the decree | No suspension; immediate effect | `decree_papally_approved` |
| The pope revokes the grants, or makes the note his own | Later absolutions invalid, and attempting them a delict | `note_as_revocation` + `note_papally_approved` |
| c. 751 read as a state, not an act | No c. 1364 delict; c. 1387 remains | `c751_state_reading` |
