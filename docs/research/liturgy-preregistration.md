# TTC-LITURGY(7)

## NAME

**ttc-liturgy** — the liturgy as witness, rite and development: one book,
two sources, the act that gives it and the prayer it holds; the prayer
weighs and never fixes; the substance kept, the rest law; rites as
documents in time, descending not in a line; predictions, committed
before encoding

## DESCRIPTION

Read as the rules stand (the base run as committed, e7aa748, only for the
outputs quoted here):

- **The liturgy only as governance.** The `liturgy` part types *Quo
  primum* (1570), the indult of 1984, *Summorum Pontificum* and
  *Traditionis custodes* as juridical sources on the use of the old Missal
  (LT1–LT3). Whether "lex orandi" in them is teaching or governance is a
  switch left open; typed as teaching, the two laws are read as teaching
  (LT5, a violation). Translations are governance under c. 838 (G1–G4).
- **No prayer is a witness.** No liturgical text is a source. As of 1500
  the Immaculate Conception is open and "no" is preferred, by the weight
  of three future Doctors at tier 5 against Scotus; the feast and its
  Office are not in the record. Rome's creed with the Filioque (1014) is
  not entered (TTC-SOURCES left it here).
- **The substance typed, changes unchecked.** `sacrament.lp` types each
  condition by its law (Trent, sess. XXI, ch. 2, DH 1728) and dispenses
  only the ecclesiastical. No act that changes a rite is checked against
  the substance. The matter of orders is the imposition of hands at every
  date (*Sacramentum ordinis*, DH 3859, entered undated); the porrection of
  the instruments is not in the base.
- **c. 846 not encoded.** A minister who changes a sacrament's form makes
  the sacrament fail for want of form, and nothing else is said.
- **Rites have no history.** No rite is a document; nothing descends from
  anything.

This file is committed and stamped before any of it is encoded. SCORING
will be appended below; nothing above it will be edited.

## THE TEXTS

Fixed here. Read on vatican.va where it holds them; Denzinger's numbering
otherwise.

- **SC 21**: "Nam Liturgia constat parte immutabili, utpote divinitus
  instituta, et partibus mutationi obnoxiis" (the liturgy is made up of
  immutable elements divinely instituted, and of elements subject to
  change). **SC 22**: its regulation "ab Ecclesiae auctoritate unice
  pendet", the Apostolic See and, as laws determine, the bishop (§1);
  territorial bodies of bishops within limits (§2); "nemo omnino alius,
  etiamsi sit sacerdos, quidquam proprio marte in Liturgia addat, demat,
  aut mutet" (§3). **SC 23**: no innovations "nisi vera et certa utilitas
  Ecclesiae id exigat", and new forms "ex formis iam exstantibus organice
  quodammodo crescant". **SC 8**: the earthly liturgy a foretaste of the
  heavenly.
- ***Mediator Dei*** (20 November 1947) n. 48: the liturgy "catholicam
  fidem absolute suaque vi non designat neque constituit" but, a
  profession of heavenly truths subject to the supreme Magisterium,
  "argumenta ac testimonia suppeditare potest"; taken in absolute and
  general terms, "Lex credendi legem statuat supplicandi". It cites the
  axiom's other form from "De gratia Dei «Indiculus»", naming no author.
- **The Indiculus** (DH 246): "ut legem credendi lex statuat
  supplicandi". "For centuries" affixed to Celestine I's letter to the
  bishops of Gaul as part of it, and "most probably the work of St. Prosper
  of Aquitaine" (*Catholic Encyclopedia*, "Celestine I"), compiled at Rome
  between 435 and 442 (M. Cappuyns, *Revue bénédictine* 41, 1929), or about
  431 (another reading). CCC 1124: "lex orandi, lex credendi (or *legem
  credendi lex statuat supplicandi*, according to Prosper of Aquitaine
  [5th cent.])"; "the Church believes as she prays"; liturgy "a
  constitutive element of the holy and living Tradition" (DV 8).
- **CCC 1125**: "no sacramental rite may be modified or manipulated at the
  will of the minister or the community. Even the supreme authority in the
  Church may not change the liturgy arbitrarily, but only in the obedience
  of faith". **CCC 1205**: an immutable part, divinely instituted, "of
  which the Church is the guardian, and parts that can be changed".
- **Trent**, sess. XXI, ch. 2 (DH 1728): the Church's power over the
  sacraments, "salva illorum substantia".
- **Florence**, *Exultate Deo* (22 November 1439, DH 1326): "Sextum est
  sacramentum Ordinis, cuius materia est illud, per cuius traditionem
  confertur ordo. Sicut presbyteratus traditur per calicis cum vino et
  patene cum pane porrectionem."
- **Pius XII, *Sacramentum ordinis*** (30 November 1947; AAS 40 (1948)
  5–7): if the porrection "ex Ecclesiae voluntate et praescripto ...
  aliquando fuerit necessaria ad valorem quoque, omnes norunt Ecclesiam
  quod statuit etiam mutare et abrogare valere" (n. 3); the matter of the
  diaconate, priesthood and episcopate "eamque unam esse manuum
  impositionem" (n. 4); "statuimus instrumentorum traditionem saltem in
  posterum non esse necessariam ad ... validitatem"; its dispositions
  "vim retroactivam non habent; quod si dubium aliquod contingat, illud
  huic Apostolicae Sedi erit subiiciendum". It names no date of force.
- **Sixtus IV, *Cum praeexcelsa*** (27 February 1477): a Mass and Office
  for the feast of the Conception.
- **CIC c. 2**: the Code for the most part "non definit ritus"; liturgical
  laws in force "vim suam retinent, nisi earum aliqua Codicis canonibus sit
  contraria". **c. 838** (as amended by *Magnum Principium*, 2017, in
  force 1 October 2017): the liturgy ordered by the Apostolic See and, as
  provided by law, the diocesan bishop (§1); the Apostolic See publishes
  the books and recognizes adaptations (§2); conferences prepare and
  approve translations, confirmed by the Apostolic See (§3); the diocesan
  bishop gives norms (§4). **c. 846 §1**: the liturgical books approved by
  competent authority are faithfully observed, "quapropter nemo in iisdem
  quidpiam proprio marte addat, demat aut mutet" (no one is to add, omit,
  or alter anything in them on one's own authority).
- **The Roman Missal**: *Quo primum* (14 July 1570); the typical edition of
  1962, by decree of the Congregation of Rites (23 June 1962), after
  *Rubricarum instructum* (25 July 1960); *Missale Romanum* (3 April 1969),
  in force "a die XXX proximi mensis Novembris ... id est a Dominica I
  Adventus"; the third typical edition (2002).
- **Benedict XVI**, letter to the bishops (7 July 2007): "There is no
  contradiction between the two editions of the Roman Missal. In the
  history of the liturgy there is growth and progress, but no rupture."
- **Rome's creed**: Leo III refused to add the Filioque to the creed and
  had it engraved without it on two silver shields at St Peter's (about
  810); the creed was first sung at Mass in Rome with the Filioque in 1014,
  at Henry II's coronation (John Paul II, audience of 7 November 1990).

## THE LAYER

Fixed here.

- **One book, two sources.** The act that gives a liturgical book (a
  constitution, a decree, a bull approving an Office) is governance:
  juridical, at the tier of its form, answering governance questions. The
  prayer the book holds is a witness: `liturgical_witness/1`, force truth,
  at the tier of the authority that approves it, of the character
  `liturgy`. The one never stands in for the other.
- **The prayer weighs and never fixes.** "Lex credendi legem statuat
  supplicandi" (*Mediator Dei*): belief rules prayer. A liturgical witness
  weighs at its tier and cleaves nothing, as a particular council's
  teaching does not (c. 753); it is not a teaching act, so no submission
  is owed to it as such. A liturgy treated as fixing a doctrine is refused
  (`lex_orandi_as_fixing`). The witness is what the Church prays (DV 8;
  the Indiculus, DH 246); the Immaculate Conception and the Assumption
  were argued from it, and settled by definitions.
- **Evidence for the ordinary universal magisterium, not the
  magisterium.** A claim that a teaching is taught by the ordinary
  universal magisterium is never preferred unless LG 25's conditions are
  shown apart from its claimant (rule 14). A prayer in the books of the
  universal Church, approved by bishops in communion with the Roman
  Pontiff, shows three of them: dispersed in communion, authentic
  teaching, faith or morals. It never shows the fourth, one position held
  as definitive: a collect proposes nothing to be held definitively. So a
  claim resting on the liturgy alone stays unshown, as *Mediator Dei* says
  the liturgy supplies "argumenta ac testimonia" and does not decide. The
  universal agreement the liturgy shows is LG 12's, "ab Episcopis usque ad
  extremos laicos fideles", made visible where the faithful pray.
- **A measure, never a verdict.** Each witness carries Vincent of Lérins'
  three counts: "quod ubique, quod semper, quod ab omnibus" (*Commonitorium*
  2): the Churches that pray it, its years in use at the evaluation date,
  and whether bishops and faithful alike pray it. A change by competent
  authority to an element so measured stands, and its measure is reported.
  The judgment that "vera et certa utilitas Ecclesiae" requires the change
  (SC 23; CCC 1125, "not ... arbitrarily") is the legislator's: supplied,
  never computed, as c. 1399's judgments are.
- **What binds and what lasts.** Law and teaching say what binds. That an
  element is loved (the chant, a hymn, a window) gives it no authority;
  that law sets it aside says nothing of its end, which is the last
  things' ("we will find them again, but freed of stain, burnished and
  transfigured", GS 39). Love is interior and never computed (rule 3).
- **The substance kept, the rest law** (SC 21; DH 1728). A change to a
  rite by the competent authority (c. 838) stands where the condition it
  changes is ecclesiastical, and is refused where the condition belongs to
  the substance (`change_to_substance`), whatever the authority. Which
  conditions belong to the substance stays the theological side's
  (`sacrament_conditions`).
- **Conditions dated.** A condition of validity set by the Church's will
  holds in its years. *Sacramentum ordinis* makes the matter of orders the
  imposition of hands, one, and the porrection of the instruments not
  necessary for validity "saltem in posterum", without retroactive force.
  Whether the porrection was required before is left open by its own words
  ("si ... aliquando fuerit necessaria"), Florence's decree for the
  Armenians having named it the matter: computed both ways, a doubt the
  constitution itself refers to the Apostolic See (rule 2).
- **c. 846 §1.** A minister who adds, removes or changes anything in a
  liturgical book's rite on his own authority acts illicitly; where the
  change touches the form, the sacrament fails as well (`sacrament.lp`).
  Two laws, two outputs.
- **Rites as documents.** A liturgical book is a body of texts with dates
  (TTC-SOURCES). Rites descend from rites (`derives_from/2`): an order, not
  a line, so a rite may have two parents. The order refuses a rite that
  descends from a later one, and one that descends from itself.
- **Development read, not settled.** "Organically" (SC 23) has no
  authentic interpretation. The reform of 1970 read as continuity or as
  rupture is a question on the authority graph, answered by what is in
  the record and no more; a theory of organic development may run on top,
  as the Grammar of the Icon does, and is not encoded here.
- **A new library**, `liturgy.lp`: rites, their descent and their
  changes, the witness's typing and measure. The witness's non-cleaving is a
  rule of `icon_authority.lp`; c. 846 sits in `canon_law.lp`.

## METHOD

Write `liturgy.lp`. In `icon_authority.lp`: a liturgical witness weighs,
cleaves nothing, is owed nothing. In `sacrament.lp`: the porrection, dated.
In `canon_law.lp`: c. 846 §1. Data in `icon_history.lp`, as parts loaded
beside the records they speak to, so that no earlier scenario changes: the
Office of the Conception approved by Sixtus IV (with `ic`); Rome's creed
of 1014 and Leo III's refusal to change the text (with `filioque`); the
Roman Missal's editions and descent; the reform's readings. Cases, all
HYPOTHETICAL: a claim of the ordinary universal magisterium for the
Immaculate Conception as of 1850, resting on the universal feast; the
measure of a long-kept element and a change to it; an element typed as
binding because it is loved; a liturgical book that drops water from baptism, and one
that changes the canonical form of marriage; ordinations to the
priesthood in 1900 and 1950, with and without the porrection; a minister
who says "We baptize", and one who changes a prayer that is not the form;
a rite that descends from a later one, and one from itself; a liturgy
treated as fixing a doctrine. Run the whole suite, the runtimes' boots and
the coverage index.

## PREDICTIONS

### LW: the witness

- **LW1.** One book, two sources. The Mass and Office of the Conception
  and the bull that establishes them (*Cum praeexcelsa*, 1477) enter
  separately: the bull answers no doctrinal question, and the prayer
  weighs on the Immaculate Conception at tier 3.
- **LW2.** As of 1500, with the prayer: the Immaculate Conception stays
  open (the witness cleaves nothing; *Grave nimis*, 1483, protects both),
  and "yes" is preferred by its weight at tier 3, where "no" was. B1–B5
  unchanged.
- **LW3.** Rome's creed with the Filioque (1014) weighs against "from the
  Father alone" as of 1100, which stays a live shadow of Leo I's letter
  alone. Leo III's refusal to change the creed's text (about 810) and
  Benedict VIII's change answer a governance question, the text sung in
  Rome: "without" as of 900, "with" as of 1100. Neither answers the
  doctrinal question.
- **LW4.** A liturgy treated as fixing a doctrine is refused
  (`lex_orandi_as_fixing`, a candidate part).
- **LW5.** No assent is owed to a prayer as such: no `owed/2` for a
  liturgical witness.
- **LW6.** LT1–LT8 and G1–G4 keep their outputs: the use of the Missal
  and the approval of translations stay governance.
- **LW7.** The axiom's source. The Indiculus, attributed to Celestine I
  (claimant: the collections that affixed it to his letter) and to Prosper
  of Aquitaine (claimant: CCC 1124, an act at tier 3, flagged as fixing
  nothing): at most one holds; three branches, the preferred neither. No
  date known to the record refutes either.
- **LW8.** A claim of the ordinary universal magisterium for the
  Immaculate Conception as of 1850, resting on the universal feast: three
  of LG 25's conditions shown by the liturgy, the fourth not; the claim
  unshown, its branch never preferred (c. 749 §3).
- **LW9.** The measure: for each witness, the Churches that pray it, its
  years in use at the evaluation date, and who prays it, reported. A
  change by competent authority to a long-kept element stands; its measure
  is reported beside it, and no verdict reads it.
- **LW10.** An element typed as binding because it is loved is refused
  (`binding_by_love`, a candidate part). No rule reads love.

### RS: the rite

- **RS1.** A liturgical book that drops the water from baptism is refused
  (`change_to_substance`), even from the Apostolic See; one that changes
  the canonical form of marriage stands (the form the Church's, since
  *Tametsi*).
- **RS2.** The matter of orders in time. An ordination to the priesthood
  in 1950 with the imposition of hands and without the porrection: valid
  (*Sacramentum ordinis* in force three months from its issue of the
  *Acta*, its date unread: a span that closes before 1950). In 1900, the
  same: computed both ways (the porrection required by the Church's will,
  or not), the branch says which, and the doubt is reported as the
  constitution disposes, to be submitted to the Apostolic See. In 1900 with
  both: valid on every branch. The ordinations already in the base (1988
  and after) keep their outputs.
- **RS3.** c. 846 §1. A minister who says "We baptize": the baptism
  invalid (the form; the responsum of 2020) and the change illicit. One
  who alters a prayer that is not the form: valid and illicit.

### RD: development

- **RD1.** The Roman Missal's editions as documents with dates (1570,
  1962, 1970, 2002), each descending from the one before; the Missal of
  1970 also from the ancient sacramentaries (two parents: not a line).
- **RD2.** A rite that descends from a later one, or from itself, is
  refused (candidate parts).
- **RD3.** The reform of 1970: continuity main, rupture a live shadow of
  Benedict XVI's letter of 2007 ("no rupture"), reintegrating if the
  letter is relaxed. *Traditionis custodes* answers nothing on it.

### M: meta

- **M1.** No earlier output changes: every scenario's atoms compared
  before and after. The TTC-SCHOOLS tables do not change.
- **M2.** Every new rule has a test that fails without it; CL1 and PR4
  stay clean.
- **M3.** In the coverage index, "Celebrating the Church's liturgy"
  (CCC 1136–1199) moves from cases to rules; the rule files cite CCC 1124,
  1125 and 1205 (42 paragraphs).

## CAVEATS

The prayer of the feast is entered as the Mass and Office *Cum
praeexcelsa* established; which Office (the one later approved, by one
report, in 1480) and its words on the Conception are read at encoding.
Where the prayer does not say "immaculate", it witnesses the feast, not the
doctrine, and LW2 is scored on what it says. DH 1400 was not read.

A witness takes the tier of the authority that approves the book. A
liturgy of a particular Church, or of a Church *sui iuris*, would weigh as
its approving authority does; none is entered here. The Byzantine feast of
the Dormition, a witness on Mary's death that *Munificentissimus Deus* left
open, waits.

*Sacramentum ordinis* names no date of force; its force is computed by
c. 8 §1's rule (the Code of 1917's c. 9 gave the same three months) from
an issue of AAS 40 (1948) whose date is unread. The porrection is entered
for the priesthood; the diaconate and the episcopate, whose matter was as
disputed, wait.

c. 2 speaks of liturgical laws, not books; the liturgical books' law as
documents beside the Code, and c. 838's competences beyond the translations
already in the base (G1–G4), are left for later. So is the reform's
"irreversibility", and every other claim about the grade of a liturgical
act (rule 14).

The bridge to the last things (SC 8; the one sacrifice, DH 1743) waits for
that step, with what love carries to the end (GS 39).

The measure counts what a case records of a witness's extent; the persons
who celebrate and receive, and the graph of celebrations they form, are
not entered. Apostolic succession is that graph restricted to
ordinations: the churches step.

### Caveats, read

- **The Office.** Its prayer is quoted in translation from a secondary
  source ("O God, Who by the Immaculate Conception of the Virgin, prepared
  a worthy dwelling place for thy Son"); the Latin and the bull approving
  it were not read. Its date (1480) rests on one report; the encoding uses
  it as the witness's date.
- ***Sacramentum ordinis*.** The issue's date is read from the *Acta*'s
  running head ("ACTA, vol. XV, n. 1. — 28-1-948"). The diaconate and the
  episcopate wait, as stated.
- **Rome's creed.** Leo III's refusal and Benedict VIII's change are
  entered as legislative acts of the pope; neither is a document read here.

### Not predicted

- **To be conferred conditionally.** Where the porrection's requirement is
  in doubt, the ordination of 1900 was at first to be conferred absolutely
  on the branch that required it. A doubt the law leaves open is a prudent
  doubt (c. 845 §2): the encoding makes it one, and the ordination is to be
  conferred conditionally on both branches.
- **Where the porrection sits.** METHOD put it in `sacrament.lp`. It sits
  in `canon_law.lp`, under *Sacramentum ordinis*'s `@norm`: a condition set
  by the Church's will, with a date of force, is law (rule 4).
- **Four tests' scope.** LO3, Rome's creed, loads the Trinity: it changes
  under monopatrism, the Thomistic stack and encoding A of DH 1330 (there
  its violations only, nothing turning red), as FW1–FW3 do. The group is kept out
  of the monopatrism test, of encoding A's and of the stamped boot set, as
  every later group is, and LO3 is added to the Thomistic stack's list.
