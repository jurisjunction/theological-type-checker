# Ford, the ends of marriage, and a bridge from a power's end to the act: an evidence record for TTC-MODERNITY

Rebuilt 27 November 2026: the record of 24 October was lost before it was committed; every item below was checked again in this pass. This is the research record for a question put on 24 October 2026: whether the library of causes (`causae.l`) needs a "bridge" part, by which the natural end of a power binds action, and if so how narrow it must be. It serves the contraception case, which PH3 of TTC-MODERNITY commits to a phase diagram of its own, with *Humanae vitae* 14 entering "on the object". `docs/roadmap.md` cites it under this name. It is an evidence record, so it keeps prose (rule 10). Nothing here is encoded, and the values are proposals. The lost record's conclusions were taken as hypotheses and tested; where the evidence disagrees, the section of that name says so.

## Corrections to the brief, kept because they shape the encoding

1. **Ford's doctorate is 1937; the book is 1938.** He "received his S.T.D. in 1937" [V, catholicbridge.com biography, which says it was checked against Fischer's dissertation of 2004]; Wikipedia gives "a doctorate in moral theology (1937)" [V]. The dissertation was printed as *The Validity of Virginal Marriage* (Worcester, MA: Harrigan Press, 1938), 139 pp., "Thesis (Ph.D.), Pontifical Gregorian University, 1938" [V, the Catholic Research Resources catalogue record]. ix + 139 pp. per the *American Ecclesiastical Review* [S: AER 100 (1939) 190–91, as reproduced on isidore.co].
2. **The dissertation's text was not read.** Its PDF on isidore.co is closed to the fetch tool (robots), and archive.org's copy was not reached. Its content comes through the AER review [S] and through Ford's own article of 1942, which says it draws on the dissertation [V].
3. **"Primarily" in *Casti connubii* 54 is the English's.** The English reads "destined primarily by nature for the begetting of children" [V]. The Latin is "Cum autem actus coniugii suapte natura proli generandae sit destinatus" [V, the bilingual edition of 1931, Barry Vail Corporation, OCR on core.ac.uk]. No word of the Latin sentence means "primarily" [V, the same]. So CC 54's argument rests on the act's own nature, not on the rank of its end. That matches Kelly in 1963 and Ford in 1942: the argument rests on an *essential* end, not a *primary* one (below).
4. **HV 14's sentence on the whole married life has three encodings.**
   - The Latin: "ideoque intrinsece inhonestum, fecundis totius coniugum vitae congressionibus comprobari posse" [S: Spindelböck, *Seminare* 40.4 (2019), n. 31, who cites it to nn. 11–12]. It speaks of the fertile unions of the whole married life: acts summed.
   - The 1968 English: "the ensemble of a fecund conjugal life" [V, the text at spot.colorado.edu, translator and source unstated].
   - vatican.va's English: a single act cannot "merge with procreative acts of past and future to form a single entity" (record: `warrants-and-prior-art.md`).

   Whether "the whole" is a sum of acts or a unit of its own is the disputed point, so the case must name it.
5. **HV 14 defines the excluded act by intention.** The act excluded is any that "intendat, ut procreatio impediatur" [S: Spindelböck, n. 31], as an end or as a means [M for the clause "vel tamquam finem ... vel tamquam viam"]. VS 80 lists "contraceptive practices whereby the conjugal act is intentionally rendered infertile" [V, catholicculture.org]. The Vademecum for confessors (1997) 2.4 has "every marital act intentionally rendered unfruitful" [V, IntraText]. The operative formula after 1968 is an object as chosen: (formal, will). This is the main finding on the first question below.
6. **"Totality" names three things.**
   - Pius XII's principle of totality, the part for the whole body. HV 17 names it, and HV 3 raises it as a question [V, the 1968 English].
   - The totality of married life: the majority's "fecundity in the totality of married life" (Schema 1.3.3) [V].
   - The act in its totality: Kelly reports that for Pius XII a moral judgment of the conjugal act "must consider the act in its totality" (p. 32), and calls the act "a totality, a life-giving act of love" (p. 41) [V, Kelly 1963].

   The minority answers the first and the second in one place (below). A trace cannot tell them apart, so each enters by its object.
7. **The minority report has three grounds, and the perverted faculty is not first.** Its section D grounds the evil in the inviolability of life in its causes and in God's dominion. Then comes the scholastic explanation, the act's "essential ordination ... to the good of the species". Then an intention "contrary to a fundamental human good" [V, all three]. These are three typings, (final, god_ethics), bind(final, nature) and (formal, will) with (final, human), so the minority is not one horizon.
8. **Grisez helped draft the minority report.** Ford drafted it "with help from American theologian Germain Grisez" [V, Wikipedia, "Pontifical Commission on Birth Control"]. Its third ground (D.4) is the shape of Grisez's own argument. The attribution of D.4 to Grisez is an inference [inference].
9. **Ford himself, in 1978, sets the perverted-faculty argument aside.** Ford and Grisez say its wide use is explained "less by its contribution to rational clarification" than by what it evokes (TS 39 (1978) 284 n. 59) [V]. They state the teaching by intention: "acts intended to impede procreation are intrinsically and gravely evil" (p. 280) [V]. So the claim of infallibility attaches to an object as chosen, not to the faculty argument.
10. **The majority grants that each act is ordered by nature.** The minority reports the view: "each and every sexual act is ordered by nature" [V]. On that view "a partial good, namely, the ordering of individual acts to procreation" may be "sacrificed for the good of the whole" [V, minority report, "Consequences", A]. The natural end of each act is held, and its binding force is denied. That is the bridge's question exactly.
11. **The Council left the question of each act open.** GS 51 n. 14 [V, New Advent]: certain questions "have been handed over ... to a commission". With doctrine "in this state", the synod "does not intend to propose immediately concrete solutions".
12. **Thomas's Latin was not reached.** The Latin of SCG III.122 and of II-II q. 154 a. 12 is [M]. Their English is [V] in Rickaby's abridged translation (nd.edu) and in the Dominican Fathers' (New Advent). The Latin of II-II q. 152 a. 2 ad 1 is [V] (aquinas.cc).

## John C. Ford, SJ

### The dissertation (1938), and Ford's own summary of it (1942)

- **Its thesis.** Part one concludes that marriage is "a moral bond between man and woman". The bond consists "in the perpetual, exclusive right to one another's persons". It is a right "with a view to the acts of conjugal life and love" [S: AER 100 (1939) 190–91, through isidore.co]. Part two compares the condition of preserving virginity with that essence and decides "that the two are not contradictory" [S: same]. An appendix lists 78 authors who favour validity and 55 who are inclined to deny it [S: same, as summarized on isidore.co].
- **Ford's article draws on it.** John C. Ford, "Marriage: Its Meaning and Purposes", *Theological Studies* 3 (1942) 333–374 [V]. He says that what follows on "the essential nature of marriage in its relation to its ends" comes from the dissertation (p. 339 n. 3) [V]. He calls the dissertation an "essay" there [V].
- **The ends are essential, all three.** "the three ends of marriage are all essential ends", as theologians generally state (p. 348) [V]. Marriage "cannot exist without being objectively ordered to the three essential ends" (p. 348) [V]. All three are "fines operis" (n. 13) [V].
- **Essential and primary are different.** "there cannot be degrees of essentialness, though there can be degrees of importance" (p. 369) [V]. Procreation is primary because, "though not more essential than mutual help, it is more fundamental" (p. 370) [V].
- **The right, not its use.** The bond always consists in "a right to the acts by which that end is attainable" (p. 349) [V]. Those acts "need only be per se apti ad generationem" (p. 351) [V]. The essential *jus in corpus* includes "the radical right to all the acts by which mutual help" is realized (p. 352) [V]. Virginal marriage is valid because the right, not its exercise, is of the essence [S: AER].
- **Negative in form, in Ford's words.** The use of marriage is subordinated to the primary end per se "only in the sense that no positive interference with the primary end is permitted" (p. 369) [V]. Spouses are "not bound ordinarily, in individual cases, to realize the primary end" (p. 369) [V]. What is required "is merely the preservation of the physical integrity of the act" (p. 369) [V]. Spouses do their essential duty "when they avoid any positive interference with the marriage act" (p. 369) [V].
- **The sterile act.** There is "only a limited sense in which the sexual act can be called procreative" in sterile marriages (p. 350) [V].
- **Against Doms.** Doms's distinction of meaning (*Sinn*) and end "will not stand up under philosophic scrutiny" (p. 365) [V]. "The ends of marriage are its meaning" (pp. 365–66) [V]. Still, the theological world "owes a debt of gratitude" to Doms (p. 374) [V]. Krempel appears only in a bibliographic note (p. 334) [V].

**For the encoding.** In 1942 Ford's bridge is plain. The procreative end binds the use of marriage only negatively ("no positive interference"), and only per act ("the physical integrity of the act"). It does not bind the marriage to actual fruit: virginal marriage is valid, and spouses are not bound "in individual cases" to realize it. The end enters marriage's essence through the object of the right, acts "per se apti". The typing is bind(final, nature) on the act and (final, human) on the institution.

### Ford and Kelly, *Contemporary Moral Theology*, vol. 2, *Marriage Questions* (1963)

The book was not reached. Its argument comes through two texts of the same year.

- **Kelly's paper.** Gerald Kelly, "Contraception and Natural Law", *Proceedings of the Catholic Theological Society of America* 18 (1963), pp. 25–45 [V, ejournals.bc.edu, for volume, title and pages]. "A considerable portion of this paper is based on, or taken from" vol. 2 (p. 25) [V].
- **The book's contents,** from a review: part one compares Protestant and Catholic views on the ends of marriage; part two applies them to contraception, sterilizing drugs and periodic continence [S: Kirkus Reviews, paraphrased there].
- **The perverted-faculty argument, stated and kept.** The major: to use a natural faculty so as "to frustrate an essential purpose of its use is intrinsically immoral" (p. 36) [V]. The minor premise: contraception so uses the reproductive faculty (p. 36) [V].
- **Essential, not primary.** The "real basis" of the argument "is not that it frustrates a primary end" (p. 39) [V]. "procreation is certainly an essential end of the act" (p. 38) [V].
- **The papal argument preferred.** God has written "a certain definite plan into the natural structure of the conjugal act" (p. 28) [V]. Each phase of generative function is "part of a divine plan that man is not free to change" (p. 30) [V]. Contraception is "an attack on the inviolable divine plan for the beginning of human life" (p. 31) [V]. "the papal approach is better than the 'perverted faculty' approach" (p. 37) [V].
- **The sterile act.** It "retains its natural inherent potential for giving life" (p. 45) [V]. The act is "essentially, and at least inherently, procreative" (p. 45) [V].
- **The personalist claim, met.** He quotes Pius XII, "The conjugal act in its natural structure is a personal action" [V]. Against a relational criterion: "'Relational' value ... seems to attach itself to the oddest sexual activities" (p. 34) [V].
- **"Each act" does not occur** in the paper [V, by absence]; its totality is the act's (p. 41) [V].
- **The book on irrevocability,** through Lynch, "Notes on Moral Theology", *Theological Studies* 25 (1964) 232–53. A chapter of Ford and Kelly's book concludes the teaching "irrevocable" (p. 236) and "at least definable" (p. 237) [S: Lynch, who calls it "one of the most important chapters"].
- **The personalist debate in the book,** including Doms, the Holy Office decree of 1 April 1944 and the hierarchy of ends: [M]. Connell's CTSA paper "The Catholic Doctrine on the Ends of Marriage" discusses the decree of April 1944 [V, that it does]. Its volume and year are [M].

**For the encoding.** The manualists of 1963 hold the bridge in two scopes, and rank them. The faculty argument is bind(final, nature); they keep it valid. The papal argument is bind(final, god_physics); they prefer it. Neither depends on the hierarchy of ends.

### Ford and Grisez (1978)

- John C. Ford and Germain Grisez, "Contraception and the Infallibility of the Ordinary Magisterium", *Theological Studies* 39 (1978) 258–312 [V, catholicbridge.com; Curran 2018 n. 54; the article's pages fetched, 258–312].
- **The thesis.** "the received Catholic teaching on contraception has been proposed infallibly by the ordinary magisterium" (p. 259) [V]. The teaching is "at least definable doctrine" (p. 258) [V]. "contraceptive acts are objectively grave violations of the law of God" (p. 259) [V]. The norm is stated as "acts intended to impede procreation are in species gravely evil" (p. 281) [V].
- **The four conditions, from LG 25.** The bishops remain in communion; they teach authoritatively on faith or morals; they agree in one judgment; they propose it "as one to be held definitively" (p. 272) [V, paraphrased from the sentence].
- **The evidence for the fourth.** The teaching "always was proposed as a received and certain part of the obligatory moral teaching" (p. 282) [V]. It was often proposed "as a moral norm divinely revealed" (p. 282) [V]. The manuals' consensus is part of the evidence (pp. 279–82) [V, as reported by the fetch, paraphrase].
- **What is not claimed.** "We do not assert that the norm is divinely revealed" (p. 286) [V]. The encyclical "contained no ex cathedra pronouncement" (p. 261) [V]. No pope "says that the teaching he reaffirms has been proposed infallibly" (p. 286) [V].
- **The norm, not the reasons.** The teaching "is what is constant and universal amidst changes in the arguments given for it" (p. 298) [V]. Infallibility is claimed for the norm, not for the natural-law arguments [V, paraphrase].
- **Each act and the whole.** "contraceptive acts are condemned as intrinsically evil" (p. 294) [V]. No teacher before 1963 made the condemnation conditional on the totality of married life (p. 294) [V, paraphrase]. They quote the Schema's "fecundity in the totality of married life" (p. 292) [V].
- **The faculty argument.** See correction 9 (n. 59) [V].

## The magisterial texts

The dates in the headings are standard citations [M], save HV's, which the *Vademecum*'s notes give [V, IntraText], VS's (record: `modernity-object.md`), and Pius XII's (record: `icon_history.l`).

### *Casti connubii* (31 December 1930)

The English quoted is at spot.colorado.edu, which states no source; its CC 54 matches the opening of the 1931 Barry Vail edition [V, both].

- **CC 17.** "The primary end of marriage is the procreation and the education of children" [V].
- **CC 54, the argument.** "the conjugal act is destined primarily by nature for the begetting of children" [V; on "primarily", correction 3]. Those who "deliberately frustrate its natural power and purpose sin against nature" [V]. The deed is "shameful and intrinsically vicious" [V]. Latin: "naturali hac ... vi atque virtute de industria destituunt, contra naturam agunt" [V, 1931 bilingual, OCR "cum" for "eum" M].
- **CC 54, the absolute.** No reason, "however grave", can make "anything intrinsically against nature" become "conformable to nature and morally good" [V].
- **CC 55.** Onan "did this and the Lord killed him for it" [V].
- **CC 56, the formula.** It covers "any use whatsoever of matrimony" in which "the act is deliberately frustrated" [V]. Such use "is an offense against the law of God and of nature" [V]. Those who indulge in it are "branded with the guilt of a grave sin" [V].
- **CC 59, the sterile act and the secondary ends.** Spouses do not act against nature when, "on account of natural reasons either of time or of certain defects", "new life cannot be brought forth" [V]. The secondary ends are "mutual aid, the cultivating of mutual love, and the quieting of concupiscence" [V]. Spouses may seek them "so long as the intrinsic nature of the act is preserved" [V].

**Typing.** CC 54 is the faculty argument in magisterial form: (formal, will) for "de industria", and bind(final, nature) for "suapte natura" and "contra naturam". CC 56's "law of God and of nature" adds a reading on (final, god_ethics). CC 17 speaks of marriage as a whole: (final, human).

### Pius XII to the midwives (29 October 1951)

The English is IntraText's [V]; the date is (record: `icon_history.l`, `pius_xii_1951`, and `warrants-and-prior-art.md`).

- The problem: recourse to "the periods of natural sterility" seems to express a will "contrary to that precept" [V].
- Marriage "imposes the accomplishment of a positive work concerning the state itself" [V].
- Serious motives, "medical, eugenic, economic and social", may exempt spouses "from the obligatory, positive debt" [V]. The exemption may last "for a long period or even for the entire period of matrimonial life" [V].
- To avoid the primary duty "without a grave reason" would be "a sin against the very nature of married life" [V].
- The use of the sterile periods "differs essentially from the abuse already mentioned". That abuse "consists in the perversion of the act itself" [V].

**Typing.** This text holds the two questions in one address. The negative per-act norm is "the perversion of the act itself": bind(final, nature). The positive norm binds the state, and serious motives excuse from it: (final, human), on the marriage as a whole. The positive is excusable and the negative is not.

### *Gaudium et spes* (7 December 1965)

New Advent's English [V]; the Latin is [M].

- GS 48: "the institution of matrimony itself and conjugal love" are ordained "for the procreation and education of children" [V].
- GS 50: marriage and conjugal love "are by their nature ordained toward the begetting and educating of children" [V], "while not making the other purposes of matrimony of less account" [V]. Marriage "is not instituted solely for procreation" [V].
- GS 51: the moral aspect of a procedure "must be determined by objective standards", "based on the nature of the human person and his acts" [V]. These standards preserve "the full sense of mutual self-giving and human procreation" in true love [V]. The sons of the Church may not undertake "methods of birth control which are found blameworthy" [V]. On n. 14, correction 11 [V].

**Typing.** GS speaks of the marriage as a whole, on (final, human) and (final, god_ethics) (the institution). On each act it reserves the question.

### *Humanae vitae* (25 July 1968)

The 1968 English is [V] (spot.colorado.edu). The Latin is through Spindelböck [S], or [M].

- **HV 10, responsible parenthood.** It is exercised either "by the deliberate and generous decision to raise a numerous family" [V], or by the decision, "made for grave motives", to avoid a new birth "for the time being, or even for an indeterminate period" [V]. This is the totality's positive norm, under prudence.
- **HV 11, each act.** Acts foreseen to be infecund "do not cease to be lawful" when this comes from "causes independent of the will" [V]. The Church teaches "that each and every marriage act (quilibet matrimonii usus) must remain open" to life [V]. Latin: "quilibet matrimonii usus ad vitam humanam procreandam per se destinatus permaneat" [S: Spindelböck n. 5]. "per se destinatus" is the act's own ordination, as in CC 54's "suapte natura".
- **HV 12, the two meanings.** "the inseparable connection, willed by God and unable to be broken by man" [V], between "the unitive meaning and the procreative meaning" [V]. The act's "intimate structure" fits it for generation "according to laws inscribed in the very being of man and of woman" [V]. The Latin "nexu indissolubili" [S: Spindelböck]; the rest of the Latin is [M].
- **HV 13, dominion and the divine plan.** Man has no dominion over his generative faculties "because of their intrinsic ordination towards raising up life" [V], "of which God is the principle" [V]. An act harmful to the faculty, "which God the Creator of all, has implanted in it according to special laws" [V], contradicts "the divine plan, according to whose norm matrimony has been instituted" [V]. It also contradicts "the will of the Author of human life" [V].
- **HV 14, the act and the whole.** It is "not licit, even for the gravest reasons, to do evil" that good may follow [V]. "one cannot invoke as valid reasons the lesser evil" [V]. An act "deliberately made infecund and so ... intrinsically dishonest" [V] cannot be made right "by the ensemble of a fecund conjugal life" [V]. On the Latin of the definition and of the whole, corrections 4–5 [S].
- **HV 16, the cycles.** Spouses may "take into account the natural rhythms immanent in the generative functions" [V].

**Typing.** HV carries every typing the case needs. HV 11 is bind(final, nature). HV 12 and HV 13's first clause are bind(final, god_physics): "willed by God", "of which God is the principle". HV 13's second clause, the plan by which matrimony was instituted, is (final, god_ethics) and needs no bridge. HV 14's definition is (formal, will). HV 10 is (final, human) on the whole. HV 14 refuses to derive the first question from the second.

### *Veritatis splendor* (6 August 1993) and the *Vademecum* (1997)

*Veritatis splendor* is Catholic Culture's English [V].
- **VS 47, the objection** that the natural law presents "as moral laws what are in themselves mere biological laws" [V]. Critics link the condemnation of contraception to "a naturalistic understanding of the sexual act" [V, as the encyclical reports it].
- **VS 48 and 50, the answer through the person.** "in the unity of body and soul" the person is "the subject of his own moral acts" (48) [V]. Natural inclinations take on moral relevance "only insofar as they refer to the human person" (50) [V].
- **VS 52, the negative precepts.** They "oblige each and every individual, always and in every circumstance" [V]. They forbid actions "semper et pro semper, without exception" [V].
- **VS 78.** The object is "the proximate end of a deliberate decision" [V].
- **VS 80.** There are acts that are wrong "per se and in themselves, independently of circumstances" [V], among them "contraceptive practices whereby the conjugal act is intentionally rendered infertile" [V].

*Vademecum for Confessors*, Pontifical Council for the Family, 2.4 [V, IntraText]: "The Church has always taught the intrinsic evil of contraception" [V], "that is, of every marital act intentionally rendered unfruitful" [V]. "This teaching is to be held as definitive and irreformable" [V]. The date, 12 February 1997, is [S: EWTN's listing].

**Typing.** VS answers the charge of physicalism through the person, (final, human) (VS 50), and through the object as chosen, (formal, will) (VS 78–80). It does not answer it by a bridge from nature. On these texts, the magisterium's account after 1993 needs no bridge. The Vademecum's last sentence is a claim about grade (rule 14, below).

## Thomas Aquinas

### *Summa contra gentiles* III.122 and 126

Rickaby's abridged translation, *Of God and His Creatures*, at nd.edu [V]; the Latin is [M].
- **III.122, the ground.** God "has care of everything according to that which is good for it" [V]. "it is good for everything to gain its end, and evil for it to be diverted from its due end" [V]. The next sentence begins "But as in the whole so" [V]; its end was not read.
- **III.122, the act.** "every emission of the semen is contrary to the good of man, which takes place in a way whereby generation is impossible" [V]. It is "repugnant to the good of nature, which is the conservation of the species" [V].
- **III.122, by accident.** "But if it is by accident that generation cannot follow", "the act is not against nature on that account, nor is it sinful" [V]. "the case of the woman being barren would be a case in point" [V].
- **III.122, the rank.** "after the sin of murder, whereby a human nature already in actual existence is destroyed, this sort of sin seem to hold the second place" [V].
- **III.122, the kind, not the case.** A woman who could rear a child alone is no objection, "for in human acts the line of natural rectitude is not drawn to suit the accidental variety of the individual" [V], but by "the properties common to the whole species" [V].
- **III.122, marriage.** The young must be advanced in good "for a long period of life" [V]. So "it is natural for man to be tied to the society of one fixed woman" [V]: "This social tie we call marriage" [V].
- **III.126.** "Natural inclinations are put into things by God, who is the prime mover of all" [V]. "it is impossible for the natural inclination of any species to be directed to an object in itself evil" [V].
- **III.123–125** (indissolubility, one to one, kindred) were not fetched beyond the heading of 125 [V, heading only; content M].

### II-II q. 154 aa. 11–12

New Advent's English [V]; Freddoso's translation (nd.edu) checked for a. 12 ad 1 [V]; the Latin is [M].
- **a. 11.** The unnatural vice is "contrary to the natural order of the venereal act as becoming to the human race" [V]. It has four kinds, the fourth "if the natural manner of sexual intercourse is not observed" [V, Freddoso].
- **a. 12.** "the principles of reason are those things that are according to nature" [V]. "reason presupposes things as determined by nature" [V]. So "it follows that in this matter this sin is gravest of all" [V]. Freddoso's PDF, as extracted, reads "in accord with reason" in the first clause. That is likely a slip in the file or the extraction, and nothing rests on it [V that it reads so].
- **a. 12 ad 1.** As the order of right reason is from man, "the ordering of nature comes from God Himself" [V, Freddoso, whose translation is in copyright]. An injury is done "to God, the Author of nature" [V, New Advent]; Freddoso has "the one who orders nature" [V].

### The positive precept and the negative

- **II-II q. 152 a. 2 ad 1** [V, aquinas.cc, Latin and English]. A duty is "debitum implendum a multitudine" or by each [V]. The precept to eat binds each, "aliter enim individuum conservari non posset" [V]. "Sed praeceptum datum de generatione respicit totam multitudinem hominum" [V]. So "sufficienter providetur humanae multitudini si quidam carnali generationi operam dent" [V], while others contemplate. English: "the precept of procreation regards the whole multitude of men" [V].
- **II-II q. 33 a. 2.** "the negative precepts of the Law forbid sinful acts, the positive precepts inculcate acts of virtue" [V, ccel.org]. "negative precepts bind always and for all times" [V]. The Latin "semper et ad semper" is [M]; VS 52 has "semper et pro semper" [V].

### What Thomas gives the bridge

- **The bridge is in Thomas, in two texts joined.** The object's goodness is "per comparationem ad rationem" (I-II q. 18 a. 5) (record: `modernity-object.md`, [V] there). In venereal matters reason "presupposes things as determined by nature" (II-II q. 154 a. 12) [V]. So the measure of the object, in this matter, is nature's determination. `object_as_form` gives the act its form. The bridge gives that form its measure.
- **Its scope is nature and God's ordering of nature.** The act's due end is "the good of nature, which is the conservation of the species" (SCG III.122) [V]. "the ordering of nature comes from God Himself" (q. 154 a. 12 ad 1) [V]. The inclinations are "put into things by God" (SCG III.126) [V]. These are (final, nature) and (final, god_physics).
- **It is per act, and by kind.** Each emission of the kind is excluded, but not one barren by accident. The rule follows "the properties common to the whole species", not "the accidental variety of the individual" (SCG III.122) [V].
- **It is negative.** The positive precept of generation binds the multitude, not each person (q. 152 a. 2 ad 1) [V]. Negative precepts bind "always and for all times" (q. 33 a. 2) [V].
- **A second reading runs.** SCG III.122 also says the act is "contrary to the good of man" [V]. Read so, it rests on (final, human) and needs no bridge. Finnis reads Thomas's sexual ethics through the good of marriage (*Aquinas*, 1998, ch. V) [M]. Both readings should run: `scg122_good`, a further reading of `th_scg122` on (final, human).
- **Its application to marital contraception** is the manualists' and CC's. Thomas's texts here speak of emission of seed and the manner of copulation, not of the marriage act deprived of its power by an art. That is a reading, entered with its claimant (Kelly; the minority's D.2, which names "scholastic philosophy" [V]).

## The questions

### Does `object_as_form` carry the contraception arguments?

**In part.** It carries the arguments that define the act by its object as chosen:
- HV 14's "intendat, ut procreatio impediatur" [S];
- VS 80's "intentionally rendered infertile" [V];
- the *Vademecum*'s "intentionally rendered unfruitful" [V];
- Ford and Grisez's "acts intended to impede procreation" [V];
- the new natural lawyers' contralife choice (GBFM 1988, with (final, human)) [V];
- the minority's D.4 [V].

It does not carry the arguments whose measure is the power's natural end. In these the object is evil because the act is deprived of what "suapte natura" it is for:
- CC 54 [V];
- Pius XII's "the perversion of the act itself" [V];
- HV 11's "per se destinatus" [S] and HV 12–13's laws and intrinsic ordination [V];
- the minority's D.2 [V];
- Kelly's faculty and papal arguments [V];
- Ford's "physical integrity of the act" (1942) [V];
- Thomas's SCG III.122 and q. 154 a. 12 [V].

The object is the "materia circa quam" as reason apprehends it (record: `modernity-object.md`). Reason in this matter "presupposes things as determined by nature" [V]. The form is one part; the measure is another.

### Is a bridge needed?

**Yes, because figures in the study hold the natural end and deny that it binds.**
- The new natural lawyers: "the mere natural teleology of the act or faculty" does not ground the obligation [S: Milhaven on Grisez]. Contraception is not "any specific pattern of behavior" [V, GBFM].
- The majority, as the minority reports it: each act is "ordered by nature", but that "partial good" may be sacrificed for the whole [V].
- The Schema: no opposition between "material conformity to the physiological processes" and intervention [V].
- Curran: HV "identifies the human moral act with the physical structure" [V].
- Kant: the end of nature is used, and its "rational demonstration" doubted (record: `modernity-object.md`).
- Ford and Grisez in 1978, who explain the faculty argument's use by what it evokes more than by what it proves (n. 59) [V].

Without a bridge, the only way to deny such an argument is to exclude `ends_in_nature`. That makes Grisez a Cartesian in physics, which no text warrants. The alternative is to type the faculty arguments by `object_as_form` and the end together, so that every horizon holding both follows them. The new natural lawyers' horizon, and the majority's, would then follow CC 54, which they reject. Either way the trace could not show where they part from Thomas.

**Why the library cannot already say it.** TTC-MODERNITY split God's intention into `god_physics` and `god_ethics` so that "the split between physics and ethics enters through the domain" (record: `modernity-preregistration.md`, THE DESIGN). An argument from (final, nature) to a moral prohibition is a crossing from physics to ethics that the domains themselves do not name. The lying case already makes that crossing unmarked: `th110_genus`, `tl_purpose` and `ccc2485_speech` are typed (final, nature) (record: `mendacium-preregistration.md`). So the crossing is held wherever the end is, and no horizon can hold the one without the other.

### How narrow: per act only

**Held, on every text.** The bridge is needed only for norms on each act of a kind.
- The positive norm on fruitfulness binds the marriage, and is excused by grave motives: Pius XII 1951 [V]; HV 10 [V]. The precept of generation "regards the whole multitude" [V, q. 152 a. 2 ad 1].
- The majority's own prohibition, of a life "in its totality ... opposed to fruitfulness", is argued from the nature of sexuality and of the marriage (1.2.2.4–5): (final, human) [V].
- GS 48 and 50 speak of the institution and of conjugal love: (final, human) and (final, god_ethics) [V].

None of these needs the bridge. Narrower still: of the per-act arguments, only those whose measure is the power's end need it (above).

### Negative in form

**Held, and in Ford's own words.**
- "no positive interference with the primary end is permitted"; spouses are not bound "in individual cases" to realize it (TS 3 (1942) 369) [V].
- CC 59: acts barren by "natural reasons either of time or of certain defects" are not against nature [V].
- HV 11: acts foreseen infecund "do not cease to be lawful" [V]. HV 16: the "natural rhythms" may be taken into account [V].
- SCG III.122: an act barren "by accident" is "not against nature ... nor ... sinful" [V].
- Kelly: the act need be only "inherently procreative" (p. 45) [V].
- The bridge forbids depriving an act of its power. It never commands that an act be fertile, or that a marriage use its right: virginal marriage is valid [S: AER].
- The negative binds "always and for all times" (q. 33 a. 2) [V]; "semper et pro semper" (VS 52) [V]. The positive counterpart binds the state, "semper sed non pro semper" [M as a tag], and the 1951 address lets grave motives excuse it "for the entire period of matrimonial life" [V].
- So the bridge's form is a refusal: an argument that uses it for a value which is not a prohibition is ill-typed (Proposed values).

### Which domain

**Its scope is (final, nature) and (final, god_physics), and nothing in ethics.**
- **(final, nature).** CC 54's "suapte natura" and "contra naturam" [V]. The faculty argument [V, Kelly 36]. The minority's D.2, "the good of the species" [V]. SCG III.122, "the good of nature, which is the conservation of the species" [V]. HV 11's "per se destinatus" [S]. Ford's "physical integrity" [V].
- **(final, god_physics).** q. 154 a. 12 ad 1, "the ordering of nature comes from God Himself" [V]. SCG III.126, inclinations "put into things by God" [V]. Kelly's "papal approach", a plan "written ... into the natural structure" [V]. HV 12, "willed by God" [V]. HV 13, "of which God is the principle" [V].
- **Not (final, god_ethics).** God's dominion over life (minority D.3) [V], and the plan by which "matrimony has been instituted" (HV 13) [V], are already ends for human action. They need no bridge, and are entered as their own readings. A bridge there would be idle.
- **Not (final, human).** The basic good of life (GBFM; minority D.4) [V], VS 50's inclinations referred "to the human person" [V] and SCG III.122's "good of man" [V] are ends of human nature. A bridge there would be idle too.
- **The same end can be typed three ways.** Procreation can be the power's end as a natural thing's, (final, nature), as the lying case types the end of speech. It can be God's ordering of the power, (final, god_physics). Or it can be an inclination of human nature, (final, human): I-II q. 94 a. 2's second order, "coniunctio maris et feminae, et educatio liberorum" [M here; q. 94 a. 2 is (record: `modernity-preregistration.md`, [V] there) for the inclinations]. The bridge belongs to the first two only. The third is the new natural lawyers' typing, and VS 50's.

**Held.** The case should be two questions, as the property case is two (products, land):
- `q(each_act_open)`: each marital act deliberately deprived of its power to generate is wrong.
- `q(marriage_open)`: a married life opposed in its totality to fruitfulness is wrong.

## What the record gives the study, and what it does not settle

**It gives:**
- a reason for a part of a new kind beside the ten cause parts: holding an end, and holding that it binds, are two things, and texts in the study hold the first without the second;
- the bridge's scope, from texts: (final, nature) and (final, god_physics);
- its form: a prohibition on each act of a kind, from Ford, Thomas and the encyclicals;
- the split of the case into q1 and q2, as the commission's documents make it;
- the three typings of the procreative end, and which needs the bridge;
- Ford's own path from the bridge to the object;
- how the claim of 1978 enters under rule 14.

**It does not settle:**
- whether the faculty argument is sound. The checker never shows that a well-formed claim is true;
- whether HV 11's "per se destinatus" and Ford and Grisez's "acts intended to impede" are one proposition;
- whether HV's own ground is the faculty, the person, or God's plan. All three readings run;
- the Latin of Thomas, of GS 50–51 and of most of HV;
- the *Documentum syntheticum*'s arguments;
- Ford and Kelly's book in its own words.

## Where the evidence disagrees with the lost record

- **"Needed narrowly, for per-act arguments only": held, and narrower.** The per-act formula the Church has used since 1968 is carried by `object_as_form`, as are the new natural lawyers' argument and Ford and Grisez's proposition. These are HV 14's "intendat", VS 80, and the *Vademecum*. The bridge is needed only for per-act arguments whose measure is the power's end. It is also not peculiar to contraception: the lying case's faculty arguments are of the same form (consequence 1).
- **"Negative in form": held.** Ford's 1942 words ("no positive interference"), CC 59, HV 11 and 16, SCG III.122 and II-II q. 152 a. 2 ad 1 agree. The positive counterpart lives in q2, as Pius XII's excusable "positive debt".
- **"Its scope includes (final, god_physics)": held, but incomplete if that was all.** The faculty argument, CC 54 and the minority's D.2 rest on (final, nature), so the scope must include it. The scope excludes (final, god_ethics) and (final, human), where a bridge is idle.
- **"Two questions": held.** The evidence adds that q2 is answered yes by every document fetched. The OUM claim attaches to q1 alone. The Council reserved q1 (GS 51 n. 14).
- **The figures, against the list in the brief.** The bridge separates the new natural lawyers and the proportionalists from Thomas and the manualists. It does not separate the manualists from Thomas, and it is idle at Kant and Hobbes. It separates `kant_mm` only by a reading.
- **A correction the lost record may not have had.** CC 54's "primarily" is not in the Latin (correction 3). Ford himself in 1978 set the faculty argument aside (correction 9). The minority report is three horizons, not one (correction 7).
