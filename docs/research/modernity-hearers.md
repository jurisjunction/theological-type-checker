# Leo's hearers: the evidence record for the hearers of *Rerum Novarum*

This is the research record behind any hearer nodes for TTC-MODERNITY. It was compiled on 23 October 2026 and is an evidence record, so it keeps prose (rule 10). It answers one question: whom RN addressed and argued against, which horizon each held, and how each answered.

**How sure each item is:**
- **[V]** The wording was checked against an online primary text or translation fetched for this record. For a secondary work, [V] means its own wording was fetched; [V, abstract] means only its abstract or publisher's page was.
- **[S]** It was checked through a named secondary source that quotes or reports the primary.
- **[M]** It comes from memory or a standard citation and was not re-checked. It is unverified until someone checks it in print.

Quotations are under 25 words. RN is quoted from vatican.va: the English, numbered as there, and the Latin, which vatican.va prints without numbers. Quotations came through a fetch tool that returns short extracts, so a quotation is exact as returned, not proof-read against a printed page.

## Corrections to the brief, kept because they shape the encoding

1. **"Sacred and inviolable" is the English, not the Latin.** The English of RN 46 has "private ownership must be held sacred and inviolable" [V]. The Latin there has only "ius privatorum bonorum sanctum esse oportere" [V]. "Inviolate" is RN 15: "privatas possessiones inviolate servandas" [V]. Fortin's title follows the English pairing. He also cites "ius sanctum, no. 46" separately, so his argument does not depend on it.
2. **"The impress of his personality" translates a form-term.** RN 9's Latin is "in qua velut formam quamdam personae suae impressam reliquit" [V]: he left on it, as it were, a form of his person impressed. The English drops the hylomorphic word.
3. **RN 9 has final premises in its first sentence.** Its first sentence gives the end of the soil's produce: "Res enim eas, quae ad conservandam vitam maximeque ad perficiendam requiruntur" [V]. The English has "the preservation of life, and for life's well-being" [V], where the Latin is "maximeque ad perficiendam": above all, for perfecting it.
4. **RN 10 makes the efficient-causal premise explicit and Locke's "mixing" literal.** It says improvements "solo inhaerent miscenturque penitus" [V]. It also has "Quo modo effectae res caussam sequuntur a qua effectae sunt, sic operae fructum ad eos ipsos qui operam dederint, rectum est pertinere" [V].
5. **RN 7 argues for land, not only fruits, and has a self-preservation clause.** It concludes "non modo terrenorum fructuum, sed ipsius terrae dominatum" [V]. On the way it says "ius ille suum ad vitam corpusque tuendum habere natura ante debuit quam civitas ulla coisset" [V], and it places man's self-government "sub lege aeterna, sub potestate omnia providentissime gubernantis Dei" [V].
6. **RN 8 paraphrases Thomas II-II q. 66 a. 2 ad 1, with labour added.** God gave the earth in common "non quod eius promiscuum apud omnes dominatum voluerit, sed quia partem nullam cuique assignavit possidendam" [V]. The division is then left "industriae hominum institutisque populorum" [V]. Thomas's "humanum condictum" (record, [V]) becomes human industry *and* the institutions of peoples.
7. **RN 22 quotes Thomas II-II q. 66 a. 2 verbatim.** "non debet homo habere res exteriores ut proprias, sed ut communes" is followed by "ut scilicet de facili aliquis eas communicet in necessitate aliorum" [V]. The duty is "Non iustitiae excepto in rebus extremis … sed caritatis christianae" [V].
8. **The first draft was Liberatore's, not Zigliara's.** Wikipedia says Zigliara wrote the first draft, and Crux (2021) says he wrote the "base" draft. Ries 2017, Ondarza 2025, Aubert (Universalis) and Fortin 1992 put Liberatore first and Zigliara second (below). This record follows the second group.
9. **The Erfurt Programme postdates RN.** It was adopted on 14–20 October 1891 [V], five months after RN (15 May 1891). In May 1891 the SPD's programme in force was Gotha (1875).

## Leo's hearers

### The bishops (the formal addressees)

**Who.** RN is addressed "To Our Venerable Brethren the Patriarchs, Primates, Archbishops, Bishops, and other ordinaries" [V], and through them to Catholic workers and employers.

**Horizon.** Thomist by formation after *Aeterni Patris* (1879) [M]. RN speaks to them in Thomas's words: RN 22 quotes q. 66 a. 2 [V] and cites "St. Thomas Aquinas" by name [V].

**What RN tells them.**
- The remedy needs "the intervention of religion and of the Church" (RN 16) [V].
- It is a mistake to hold "that class is naturally hostile to class" (RN 19) [V].
- The use of goods falls under "caritatis christianae", not justice except in extremity (RN 22) [V].

### The socialists, as RN names them

**Loci in RN.**
- Their proposal: "individual possessions should become the common property of all, to be administered by the State or by municipal bodies" (RN 4) [V].
- Its effect: it would "strike at the interests of every wage-earner" (RN 5) [V].
- The verdict: "community of goods, must be utterly rejected" (RN 15) [V].
- Against them, RN 5 holds that land bought from savings "is only his wages under another form" [V].

**Leo's earlier text.** *Quod Apostolici Muneris* (28 Dec. 1878) names "socialists, communists, or nihilists" (§1) [V], who "assail the right of property sanctioned by natural law" (§1) [V]. It grounds property in what "springs from nature itself" (§9) [V]. Labour appears there only as one lawful way of acquiring, "labor of brain and hands" (§1) [V]. It gives no argument from labour [V].

**Which socialists.** RN names no one [V]. Its targets, as the literature reads them:
- Continental collectivism.
- Henry George's movement, which Cathrein (1889) called "agrarian socialism" [S: Gaffney 2000].
- The SPD, after the anti-socialist laws lapsed in 1890 [M].

Leo reportedly called socialism a torrent: "Bisogna regolarlo, bisogna incanalarlo" [S: Ondarza 2025]. Msgr. Jacobini told him in 1885 that social Catholics "were called socialists in certain Catholic circles", and Leo replied "Socialism! It's Christianity" [S: Storck 2006, reviewing Aubert]. The anecdote is weakly attested.

**The SPD in 1891.**
- Programme in force: Gotha (1875). Its first sentence, as Marx quotes it, is "Labor is the source of all wealth and all culture" [V]. It holds "the proceeds of labor belong undiminished with equal right to all members of society" [V].
- Marx's critique of it was first published, abridged, in *Die Neue Zeit*, Bd. 1, No. 18, 1890–91, with a foreword by Engels [V].
- Erfurt (October 1891) calls for "the transformation of the capitalist private ownership of the means of production … into social property" [V], and for a "Declaration that religion is a private matter" [V]. It does not mention natural law, natural rights, God or human nature, and it makes no labour-title claim [V].

### Karl Marx

**Final causes in nature.** Darwin's book gives "the death-blow dealt here for the first time to 'teleology' in the natural sciences" [V]. Their "rational meaning is empirically explained" [V]. Source: Marx to Lassalle, 16 Jan. 1861 (marxists.org, from a 1942 International Publishers selection).

**Formal causes.** No locus found. His materialism excludes substantial forms [M].

**God's ends.**
- "Man makes religion, religion does not make man" [V].
- "the criticism of religion is the prerequisite of all criticism" [V].
- Both from the *Critique of Hegel's Philosophy of Right*, Introduction (1844).

**Ends of human nature.** Held early and, in a form, late. Contested.
- "Man is a species-being"; "free, conscious activity is man's species-character" (1844 Manuscripts, "Estranged Labour") [V].
- Against Bentham, one must study "human nature in general, and then with human nature as modified in each historical epoch" (*Capital* I ch. 24 §5 n. 50) [V].
- Against these: "the human essence is no abstraction inherent in each single individual" (Theses on Feuerbach VI, 1845) [V].

**Ends desired.** The labourer "also realises a purpose of his own that gives the law to his modus operandi" (*Capital* I ch. 7) [V].

**Self-preservation and labour.**
- Labour is "the everlasting Nature-imposed condition of human existence" (ch. 7) [V].
- In it man "at the same time changes his own nature" (ch. 7) [V].

**Labour as title.** Granted as the first law of appropriation, then denied under capital:
- "At first the rights of property seemed to us to be based on a man's own labour" [V].
- Then it "turns out to be the right, on the part of the capitalist, to appropriate the unpaid labour of others" (ch. 24 §1) [V].
- Private property is "the product, the result, the necessary consequence, of alienated labor" (1844) [V].
- Against Gotha: "Labor is not the source of all wealth"; "Nature is just as much the source of use values" [V].

**RN 5's point, answered in advance.**
- The *Manifesto* (1848, ch. II) states the objection: abolition takes "the right of personally acquiring property as the fruit of a man's own labour" [V], which is "the groundwork of all personal freedom, activity and independence" [V].
- Its reply: "does wage-labour create any property for the labourer? Not a bit." [V].
- So RN 5 ("wages under another form") contradicts the *Manifesto* on a fact, not on a cause. That RN had the *Manifesto* in view is not documented.

**Land and the common destination.** Land and the means of production are to be social property (the *Manifesto*; Erfurt) [V]. There is no God-given destination [V, by the loci above].

### Ferdinand Lassalle, and the Gotha Programme

**Who, when.** Founded the ADAV (1863), died 1864. His line shaped the Gotha Programme (1875) [M].

**Ends of human nature.** The State's purpose "is the training and development of the human race to freedom" [V]. The State is "this unity of individuals into a moral whole" [V]. Both from *The Working Man's Programme* (1862, tr. Peters 1884), which also cites Fichte [V]. Hegelian, from memory [M].

**God's ends.** The *Programme* makes no reference to God or Providence [V, by absence]. An absence is not an exclusion.

**Final and formal causes in nature.** No locus found [M].

**Self-preservation.** Wages are held at subsistence by "the cruel, rigorous law that governs wages under the present system" (Open Letter, 1 March 1863) [V]. This is the "iron law" [S: Iserloh].

**Labour as title.** "The whole production of labor becomes the claim of the worker, unaffected by any employer" (Open Letter) [V]. Yet "the right to all property once lawfully earned is thoroughly legitimate and unassailable" (*Programme*) [V].

**Land and the common destination.** Workers' producer associations with State credit [V: "the State ought to regard it as its holiest duty to assist"]. No land doctrine found [M].

### Henry George, and the McGlynn affair

**Who, when.**
- *Progress and Poverty* (1879).
- New York mayoral campaign, 1886, with Fr. Edward McGlynn [S: Wikipedia].
- *The Condition of Labor: An Open Letter to Pope Leo XIII*, dated September 1891 [V]; New York, 11 September [S: Wikipedia].

**Labour as title, for products only.**
- "What constitutes the rightful basis of property?" [V]; "Is it not, primarily, the right of a man to himself" [V].
- "Nature acknowledges no ownership or control in man save as the result of exertion." [V] (*P&P* VII.1).
- In the letter, the right is "originating in the right of the individual to himself" [V].
- It "attaches to things produced by labor, but cannot attach to things created by God." [V].

**Land and the common destination.**
- "The equal right of all men to the use of land is as clear as their equal right to breathe the air." (*P&P* VII.1) [V].
- "men are equally entitled to the use of land" (letter, Part I) [V].
- He writes of "the bounty that God has provided for all" (Part III) [V].
- His remedy is to take land value, not land: "it is only necessary therefore to take for common uses what value attaches to land" [S: Wikipedia].

**God's ends, natural law.**
- "The laws of nature are the decrees of the Creator." (*P&P* VII.1) [V].
- "God cannot contradict himself nor impose on his creatures laws that clash." (letter, Part I) [V].
- He quotes Thomas: "Human law is law only in virtue of its accordance with right reason." [V].

**Ends of human nature, reason.** Accepts RN's premise and denies its conclusion for land:
- "Reason, with its attendant forethought, is indeed the distinguishing attribute of man" [V].
- "But it cannot attach to things provided by the reason and forethought of God!" [V].

**Final and formal causes in nature.** No locus checked. His conclusion argues for design and immortality [M].

**The McGlynn affair** [S: Wikipedia, "Edward McGlynn" and "The Condition of Labor"]:
- **1886–87.** Suspended in 1886. Removed from St Stephen's on 14 Jan. 1887. Excommunicated on 4 July 1887.
- **1889.** The Holy Office told Gibbons that George's doctrine was incompatible with Catholic teaching. Nuesse reports it had decided George's works were "deserving of condemnation" [S: Nuesse, quoted on ResearchGate]. Gaffney says the Index ban was kept secret [S: Gaffney 2000, p. 19]. The ban is contested.
- **1892.** Four professors of the Catholic University found McGlynn's single-tax views not contrary to Catholic teaching. Satolli lifted the excommunication on 23 Dec. 1892.
- **1893.** In a private audience the Pope asked whether McGlynn admitted the right of property, and he said yes for "the products of individual industry".
- **Sources.** Mazzella and Zigliara, later drafters of RN, had recommended the excommunication [S: Gaffney, citing Molony p. 57]. Schratz 2010 is the archival study of the Inquisition files on the Knights of Labor, George, McGlynn and Burtsell [V, publisher's page].

### Classical political economy (Smith, Ricardo), the liberal economy's premise

**Labour as title.**
- The property every man has in his own labour is "the original foundation of all other property", and so "the most sacred and inviolable" (*WN* I.x, Part II) [V]. The paragraph number I.x.c.12 is [M].
- "The patrimony of a poor man lies in the strength and dexterity of his hands." [V].
- "Labour was the first price, the original purchase-money that was paid for all things" (*WN* I.v) [V].
- Ricardo: value depends on the relative quantity of labour (*Principles* ch. 1 §1) [M].

**Final causes.**
- "we still distinguish the efficient from the final cause of their several motions and organizations" (*TMS* II.ii.3) [V].
- "means adjusted with the nicest artifice to the ends which they are intended to produce" (*TMS* II.ii.3) [V].
- "the purpose which the Author of nature seems to have intended by it" (*TMS* II.iii, intro.) [V].
- The ends are intended by the Author, so they are extrinsic, as in Locke.

**Self-preservation.** "the support of the individual, and the propagation of the species" (*TMS* II.ii.3) [V].

**Forms, the soul, ends of human nature.** No locus checked [M].

**In RN.** RN names no economist. It blames "the hardheartedness of employers and the greed of unchecked competition" (RN 3) [V], employers who "misuse men as though they were things in the pursuit of gain" (RN 20) [V], and the bargain that falls short of "a dictate of natural justice more imperious and ancient than any bargain between man and man" (RN 45) [V].

### The Catholic liberal economists: the school of Angers (Freppel, Périn)

**Who, when.** Bp Charles-Émile Freppel of Angers (1827–1891) [S: fr.wikipedia]. Charles Périn (1815–1905), Louvain [V, Stevens 2021 abstract].
- In 1890 Freppel organized a conference opposed to the Liège congresses [S: Storck 2006, reviewing Aubert].
- Périn was "strongly influenced by Charles de Coux, the first holder of the Chair of Political Economy" [V, abstract].

**What they hold.**
- They accepted liberal economics and opposed State intervention [S: Storck/Aubert].
- Freppel judged State schooling "inutile, inefficace, et tendant au socialisme d'État" [S: fr.wikipedia].
- Périn placed himself between hedonistic liberalism and the socialism of Lassalle and Marx [S: fr.wikipedia]. His principle: "Le renoncement est la loi de toute créature libre, c'est la loi chrétienne." [S: fr.wikipedia].
- In theology they were Thomist or ultramontane [M]. The four causes do not separate them from Liège.

**RN against them.** RN's option was "opposée au libéralisme excessif de l'école d'Angers" [V, Aubert, Universalis]. It "cautiously sided" with Liège [S: Storck]. Fr.wikipedia says Freppel "influença fortement la rédaction" of RN, unsourced [S]. That conflicts with Aubert.

**Accommodation.** Behr: Leo made "more than one compromise in the text" [V], to keep CST from being "stillborn from hostile reaction by Catholic liberals" [V] (Behr 2024, p. 230, citing Behr 2019).

### The social Catholics: Ketteler, the Liège school, the Fribourg Union

**Ketteler** (Bp of Mainz, d. 1877).
- **1848.** His Advent sermons were "developing the sociology of St. Thomas Aquinas" [V, Goyau, *Catholic Encyclopedia* 1910]. In translation: "The false doctrine that property confers strict rights is a perpetual sin against nature" [S: Droel 2016]. Only God has "full and genuine property rights" [S].
- **1864.** In *Die Arbeiterfrage und das Christentum* he "adopted Lassalle's criticism of the modern treatment of labor" [V, Goyau]. It opens: "Die sog. Arbeiterfrage ist in ihrem Wesen Arbeiterernährungsfrage." [S: Iserloh, *Aufsätze* I, p. 260, attributed from the archive path].
- **Cooperatives.** He wanted cooperatives funded by Christian charity, not State credit [S: Iserloh, p. 261].
- **Leo.** That Leo called him "my great predecessor" is [M].

**The Union de Fribourg** (1884/85–1891; Mermillod, La Tour du Pin, Löwenstein, Liechtenstein, Blome, Kuefstein).
- Its members were "partisans de l'antilibéralisme de la papauté et opposés au libéralisme économique et au socialisme" [V, HLS].
- Its ideas influenced RN "de manière significative (mais pas exclusive)" [V, HLS], but its corporatism did not [V, HLS].
- Its 1888 memorandum asked for subsidiary State wage control and minimum wages. RN did not adopt these [S: Ries 2017, p. 280 n. 24].

**The Liège congresses** (1886, 1887, 1890; Bp Doutreloux) accepted State intervention [S: Storck/Aubert]. Dates are [M].

**Horizon.** Thomist. Their dispute with Angers is about the State and the wage, not the causes.

### Employers, usury and the State: named in RN, not horizons

- **Usury.** RN 3 condemns "rapacious usury" [V], "more than once condemned by the Church" (wording [M]). It is a practice, not a doctrine.
- **Workers' condition.** Workers are under "a yoke little better than that of slavery itself" (RN 3) [V]. The guilds were "abolished in the last century" (RN 3) [V].
- **The State.**
  - "the State must not absorb the individual or the family" (RN 35) [V].
  - It must act "Whenever the general interest or any particular class suffers, or is threatened with harm" (RN 36) [V].
  - The right "is derived from nature, not from man" (RN 47) [V].
- **No node.** These are agents RN instructs. The horizon behind "unchecked competition" is the liberal economy's, entered above.

## Documented replies to RN

- **George**, *The Condition of Labor* (Sept. 1891) [V]. He holds RN is "directed against a theory that we who hold it know to be deserving of your support" [V], and protests "the injustice into which you fall in classing us" with socialists (Part III) [V]. He numbers and answers eight reasons. The mapping to RN paragraphs is mine:
  1. Wages invested (RN 5).
  2. "That private property in land proceeds from man's gift of reason." (RN 6–7) [V].
  3. The earth serves all (RN 8): "the difference between the robber who leaves his victim to die and the robber who puts him to ransom" [V].
  4. "That Industry expended on land gives ownership in the land itself." (RN 9–10) [V]. His answer: "This contention, if valid, could only justify the ownership of land by those who expend industry on it." [V].
  5. Common opinion and divine law (RN 11).
  6. The father (RN 13–14): "With all that your Holiness has to say of the sacredness of the family relation we are in full accord." [V]. But: "how the obligation of the father to the child can justify private property in land we cannot see." [V].
  7. Industry, wealth and love of country (RN 47).
  8. From nature, not from man (RN 47): "masked in the use of the indefinite terms" [V].

  Against RN 10's inseparability he writes: "So far from the value of improvements being inseparable from the value of land, it is in individual transactions constantly separated" [V].
- **Robert Blatchford**, *Socialism: A Reply to the Pope's Encyclical* (Clarion Pamphlet No. 1, 1891) [V]:
  - "The Socialists do not endeavour to destroy private property." [V].
  - "The land is not created by labour, but is the gift of God to all." [V].
  - Turning RN 9 and RN 5: "the land is the right of those who cultivate it, or who support and equip the cultivators" [V]; "he who invests his savings in land becomes a purchaser of stolen property." [V].
- **Charles Périn**, *L'économie politique d'après l'encyclique sur la condition des ouvriers* (1891) and *Note sur le juste salaire d'après l'encyclique Rerum novarum* (1892) [S: fr.wikipedia, titles only]. Contents unchecked [M].
- **Corrigan** read RN as "a pronouncement against Henry George" [S: Gaffney, citing Isacsson]. Manning and Corrigan read it as a direct attack on George [S: Gaffney, citing Geiger].
- **McGlynn** in 1893 affirmed property in "the products of individual industry" [S: Wikipedia]. That is assent to the products half of RN's claim.
- **Charles Coppens SJ**, an American manual written after 1891. It cites the "Encyclical on Labor, 1891" for the just wage [V].
  - It holds "Mere first occupancy is by itself a valid title to ownership." (Thesis XVIII) [V].
  - Against labour as the only title: "If the principle were true that right to ownership can be established only by transforming labor, man could own nothing." (no. 173) [V].
  - It calls George's reasoning "full of sophistry" (no. 173) [V].
  - Title and year (*A Brief Text-Book of Moral Philosophy*, 1895) are [M].
- **Erfurt** (Oct. 1891) was not checked for any reference to RN [M]. It argues from class and production, not from right or nature [V].

## The drafting and the source of RN 9

**The sequence.** Liberatore wrote the first draft in early July 1890 and gave it to Volpini. Zigliara made a second within two months. Mazzella made the main further revisions, Liberatore revised again, and Boccali made the last changes [S: Ries 2017, pp. 280–282, from Antonazzi].
- Ondarza 2025 gives three schemas: Liberatore; Zigliara, reworked by Liberatore and Mazzella; then Volpini for the Latin and Boccali for revision [V].
- The early incipit "Excitata semel rerum novarum cupidine" was changed later [V, Ondarza].
- Leo's own interventions cannot be traced by hand; he suggested changes orally [V, Ondarza].
- Fortin lists "Liberatore, Zigliara, Mazzella, Boccali, Pope Leo himself" among the drafters (p. 229) [V].
- The edition is Giovanni Antonazzi, *L'enciclica Rerum Novarum: testo autentico e redazioni preparatorie dai documenti originali* (Rome: Edizioni di Storia e Letteratura, 1957) [S: Gaffney, references; Fontana 2025]. Ries cites it with a 1991 date (n. 20), perhaps a reissue [S].
- An autograph of Liberatore's first and third Italian schemas has since been found in the archive [S: Fontana 2025, reporting Lombardi, *La Civiltà Cattolica* n. 4199, Nov. 2025].

**What the drafts are known to contain.**
- **First draft.** It derived property as a conclusion from first principles: "Essa [la proprietà privata] discende per immediato discorso da' primi principii della ragione" [S: Fortin n. 16]. It also said "la proprietà privata è sacra" [S: Fortin n. 18].
- **The *ius gentium*.** It "had been given due prominence in the first and the third drafts, both of them prepared mainly by the Jesuit Matteo Liberatore" [V, Fortin p. 211]. The final text omits it. Fortin judges that "cannot have been an oversight" [V], since critics were using Thomas's placing of the *ius gentium* in positive law [V].
- **Zigliara's draft.** In it "both McGlynn and Henry George were given fuller treatment" [S: Gaffney, quoting Molony p. 79].
- **Corporations.** Liberatore's first draft was corporatist [V, Ries p. 281]. The phrase admitting purely workers' unions, "sive totas ex opificibus conflatas", entered only in the third Latin redaction [S: Ries p. 282].
- **Timing.** George's ideas "had a decisive effect on the timing of Rerum and, to some degree, on its contents" [S: Gaffney, quoting Molony p. 50].
- **Where the labour argument entered.** No draft is documented. Fortin does not say which draft introduced it [V, by absence].

**The candidate sources of RN 9–10.**
1. **Thomas, II-II q. 57 a. 3.**
   - A field considered as to its cultivation and peaceful use suits one owner. This is the *ius gentium* (record, [V]).
   - So RN 9's field has a Thomist parent. In Thomas the reason is the field's better use, an end, and not labour as title.
   - RN 8 paraphrases q. 66 a. 2 ad 1 (correction 6), so the paraphrase is textual. The q. 57 link is my inference.
2. **Taparelli, *Saggio teoretico*, Diss. II, c. IV, §§ 398–412.** Table of contents [V, frommann-holzboog edition, ed. Schrage, 2026, reading sample, p. XXVIII]:
   - § 398 "Altro dovere di conservazione, sostentarsi"; § 399 "ne nasce il dritto di dominio".
   - § 403 "Prova di istinto in favor del dominio naturale"; § 404 "il dominio non è tutto istituzione umana".
   - § 405 "equivoco della comunione primitiva dei beni".
   - § 406 "Proprietà stabile: nasce dal dritto di non servire"; § 407 "e dai fatti di propagazione e di necessaria coltura".
   - § 408 "La divisione dei fondi è dello stato naturale non del natio".
   - § 409 "Il coltivare è fatto libero. All'individuo sì non alla specie umana."
   - § 410 "Si spiega un'apparente contraddizione del Suarez."
   - § 411 "Equivoco della ipotesi di prima divisione."
   - § 412 "Modi di acquistar dominio: modi primitivi. Occupazione o accessione."

   RN 6–13 runs in the same order: instinct (RN 6), stable provision (RN 7), cultivation (RN 9), propagation and family (RN 12–13). The parallel is my inference, not documented.

   Taparelli derives dominion from the duty of self-conservation (§§ 398–399), as Wolff derives rights from duties [M on Wolff]. His primitive titles are occupation and accession (§ 412), not labour.

   Liberatore's article "Of Political Economy" "anticipates many passages that reappear in the first draft" [S: De Rosa 1971, quoted by Behr 2024]. Behr argues that Leo and Liberatore followed Taparelli in content but kept his name in the background, to avoid provoking bourgeois opposition [S: Schrage 2019, n. 21].
3. **Liberatore, *Principii di economia politica* (1889).** Costa calls its author "the main drafter" of RN [V, abstract]. Costa reports that it treats land as "the only original all-important production factor" [V, abstract], defends property in land, and that its themes "recur in Rerum Novarum" [V, abstract]. Its property chapter was not reached: archive.org's French text (1899) cut off in the Introduction. Content [M].
4. **Locke, *Second Treatise* §27, and Smith, *WN* I.x.**
   - Fortin: "In Locke's words, which the encyclical echoes almost textually" (p. 214, citing RN 8–10) [V].
   - He adds that "nobody prior to the 17th century had claimed that labor was of itself a title to property" (p. 215) [V].
   - The older teaching: "the laborer deserves a just wage or a material reward commensurate with his efforts" (p. 215) [V].
   - RN 10's "miscenturque" matches Locke's "mixed" (record, [V]). The formula "most sacred and inviolable" is Smith's, then the 1789 Declaration's, art. 17 [V: Fortin p. 212 n. 20; Smith, above].
   - RN cites neither Locke nor Smith [V].
5. **The manual tradition.** Coppens (above) makes first occupancy the title and rejects labour as the only title [V]. Labour is not the manuals' title, as far as checked.

**Finding.** Labour as a title for land is in none of the Catholic sources checked before RN:
- QAM 1878 has labour only as a lawful mode [V].
- Taparelli's titles are occupation and accession [V, contents].
- Coppens, after RN, still makes occupancy the title [V].

In RN the labour argument comes with the products/land distinction. RN 7 claims "non modo terrenorum fructuum, sed ipsius terrae dominatum" [V]. RN 10 answers those who grant "usum quidem soli" but deny ownership [V] by saying improvements cannot be separated from the soil. That is the point George grants (property in products) and the point he denies (inseparability), clause by clause.

Molony puts George's fuller treatment in Zigliara's draft. The likeliest reading is that RN 9–10 answers George's premise from inside it, grafted onto a Taparellian frame, with Lockean and Smithian wording. Which draft introduced it, and with what words, needs Antonazzi.

A side observation, my inference: RN 10 reverses the Roman rule *superficies solo cedit*. The Roman rule gives the improvement to the soil; RN gives the soil to the improver because the improvement cannot be separated.

## Documented clashes

A clash is where a figure inherits incompatible positions and resolves them his own way.

- **Leo, in RN.** Thomas's *ius gentium* (q. 66 a. 2 ad 1, paraphrased in RN 8 and quoted in RN 22) against a natural, prepolitical title (RN 6–7, 9–10, 46). He resolves it three ways:
  - The right is natural (RN 6, 47).
  - Its delimitation is left "industriae hominum institutisque populorum" (RN 8).
  - Use is common by charity, not justice (RN 22).

  Fortin documents the *ius gentium* being removed between drafts (p. 211) [V]. Costa finds "no consistent theory of appropriation can be found in the Encyclical" [V, abstract].
- **George.** The labour title, which he holds, against the common gift of land, which he also holds. He resolves it with property in products and the common value of land, taken by the single tax (letter, Part I) [V].
- **Marx.** The bourgeois law of appropriation by one's own labour, which he grants as "at first", against capitalist appropriation. He resolves it dialectically: the law turns "into their very opposite" (*Capital* I ch. 24 §1) [V].
- **Marx against Gotha.** "Labor is the source of all wealth" against "Nature is just as much the source of use values" [V].
- **Ketteler.** Thomist property-as-use against Lassalle's economics. He takes the wage law and refuses State credit, leaving cooperatives to charity [S: Iserloh].
- **Angers against Liège.** The liberal economy against State intervention. RN takes Liège's side, cautiously [S: Storck/Aubert; V: Aubert, Universalis].
- **Blatchford.** The labour title against landlord property. Land belongs to its cultivators and is God's gift to all [V].

**Flags on the case's typing.** Readings are alternatives (CV4), so a switch can only add availability. Where the Latin adds premises to the argument as committed, a sibling case is needed, not a switch.

- **RN 7, from self-preservation (a switch, `rn7_from_self`, (final, self)).**
  - Warrant: "ius ille suum ad vitam corpusque tuendum habere natura ante debuit quam civitas ulla coisset" [V]; Taparelli §§ 398–399, dominion from the duty "sostentarsi" [V, contents]; the English "the right of providing for the substance of his body" [V].
  - Effect: RN 7 becomes available wherever self_preservation is held, every runtime but `bare`, so `sole_reason(rn_1891, rn9, …)` fires nowhere. PR4's "however RN 7 and RN 13 are read" holds only for the two switches entered.

- **RN 7, under providence (a switch, (final, god_ethics)).** Warrant: "sub lege aeterna, sub potestate omnia providentissime gubernantis Dei" [V]. Effect: like `rn13_as_command`, it keeps RN 7 at `locke` and at the will readings.
- **RN 7's formal premise may be over-typed.** George, a Lockean-type hearer, grants its premise, reason and forethought [V]. Typing it by (formal, human), the soul as form of the body, makes it unfollowable for him.
- **RN 8 is two arguments.**
  - For property: delimitation left "industriae hominum institutisque populorum" [V], which is Thomas's positive addition (efficient, will) with labour added.
  - For use common: its ground is "quoniam nemo est mortalium, quin alatur eo, quod agri efferunt" [V], the sustenance of all, (final, self), beside the common gift. The gift itself is reported with "dicitur" [V].
  - A reading `rn8_from_sustenance` would keep use common reasoned at `hobbes` and `spinoza`, so PR5's first bullet would fail under it.
- **RN 9 is under-typed as committed.** Besides (efficient, human) and (material, nature), the Latin gives:
  - (final, self) and (final, human), in "ad conservandam vitam maximeque ad perficiendam" [V];
  - (formal, human), in "industriam mentis" and "velut formam quamdam personae suae impressam" [V].
  - RN 10 supports the efficient typing: "Quo modo effectae res caussam sequuntur" [V].
  - If the committed typing includes (final, human) or (formal, human), RN 9 is lost exactly where the study finds it sole: `locke`, `hobbes`, `spinoza`, the will readings, and `ockham_nominal` for the formal premise. The finding would reverse. Suggest running a sibling case `rn9_latin` beside the committed one.
- **RN 13.** The Latin is "Sanctissima naturae lex est" [V]: nature's law, not a command. The committed typing (final, human) fits the text. New support for `rn13_as_command` as a hearer's reading: George, in Locke's horizon, was "in full accord" with the premise [V].
- **RN 22 needs a second reading (`rn22_as_stewardship`, (final, god_ethics)).**
  - Warrant: the rich man holds his goods "velut minister providentiae divinae" [V], beside "ad perfectionem sui" [V] (the committed (final, human)).
  - Effect: RN 22 is kept at `locke` and the will readings. PR5's `sole_reason(rn_1891, rn8, q(use_common), yes)` there, and VM2, would not fire under it.
  - Also, RN 22's conclusion is a duty "caritatis christianae" [V], not of justice; th66 is natural right. Whether they support the same value of `use_common` is itself a typing choice.

**What challenges the design.**
1. **The question conflates two objects.** `q(property_by_nature)` covers products and land alike. RN's opponents concede property in products: George [V], Lassalle [V], Blatchford [V], the *Manifesto* on "self-earned property" [V], McGlynn in 1893 [S]. They deny it for land or the means of production. RN itself argues "non modo terrenorum fructuum, sed ipsius terrae" [V]. A split, `q(property_in_products)` and `q(property_in_land)`, would show RN 9–10 as the bridge from the first to the second, which is the step George denies.
2. **The disputes are on facts and objects, not causes.** Angers and Liège share a horizon. George follows RN's premises and denies a minor premise (land is not a product; improvements are separable). Marx denies RN 5's fact (wage labour creates no property [V]). A VM of horizons cannot show any of these.
3. **The hearers' parents are outside the DAG.** Hegel, Fichte, Feuerbach and Darwin (the socialists); Smith and Ricardo (the liberal economy); Spencer and Jefferson (George); Wolff (Taparelli). Every hearer node above rests on `default`, `thomist` or `locke` plus marked edges.
4. **On accommodation.** RN 9 is sole only in the Lockean hearers (`smith`, `george`). For the socialists who held the labour title (`lassalle`, `marx`) it is idle by horizon. The evidence fits an argument *ad hominem* from a premise RN's opponents shared, labour gives property in its fruits, better than an accommodation to a horizon without ends. Fortin (p. 226), Costa (2010) and Behr (2024) support a strategic reading [V]. Hittinger and Booth read it as Thomist [V].
