# Forms in sign: the canons, the Church's practice and the linguistics, the evidence record for TTC-LINGUAE

This is a research record for TTC-LINGUAE, compiled on 23 October 2026. It is an evidence record, so it keeps prose (rule 10). It serves one decision: signed languages enter now as encodings, their forms entered as given through a notation, each notation a claimant; reading a sign from video waits for the senses step. It asks what the law, the Holy See, the bishops' conferences, the theologians and the linguists give for one new case, a sacramental form or consent in a signed language.

**How sure each item is:**
- **[V]** The wording was checked against a primary text or translation fetched for this record. For a secondary work or a claimant's own page, [V] means its own wording was fetched.
- **[S]** It was checked through a named secondary source that quotes or reports the primary.
- **[M]** It comes from memory or a standard citation and was not re-checked. It is unverified until someone checks it in print.

Quotations are under 25 words. They came through a fetch tool that returns short extracts, so a quotation is exact as returned, not proof-read against a printed page. vatican.va was reachable only through that tool. "Proposal" marks this record's own design. "Observation" marks a comparison of two fetched texts that no fetched source makes.

**Corrections to the brief, kept because they shape the encoding:**

1. **Coughlin was not the first deaf priest.** Cyril Axelrod was ordained in Johannesburg in November 1970, and is called "the second born-deaf person to be initiated" into the priesthood [S: Wikipedia, "Cyril Axelrod"]. Coughlin (1977) is the first in the United States [S: UCA News, 3 December 2022]. McNamara's "deaf priests have been ordained since 1977" (2019) [V] is therefore too late.
2. **The 1917 counterpart of c. 1104 §2 is c. 1088 §2, and it is a prohibition, not a permission.** It forbids equivalent signs "si loqui possint", and its verb is "licet" (Canons, below). The 1983 canon turns it into a permission for those who cannot speak.
3. **The 1917 Code allowed confession through an interpreter only to those who could not confess otherwise** (c. 903, "Qui aliter confiteri non possunt") [V]. The 1983 c. 990 drops that condition ("Nemo prohibetur") [V].
4. **The interpreter canon of the trial is c. 1471**, and it names the deaf and the mute [V]. Its Eastern parallel is CCEO c. 1130 [V].
5. **The 1965 permission covered the vernacular parts, and the celebrant was to speak and sign at once** (*Notitiae* 2 (1966) 30–31, n. 95) [V, transcription]. It does not touch a form signed alone.
6. **HamNoSys** was "first version defined in 1984, first published version Prillwitz et al., 1987" [S: Hanke, LREC 2004]. The often-cited 1989 is version 2.0 [M].
7. **The 2009 conference's title differs by text.** Benedict XVI's address gives "Ephphatha! The hearing-impaired person in the life of the Church" [V]. *Dolentium Hominum* 73 gives "Ephphatha! The Deaf Person in the Life of the Church", 19–21 November 2009 [S].

## The canons

### Consent in words or equivalent signs

- **CIC 1983 c. 1104 §2.** "Sponsi consensum matrimonialem verbis exprimant; si vero loqui non possunt, signis aequipollentibus." [V, vatican.va]
- **§1** has "Ad matrimonium valide contrahendum" [V]; §2 has no clause of validity. With c. 10, "Irritantes aut inhabilitantes eae tantum leges habendae sunt, quibus actum esse nullum aut inhabilem esse personam expresse statuitur" [V], §2 does not invalidate (observation).
- Dzierżon reads the requirement of words the same way: "This requirement is not required for validity, but for the fairness of the act" [S: G. Dzierżon, *Ius Matrimoniale* 28 (2017) no. 3, as returned in English]. He reports Bersini: moral impossibility qualifies, not only physical; silence does not [S].
- **CIC 1917 c. 1088 §2.** "Sponsi matrimonialem consensum exprimant verbis; nec aequipollentia signa adhibere ipsis licet, si loqui possint." [V, droitcanonique.fr] The verb is *licet*: liceity.
- **The decretal behind them.** Innocent III (1198) allowed the deaf to marry by signs: what they "cannot express by words, they can declare by means of signs" [S: R. Oates, *The Limping Chicken*, 2023]. That it is *Tuae fraternitatis*, X 4.1.23, is [M].
- **CCEO.** Dzierżon says the Eastern law does not expressly require words, and that some Eastern rites use gestures [S]. The Latin of CCEO c. 837 was not fetched (What I could not verify).
- **The assistant must receive the consent.** "Assistens matrimonio intellegitur tantum qui praesens exquirit manifestationem contrahentium consensus eamque nomine Ecclesiae recipit" (c. 1108 §2) [V]. "Matrimonium facit partium consensus … legitime manifestatus" (c. 1057 §1) [V].
- **Through an interpreter.** "Matrimonium per interpretem contrahi potest; cui tamen parochus ne assistat, nisi de interpretis fide sibi constet" (c. 1106) [V]. 1917: "Matrimonium per interpretem quoque contrahi potest" (c. 1090) [V]; the parish priest is not to assist "nisi adsit iusta causa" and the interpreter's fidelity is beyond doubt (c. 1091) [V].
- **No authentic interpretation** of c. 1104 §2 was found (c. 16 §1: "Leges authentice interpretatur legislator") [V for c. 16; the absence is M]. c. 17 reads a law "secundum propriam verborum significationem in textu et contextu consideratam" [V]. In its own context, *verbis* is set against "loqui non possunt" (observation).

### Confession through an interpreter

- **c. 990.** "Nemo prohibetur quominus per interpretem confiteatur, vitatis quidem abusibus et scandalis atque firmo praescripto can. 983, § 2." [V]
- **c. 983 §2.** "Obligatione secretum servandi tenentur quoque interpres, si detur, necnon omnes alii ad quos ex confessione notitia peccatorum quoquo modo pervenerit." [V]
- **c. 983 §1** on vatican.va reads "verbis vel alio quovis et quavis modo de causa" [V, twice returned so]. The standard order, "alio quovis modo et quavis de causa", is [M]; vatican.va may carry a slip.
- **The penalty.** "Interpres aliique, de quibus in can. 983, § 2, qui secretum violant, iusta poena puniantur, non exclusa excommunicatione" (c. 1386 §2, as vatican.va now numbers it) [V]. USCCB 2017 cites it as c. 1388 §2, the number before *Pascite gregem Dei* [V for the footnote; the date of the renumbering, 8 December 2021, is M].
- **1917.** c. 889 §1: the confessor is not to betray the penitent "verbo aut signo aut alio quovis modo" [V]. §2: "Obligatione servandi sacramentale sigillum tenentur quoque interpres aliique omnes" [V]. c. 903 (correction 3) [V].
- **CCEO c. 733.** §1: "ne verbo aut signo aut alio quovis modo et quavis de causa prodat aliquatenus paenitentem" [V]. §2: "tenentur quoque interpres, si datur, necnon omnes alii" [V]. The seal is broken by a sign as by a word, in 1917 and in the Eastern code. A CCEO canon permitting confession through an interpreter was not found.

### Interpreters in trials

- **c. 1471.** "Si qua persona interroganda utatur lingua iudici vel partibus ignota, adhibeatur interpres iuratus a iudice designatus." "Interpres etiam adhibeatur si surdus vel mutus interrogari debet, nisi forte malit iudex quaestionibus a se datis scripto respondeatur." [V]
- **CCEO c. 1130.** "interpres etiam adhibeatur, si surdus vel mutus interrogari debet, nisi forte mavult iudex quaestionibus a se datis scripto respondeatur." [V]

Whether deafness was held to fall under 1917 c. 984 2° is [M]; Peters treats the history in *Josephinum Journal of Theology* 15 (2008) 427–443 [S, title only].

## Sacramental forms in sign

### The Holy See

- ***Notitiae* 2 (1966) 30–31, n. 95** [V, transcription at notitiae.ipsissima-verba.org; the PDF not opened]:
  - The question: "Utrum lingua sic dicta « per gestus » (sign-language) adhiberi possit in celebratione liturgiae pro surdis?"
  - "Affirmative. Nam est unica ratio qua surdi vere actuose participare possunt sacram liturgiam."
  - The Pope "recenter (14 dec. 1965) benigne concessit", at the request of some conferences.
  - Its scope: "quandocumque ratio pastoralis id suadeat, in omnibus partibus quae lingua vernacula dicuntur."
  - The celebrant's parts: "quae a solo celebrante proferuntur, ipse eodem tempore verba profert et simul illa gestibus significat".
  - The people answer by gestures; in common parts "fideles per gestus signa celebrantis sequuntur."
  - Observation: in December 1965 the Roman Canon was still said in Latin, so the words of consecration were not a part "quae lingua vernacula dicuntur". The vernacular Canon came in 1967 [M].
- **The Consilium's reply of 10 December 1965** to the American bishops' request of July 1965: "With great willingness and kindness, the Holy Father has given his full approval to these suggestions" [S: Hitchcock and Benofy, *Adoremus Bulletin*, June 1997]. Peters gives it as a "Private Reply", in *Canon Law Digest* VI: 552–553 and *The Jurist* 26 (1966) 388–389 [S: canonlaw.info bibliography]. The two dates, 10 and 14 December, are not reconciled by any fetched source.
- **Pius XII, Assisi, 22 September 1956.** "The concelebrants themselves must say over the bread and the wine: 'This is my body,' 'This is my blood,'" [V, English, AAS 48 (1956) 711–725]. "It does not suffice to have and to manifest the intention of making one's own the words and actions of the celebrant" [V].
- **The Holy Office, 8 March 1957**: "only he who pronounces the words of consecration validly celebrates" [S: McNamara, Zenit, 1 July 2008]. The Latin and the AAS page are [M].
- **McNamara's reading of both:** they concerned the "verbum mentis", "the simple mental or interior repetition of the sacramental form with no exterior sign" [V, Zenit, 5 March 2019].
- **DDF, *Gestis verbisque* (2 February 2024).** "The form of the Sacrament is constituted by the word, which gives a transcendent meaning to matter" (n. 14) [V]. Matter includes "a particularly eloquent action (like the sign of the cross, the laying on of hands", and "marital consent" (n. 13) [V]. "the priest pronounces the Lord's own words" (n. 24) [V]. "In doubtful cases, where there has been an alteration in the form or the matter", discernment "lies within the competence of the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith" (footnote 31) [V]. No sign language, deaf person or disability is named [V, by absence].
- **Benedict XVI, 20 November 2009**, to the 24th international conference of the Pontifical Council for Health Care Workers: "you are not only recipients of the Gospel message but also legitimately heralds of it, by virtue of your Baptism" [V]. The address does not mention sign language or the liturgy [V, by absence].
- **The conference** (*Dolentium Hominum* 73) [S, as returned]:
  - Castiglione counts "the thirteen priests without hearing who at the present time work in many parts of the world".
  - Graybill: "The Untied States of America currently has eleven deaf priests and seven deaf deacons" (sic). The two counts disagree.
  - Graybill asks for "liturgies in ASL" and to "receive sacraments in the same language".
  - Kelly says a BSL Mass prayer "was accepted here in Rome as good and true". No act is cited.
  - Castiglione quotes Roetti (1879): "Holy Communion can never be given to people deaf from birth because they are perpetual infants".
  - The fetched part has no paper on a signed form. Axelrod's paper was not reached.
- **No text of the Holy See** was found that addresses a minister signing the form of baptism, absolution or consecration, or any indult for a deaf priest. McNamara: "I have been unable to find a definitive official declaration that would close the debate" [V, 2019].

### The bishops' conferences

- **England and Wales, *Eucharistic Prayer for the Deaf*** [V, liturgyoffice.org]:
  - It "is only for use in an assembly of the deaf and is to be signed by the celebrant himself".
  - "This prayer may never be used without it being signed."
  - "The celebrant must always pronounce all the words."
  - It was approved for use in England and Wales on 23 May 1992. It was submitted to the Congregation for Divine Worship in 1977, and the note reports no answer.
  - It was "composed by a group of priests expert in the pastoral care of the deaf". It names no sign language.
  - Its words of institution are the English: "THIS IS MY BODY WHICH WILL BE GIVEN UP FOR YOU."
- **USCCB, *Guidelines for the Celebration of the Sacraments with Persons with Disabilities*, revised edition** (approved 15 June 2017) [V, the PDF as hosted by the Diocese of Allentown]:
  - n. 30: the deaf "should have the opportunity to confess to a priest able to communicate with them in sign language", "if sign language is their primary means of communication." They "may also confess through an approved sign language interpreter of their choice." "The interpreter has the obligation to observe secrecy." Failing both, in writing or by a device passed back and forth. Footnotes: c. 990; cc. 983 §2 and 1388 §2.
  - n. 45: the deaf "are to be offered the opportunity to express their matrimonial consent in sign language, if sign language is their primary means of communication." Footnote 55: "See CIC, c. 1104, §2." Or "with the assistance of a sign language interpreter whose trustworthiness has been certified by the pastor" (fn. 56: c. 1106).
  - n. 37: "The existence of a physical disability is not considered in and of itself as disqualifying a person from Holy Orders."
  - Nothing on a minister signing a form [V, by absence]. The 1995 original was not fetched; its approval date (June 1995) is [M].
  - Observation: n. 45's condition is "primary means of communication", not the canon's "loqui non possunt".
- **The American plan to make ASL a liturgical language.** In November 1996 the bishops accepted a plan including it. The Bishops' Committee on the Liturgy said such requests "require confirmation by the Apostolic See prior to the preparation of liturgical texts". In June 1993 it had declined the England and Wales prayer [S: Hitchcock and Benofy 1997]. No outcome was found.
- **Approved texts in a sign language.** None with a confirmation of the Holy See was found, in ASL, BSL, LIS or LSF. McNamara: "there is still no official translation of the Latin Missal into sign language" [V, 2019]. Paris, 2022: a hearing priest trained in LSF; no approval of an LSF text mentioned [V, *Paris Notre-Dame*]. A German Eucharistic prayer for the deaf is the subject of A. Lameri, *Rivista Liturgica* (2003) 135–141 [S, title only].

### The deaf priests

- **Cyril Axelrod**, born 1942, ordained November 1970 in Johannesburg; later a Redemptorist; deafblind, he signs by touch [S: Wikipedia].
- **Thomas Coughlin**, "Ordained in 1977 for the Trinitarians" [S: UCA News, 3 December 2022]. In 2022 he "prays the Eucharistic Prayer using sign language", while a speaker at a microphone voices "Father Coughlin's words to the hearing audience" [S].
- **Benedict Park Min-seo**, ordained in Seoul on 6 July 2007, "the first deaf-mute priest in Asia"; at his first Mass (8 July) he said the words "Using sign language" [V, Fides, 17 July 2007]. Fides says nothing of voicing or permission. CNS (2021) calls him "one of fewer than two dozen deaf priests worldwide" [S, *Catholic Leader*].
- **Matthew Hysell**, ordained 7 December 2012, "Canada's first deaf priest" [S: *Catholic Register*, 13 November 2016].
- **Joseph Bruce** (Providence) celebrated in ASL with a voice interpreter [S: *New York Times*, 22 April 2006, through J. Miller's blog].

### Whether a deaf priest celebrating in sign must also voice the words

No act of the Holy See answers it. The views:

- **Speak and sign:**
  - *Notitiae* 1966 (above): "verba profert et simul illa gestibus significat" [V]. This is a rule for a celebrant who can speak.
  - England and Wales 1992: "The celebrant must always pronounce all the words" [V].
  - Akin (*This Rock*, 1998): "the priest is to say the parts of the Mass particular to him … both out loud and in sign". He adds that "the Church has not established that sign language constitutes a valid method" [S: Miller blog, 2006]. In 2008 he "could see it going either way" [V, his blog].
  - Zuhlsdorf (2011): the forms "must be pronounced aloud, even if very quietly". "I don't believe that the forms of sacraments can be done in sign language" [V].
- **Sign suffices:**
  - Peters: the question is "much more theological than linguistic or pastoral" [V, canonlaw.info I]. His study is "The ordination of men bereft of speech and the celebration of sacraments in sign language", *Studia Canonica* 42 (2008) 331–345 [S, cited on his site]. It "proposes a positive conclusion" [S: McNamara 2019].
  - McNamara (2019): "I concur with the conclusion as to the validity of sign language alone when the priest lacks speech" [V]. The signing priest uses "visible and not audible words" [V]. "This would be the position found in most theological manuals" refers to vocalization [V].
  - Hysell (thesis, Newman Theological College, 2009): "the power of the words is not in the utterance but in the faith of the Church that it conveys" [S: *Catholic Register*].
- **Open:** Catholic Answers (Broussard, 2022): the Church "has no official position" [V].

## Theology

- **Thomas, ST III q. 60 a. 6** [V, Corpus Thomisticum]:
  - With Augustine: "verba inter homines obtinuerunt principatum significandi".
  - So "necesse fuit ut significatio rerum sensibilium per aliqua verba determinaretur".
  - arg. 1 calls the sacraments "quasi quaedam verba visibilia". ad 1: visible things are called words "per similitudinem quandam", by a power of signifying "quae principaliter est in ipsis verbis".
  - ad 2: "Sub rebus autem comprehenduntur etiam ipsi actus sensibiles." A sensible act is, by default, matter.
- **III q. 60 a. 7 ad 1**: the sense "est idem apud omnes, licet non eaedem voces quantum ad sonum" [V]. Thomas's variable is the *vox* in its sound. Whether a manual form is another *vox* of the one sense, or no *vox*, is the question this record leaves open.
- **Florence**, *Exultate Deo*: the sacraments are perfected "rebus tamquam materia, verbis tamquam forma" (DH 1312) [M; *Gestis verbisque* cites DH 1312 without the phrase, V].
- **Marriage: signs stand for words.** Supplement q. 45 a. 2 [V, English, Dominican Fathers]:
  - "the consent which makes a marriage must also be expressed in words".
  - Objection 2 names those "unable to express their mutual consent in words, through being dumb or of different languages".
  - Reply: "they can do so by signs, and such signs count for words". The Latin is [M].
- **Confession by signs.** Supplement q. 9 a. 3 [V, English]:
  - "we generally make use of that act which is most commonly employed for the purpose of manifestation".
  - "It is enough for one who is ignorant of a language, to confess by writing, or by signs, or by an interpreter" (ad 2).
  - The Supplement is compiled from the *Scriptum* on the Sentences [M].
- **Augustine, *De doctrina christiana* II.3.4** [V, Shaw]:
  - Signs to the eyes: "when we nod, we give no sign except to the eyes of the man"; "some convey a great deal by the motion of the hands"; actors give signs "by movements of all their limbs".
  - Then: "words have obtained far and away the chief place as a means of indicating the thoughts of the mind".
  - This is the sentence Thomas quotes in a. 6. Augustine sets the hand's signs in the class beneath words. Whether a sign language falls in that class is the record's question, not his.
  - II.4.5, letters as signs of words that make voices visible, is [M].
- **Augustine, *De magistro* 3.5**: "ut homines cum surdis gestu quasi sermocinentur" [S: I. Correia, *A Arte de Ensinar os Surdos pela Vista*, p. 49 n. 3]. The *quasi* marks the likeness. *De quantitate animae* 18.31–32 was not reached; whether it speaks of the deaf is [M].
- **The moral manuals.** Bamberg, "Passion autour des signes et confession du sourd: enquête à partir de manuels de morale", *Praxis Juridique et Religion* 15 (1998) 97–155 [S, title only]. Naz, *DDC* VI (1957) 958, "Muet"; VII (1965) 1117–1118, "Surdité"; Palazzini, *Dictionarium* IV 422–423, "Surditas" [S, titles only]. None was read.
- **The positions, as the record has them** (observation):
  - **r_vocal.** "Verba" and *voces* are spoken. A manual form is an act, so matter (III q. 60 a. 6 ad 2; *Gestis verbisque* 13). The sacrament needs a spoken form. Holders: Zuhlsdorf; Akin, for practice; the manuals as McNamara reports them.
  - **r_lingual.** "Verba" are the words of a language in any route, and the sense is "idem apud omnes". A sign language's signs are words, not gestures in Augustine's class. *Gestis verbisque* 14 has "the word", without voice. Holders: Peters; McNamara, where speech is lacking; Hysell.
  - Thomas and Innocent III already let signs count for words in consent. That is the one sacrament with a text, and it is a sacrament whose form is the parties' own expression.

## Linguistics

- **Stokoe.** *Sign Language Structure: An Outline of the Visual Communication Systems of the American Deaf*, Studies in Linguistics, Occasional Papers 8 (Buffalo, 1960) [S: catalogue records]. The notation was "first published as the organizing principle of Sign Language Structure" and used in the *Dictionary* of 1965 (Stokoe, Casterline, Croneberg) [S: Wikipedia, "Stokoe notation"].
  - Parameters: tab (location), dez (handshape), sig (movement); some "split off ori (orientation of the hand) as a fourth parameter" [S].
  - It "does not provide for facial expression", mouthing, eye gaze or posture [S].
  - "Recognition of the legitimacy of ASL was achieved by William Stokoe" [S: Wikipedia, "American Sign Language"].
- **ASL's descent.** ASL "emerged as a language at the American School for the Deaf," founded in 1817 at Hartford, where Laurent Clerc "taught using French Sign Language (LSF)" [S: Wikipedia]. It drew also on Martha's Vineyard Sign Language, which "was used even by hearing residents whenever a deaf person was present" [S]. Groce, *Everyone Here Spoke Sign Language* (1985), is [M].
- **Not English.** "ASL is not based on English; it has its own grammar and vocabulary." "ASL is not mutually intelligible with either British Sign Language (BSL) or Auslan." [S: Wikipedia] BSL and ASL have "only 31% signs identical, or 44% cognate" [S: Wikipedia, "British Sign Language"]. ISO 639-3: ASL `ase`, BSL `bfi` [S].
- **Signed English is a code, not a language.** SEE-II, "First published in 1972 by Gustasen [sic], Pfetzing, and Zawolkow", aims to be "an exact representation of English language vocabulary and grammar"; it "is not considered a language itself like ASL" [S: Wikipedia, "Signing Exact English"]. It takes much vocabulary from ASL, with initialized handshapes [S]. Peters: "ASL is *not* simply English rendered in manual form" [V, canonlaw.info II]. He grants ASL takes "some Pidgin Signed English structures" [V, I].
- **The notations, as schemes with claimants.**
  - *Glosses.* Glosses are "typically all-capitalized", but "glossing is not used to write the language for speakers of ASL" [S: Wikipedia]. A gloss names a sign with an English word; the word is the glosser's claim.
  - *Stokoe notation.* Alphabetic, three or four parameters, no non-manuals [S].
  - *HamNoSys.* "an alphabetic system describing signs on a mostly phonetic level", meant for "virtually all sign languages". "the descriptive power of the existing system with respect to nonmanuals is rather limited". It "still lacks a reduction method to identify one of these as the canonical notation" [S: Hanke, LREC 2004].
  - *SignWriting.* Valerie Sutton, 1974; "the first sign writing system included in the Unicode Standard" [S: Wikipedia]. Its ISO 15924 code `Sgnw` is [M].
- **Pronouns and verbs in ASL.**
  - "A first person pronoun is indicated when a signer points at herself"; pointing at the addressee "would be translated as a second person pronoun" [S: D. Michel, *San Diego Linguistic Papers* 4, 2013].
  - Singular "YOU" is "a singular point (i.e., no sweeping motion)". For the plural "you would sweep that motion across space" [S: UT Austin, ASL Online].
  - "Verbs must agree with both the subject and the object" [S: Wikipedia]. The analysis is disputed: Michel holds the paths "are cases of pronominal incorporation rather than agreement" [S]. Liddell (*Grammar, Gesture, and Meaning in ASL*, 2003) calls them indicating verbs [M; fetch failed].
  - Plural reference "results in a loss of indexicality" [V, abstract: K. Cormier, PhD thesis, Texas, 2002].
  - ASL may be counted "a 'pro-drop' language" [S: Michel]. Lillo-Martin (1986) on null arguments is [M].
  - That a raised brow marks a yes/no question is [M]: the fetched page says only that non-manuals "can indicate grammatical properties" [S].
- **BAPTIZE.** Two "A" hands moved "down a bit as you turn them sideways" (immersion), or a hand "as if sprinkling your own head". The sign "depends on which church you go to and what manner of baptism your church uses" [S: Lifeprint, W. Vicars]. Lifeprint does not say whether it is a plain or an agreeing verb; that it is plain is [M]. The sign depicts a manner the Latin *baptizo* leaves open.
- **Religious vocabulary.** Costello, *Religious Signing*, "nearly 600 signs" [S: Hitchcock and Benofy]; its date, 1986, is [M]. Peters: "The theological deficiencies of ASL vocabulary are real" [V].

## Proposed case

`linguae_signed` (proposal). The forms are hypothetical, because no approved signed form exists. So the case is a switch program marked `HYPOTHETICAL` (rule 6), with no names or places (rule 13). It is loaded beside `linguae_we_baptize`, whose part `we_baptize` stays as it is. Video stays with the senses step: a form enters only as some notation's claim.

### Facts

- **Baptism.** In danger of death, a deaf lay person baptizes an infant by pouring water. No hearing person is present (c. 861 §2). The form is signed in ASL, and later given by two transcribers. The infant lives, and the parish must decide whether the baptism stands (c. 869 §1).
- **Absolution.** A deaf priest absolves a deaf penitent in ASL, without voice. A variant uses a hearing priest and an interpreter (c. 990).
- **Consent.** Two deaf parties consent in ASL before an assisting priest and two witnesses. One party can also speak. Variant A: the priest signs. Variant B: he does not, and an interpreter is used (c. 1106).

### Encodings and notations

- **Encodings.**
  - `encoding(ase_2026,"ase",2026,2026)`, with the proposed `encoding_route(ase_2026, manual)`.
  - `en_2026` and the Latin typical text, from `linguae_we_baptize`.
  - `en_see2`, SEE-II, with `code_of(en_see2, en_2026)`: a manual code of English, first published 1972. Its tag, `en-x-see2`, is a private-use proposal.
- **Notations.** Each is a scheme with a claimant, never an encoding:
  - `notated(F, gloss_en, transcriber_a)`: an English gloss, such as `IX-1 BAPTIZE IX-2 NAME FATHER SON HOLY-SPIRIT`.
  - `notated(F, hamnosys, transcriber_b)`: phonetic, with no lexicon.
  - `signwriting` and `stokoe`, as further claimants.
  - Two notations of one performance may disagree, and then both run.
- **Tokens and words.** `token(F, I, T)` from a notation, and `means(T, W, C)` from a lexicon claim (Costello's register; an everyday lexicon). A gloss label is not a word: `gloss_as_word(F)` is refused where a gloss token is entered as the word, or read as an English form.
- **Fingerspelling** is the manual alphabet, a script of English. A fingerspelled token is an English form inside an ASL form: `form_in` with two encodings, never one.

### The features, computed

- **`minister_act`** from the subject of BAPTIZE (or of the absolution verb):
  - `IX-1` gives `expressed`.
  - `WE` gives `other_agent`, as "We baptize".
  - No subject token gives two readings under the pro-drop claim, `expressed` (recovered from the signer) and `unexpressed`. They are `disputed`, and both run.
- **Number of the addressee.**
  - `IX-2`, a point with no sweep, closes `r_plural`. It renders *te* univocally on number, against English "you" (BF3).
  - `IX-2` with a sweep is plural.
  - The SEE-II "YOU" encodes the English form, so it opens `r_plural` as English does. A SEE-II form is a transliteration (`code_of`), so no rendering mode is computed between it and the English, just as `revises/2` is never a rendering.
- **`trinity_invoked`**: `yes` where FATHER, SON and HOLY-SPIRIT mean the three Persons under the religious lexicon. Under an everyday lexicon FATHER is a human father and the value is `no`. Both run, each with its claimant.
- **Manner.** `form_feature(F, depicts_manner, immersion, C)` against `matter_used(E, infusion)` gives `info(iconic_manner_differs(F,E))`, never a refusal. The sign's iconicity is not matter (c. 854 admits both). `broader(baptizo, baptize_immersion, C)` makes the rendering narrower.
- **Non-manuals.** A notation without a non-manual tier (Stokoe; most glosses) leaves the mood unentered, whether a statement or a yes/no question. This gives `info(notation_gap(F, N, nonmanual))`. A notation that has the tier can supply it.
- **The route.** `form_feature(F, route, manual, C)`.

### Whether ASL's verbs settle "I" and "we"

They do not need to. IX-1 and WE are distinct signs, just as "I" and "we" are distinct words, so the written form is not ambiguous in either tongue. The gain is in the second person: ASL's pronoun closes the number that English "you" opens. Agreement does not help BAPTIZE, which the sources do not class as agreeing [M]. Where a verb does carry paths, the analysis is a claim (agreement, indicating verbs or incorporation). A dropped IX-1 reopens `minister_act`. Whether a video shows IX-1 or WE, and whom IX-2 points at, is the senses step's question.

### What is disputed

- **Teaching.** Is a manual form a "verborum forma" (c. 849), "verbis tamquam forma" (DH 1312), "the word" (*Gestis verbisque* 14)? This is `form_reading(route_manual, r_vocal | r_lingual, C)`, with the claimants of Theology above.
  - No source of tier 1 to 3 answers it. c. 841 reserves it, and *Gestis verbisque* sends doubts to the DDF.
  - So `q(valid_form(baptism, ase_i_baptize))` and `q(valid_form(penance, ase_absolution))` have no value from a source, and run both ways.
  - The England and Wales rubric and the 1966 response are governance (c. 838). Entered as answering validity, each is refused (`teaching_from_law`). Entered as practice, each weighs at its tier.
  - A view entered as fixing is refused (`mode_fixed_by_claim`).
- **Canon, baptism.** The doubt of c. 869 §1 is a fact the canon layer takes from teaching (`premise/3`). On the `r_vocal` branch the doubt stands, and conditional baptism follows. On `r_lingual`, with `expressed` and `yes`, it does not. A missing IX-1 or an everyday FATHER puts the doubt on both branches.
- **Canon, consent** (c. 1104 §2: rule 2's disputed reading, no authentic interpretation found):
  - `r_verba`: the signs of a sign language are *verba*, so consent in ASL satisfies the first clause, whether or not the party can speak.
  - `r_signa`: they are *signa aequipollentia*, licit only "si loqui non possunt". Its claimants are the context of §2 (c. 17), the 1917 c. 1088 §2, and Thomas's "such signs count for words".
  - The USCCB (n. 45, fn. 55) is a third claimant, whose condition is "primary means of communication".
  - Validity holds on every branch: c. 10, with §1's "valide" against §2's silence, and Dzierżon. Liceity fails only on `r_signa` for the party who can speak, and only where that fact is not disputed.
  - c. 1108 §2: the assistant must receive the manifestation. In variant B, c. 1106 makes his assisting licit only if the interpreter's fidelity "sibi constet". That is a fact, entered yes or no.
- **Canon, absolution.** The interpreter variant is c. 990 and c. 983 §2, with the penalty of c. 1386 §2, and it computes cleanly. The signed absolution itself carries the baptism's dispute. The internal forum is never computed (rule 2).

### Predictions to add (proposal, in the BF style)

- **SG1.** `IX-1 BAPTIZE IX-2 …` under the religious lexicon: `minister_act` `expressed`, `trinity_invoked` `yes`, and number closed (no `opens(…, r_plural)`).
- **SG2.** The same in SEE-II: `opens(…, r_plural)` as in English (BF3), and no rendering mode computed between SEE-II and English.
- **SG3.** No subject token: `minister_act` is `disputed`, both values run, and `q(valid_form(…))` keeps every branch.
- **SG4.** A gloss token entered as a word is refused (`gloss_as_word`). A Stokoe notation of the same form reports `notation_gap(…, nonmanual)`.
- **SG5.** Baptism: `valid_form` has no sourced value. The England and Wales rubric entered as answering it is refused (`teaching_from_law`). c. 869 §1 gives conditional baptism on `r_vocal` only.
- **SG6.** Consent: valid on `r_verba`, `r_signa` and the USCCB branch alike. Illicit only on `r_signa` with "can speak" not disputed. Variant B is licit only where the interpreter's fidelity is entered as established.
- **SG7.** The iconic manner of BAPTIZE against poured water gives `info`, never a refusal.
- **SG8.** `linguae_we_baptize`'s outputs are unchanged (M1).
