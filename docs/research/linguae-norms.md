# Where fidelity lives: the Church's norms of translation, the evidence record for TTC-LINGUAE

This is a research record for TTC-LINGUAE, compiled on 23 October 2026. It is an evidence record, so it keeps prose (rule 10). It reads the Holy See's documents on translation as a theory of translation, and asks one question of them. Is the mode of a rendering ("a faithful translation", "an interpretation") a fact about two texts, a claim, or something relative to a hearing? It answers `linguae-sources.md`, "What challenges the design", points 5, 6, 11 and 12.

**How sure each item is:**
- **[V]** The wording was checked against a primary text or translation fetched for this record. For a secondary work, [V] means its own wording was fetched.
- **[S]** It was checked through a named secondary source that quotes or reports the primary.
- **[M]** It comes from memory or a standard citation and was not re-checked. It is unverified until someone checks it in print.

Quotations are under 25 words. They came through a fetch tool that returns short extracts, so a quotation is exact as returned, not proof-read against a printed page. "Proposal" marks this record's own design. "Observation" marks a comparison of two fetched texts that no fetched source makes.

## Corrections to the brief, kept because they shape the encoding

1. **LA does not name CLP.** n. 8 substitutes its norms "for all norms previously published on the matter", except *Varietates legitimae* [V]. Neither the name nor the date of CLP was found in the English [V, by absence]. EWTN's note: CLP's norms "remained in force until 25 April 2001" [V]. So the replacement is general, not express.
2. **"Foreignizing" and "domesticating" are Venuti's terms.** Venuti traces their roots "to Schleiermacher and his 1813 essay" [S: Munday 2016, p. 225]. Schleiermacher's own figure is moving the reader or the writer (Theory, below) [V].
3. **Francis to Sarah did not abrogate LA 79–84.** In the Italian, some numbers "sono stati abrogati o sono decaduti", with 76 and 80 as examples. The rest "vanno attentamente ri-compresi, inclusi i nn. 79-84" [V, the Italian as printed by InfoVaticana]. `linguae-sources.md` had "LA 79–84 'have been abrogated'" [S: Adoremus]. That item should be corrected.
4. **MP borrows two sentences without citing them.** Its "utpote partes ipsorum rituum … vox" is Paul VI's 1965 "versiones factae sunt partes ipsorum rituum, factae sunt vox Ecclesiae" [V, both]. Its statement of the translator's end is CLP 6's [V, both]. The echoes are this record's observation; no commentary fetched makes them.
5. **CLP's English is ICEL's.** The liturgyoffice text is "© 1982 International Committee on English in the Liturgy" [V]. The French original is in *Notitiae* 5 (1969) 3–12, and the English is DOL 123 [S: Lang 2010]. Moroney (USCCB, 2011) dates CLP to "1968" [V]; that is a slip.
6. **No fetched source documents CLP's dependence on Nida.** CLP uses none of the words "dynamic", "equivalence", "Nida", "linguistics" [V, by absence]. The dependence is asserted (Lang, Beall, Holquin) and argued from likeness. See CLP, "Nida".
7. **The Italian Missal of 2020 keeps "per tutti".** It "continues to say Jesus' blood was poured out 'per tutti'" [S: Crux, CNS, August 2020]. That is after Benedict XVI's letter of 2012 and under MP.
8. **CLP itself sends explanation to catechesis.** In biblical readings "In no way should there be a paraphrasing"; "All this is the task of catechesis and the homily" (CLP 31) [V]. Arinze's "rather an explanation of the sort that belongs properly to catechesis" (2006) and LA 29 have the same shape [V].

## 1. *Comme le prévoit* (1969)

### The act

- Consilium for Implementing the Constitution on the Sacred Liturgy, "On the Translation of Liturgical Texts for Celebrations with a Congregation", "Issued January 25, 1969" [V, EWTN].
- Lang: it "was published in six major languages, but not in Latin"; "it bears no official signature and it was not published in the Acta Apostolicae Sedis" [S: Lang 2010]. These are facts of form, which rule 14 needs; none was checked here.
- It foresees its own revision: "Above all, after sufficient experiment and passage of time, all translations will need review" (CLP 1) [V]. "It is the duty of the episcopal conferences to decide which texts are to be translated" (CLP 2) [V].

### Translation as communication

- "A liturgical text, inasmuch as it is a ritual sign, is a medium of spoken communication" (CLP 5) [V].
- The end: "The purpose of liturgical translations is to proclaim the message of salvation to believers", and to express the Church's prayer (CLP 6) [V; the second clause S, liturgyoffice paraphrase].
- "To achieve this end, it is not sufficient that a liturgical translation merely reproduce the expressions and ideas of the original text" (CLP 6) [V].
- It must "faithfully communicate to a given people, and in their own language," what the Church "originally intended to communicate to another people in another time" (CLP 6) [V].
- "A faithful translation, therefore, cannot be judged on the basis of individual words" (CLP 6) [V]. Instead "the total context of this specific act of communication must be kept in mind," with the target's literary form [V].
- One must weigh "the message to be conveyed, but also the speaker, the audience, and the style" (CLP 7) [V]. "Translations, therefore, must be faithful to the art of communication in all its various aspects" (CLP 7) [V].
- "the translator should give first consideration to the meaning of the communication" (CLP 8) [V].
- "The accuracy and value of a translation can only be assessed in terms of the purpose of the communication" (CLP 14) [V].

### The unit

- "The translator must always keep in mind that the 'unit of meaning' is not the individual word but the whole passage" (CLP 12) [V]. Its example: "the piling up of ratam, rationabilem, acceptabilem may increase the sense of invocation" [V].
- Three cases (CLP 21–24): "Sometimes a text can be translated word for word and keep the same meaning as the original" (22) [V]. "Sometimes the metaphors must be changed to keep the true sense, as in locum refrigerii in northern regions" (23) [V]. The third: "so long as the teaching of the Gospel remains intact", inappropriate expressions are to be avoided (24) [V].
- The collects "may need to be rendered somewhat more freely while conserving the original ideas" (CLP 34) [V], "by moderately amplifying them or, if necessary, paraphrasing" [V].
- Said aloud: "It will be insufficient to translate only the exact meaning of an idea unless the text can also be expressed by sound and rhythm" (CLP 35) [V].
- "The literary genre of every liturgical text depends first of all on the nature of the ritual act" (CLP 26) [V].

So CLP's unit is the passage within the act, and the measure is the communication's purpose.

### The hearer

- "The language chosen should be that in 'common' usage" (CLP 15) [V], for "children and persons of small education", quoting Paul VI's address of 10 November 1965 (note: "DOL 113 no. 787") [V].
- Its example: "temptation as a translation of tentatio in the Lord's Prayer is inaccurate" (CLP 15) [V].
- Further principles serve so that "a translation will be understood by the hearers in the same sense as the revealed truths expressed in the liturgy" (CLP 16) [V].
- "The prayer of the Church is always the prayer of some actual community, assembled here and now" (CLP 20) [V]. "It is not sufficient that a formula handed down from some other time or region be translated verbatim"; it "must become the genuine prayer of the congregation" [V].
- A common text must satisfy the "different needs and mentalities of each region" (CLP 42) [V].

The hearer is named, situated and dated: this congregation, here and now. CLP 16 adds that the hearer is to understand the same sense as the revealed truths, so the hearing is not the measure alone.

### The limits it sets itself

- Latin terms "must be considered in the light of their uses-historical or cultural, Christian or liturgical" (CLP 11) [V]. "ieiunium now has the sense of lenten observance, both liturgical and ascetic" (CLP 13) [V].
- Where "there is no word in common use that exactly corresponds to the biblical or liturgical sense of the term" (CLP 18) [V]: "In many modern languages a biblical or liturgical language must be created by use" (CLP 19) [V].
- Scripture: "the revelation communicated by the documents cannot be entirely divorced from these forms or genres" (CLP 30) [V]. The characteristics of each mode of communication "should be preserved with special accuracy", "particularly important in the translations of psalms and canticles" [V].
- Readings "ought to conform 'with the Latin liturgical text'"; "Nor should words or explanatory phrases be inserted" (CLP 31) [V].
- It repeats DV 22: "suitable and accurate translations be made into the different languages from the original texts" (CLP 32) [V].
- Anaphoras, prefaces, consecratory prayers "should be translated integrally and faithfully, without variations, omissions, or insertions" (CLP 33) [V]. Yet for ancient texts, "an astute handling and sometimes a paraphrasing, in order to render accurately the original pregnant meaning" [V].

So CLP does not abstract the message from every form. For Scripture and the anaphora it binds the translator nearly as LA does. The words over the chalice fall under both (31 and 33).

### New texts

- "Texts translated from another language are clearly not sufficient for the celebration of a fully renewed liturgy." "The creation of new texts will be necessary" (CLP 43) [V].
- Translating the tradition is "the best school and discipline for the creation of new texts"; SC 23's organic growth is cited [S: liturgyoffice, paraphrase with that phrase quoted].
- Committees "of experts in the various disciplines" (38); "An ad interim translation should be properly approved by the liturgical commission" (39); a "mixed commission" for a common language (41) [V].

### Nida

- **Nida's own terms** (1964, as reprinted in Venuti, *The Translation Studies Reader*): "Formal equivalence focuses attention on the message itself, in both form and content" (p. 129) [V]. A dynamic translation follows "the principle of equivalent effect": "the relationship between receptor and message should be substantially the same" (p. 129) [V]. It "aims at complete naturalness of expression" (p. 129) [V]. It is "the closest natural equivalent to the source-language message" (p. 136) [V].
- The orientation is "not so much toward the source message, as toward the receptor response" (*Toward a Science of Translating*, 1964, pp. 166–168) [V, Marlowe's excerpt].
- Nida and Taber, *The Theory and Practice of Translation* (1969), define dynamic equivalence by the receptors' response [M; the definition's wording was not found]. Nida later preferred "functional equivalence" (de Waard and Nida 1986, pp. 7, 36) [S: Wikipedia, quoting Long 2005].
- **The claim of dependence.** Lang: "The Instruction Comme le prévoit endorsed a translation theory known as 'dynamic equivalence'", "developed by Eugene Nida" [V, Lang 2010]. His note cites only Nida 1964 (Brill) [V]. Beall: CLP "does not use the term" but endorses "its substance" [V, Beall 1996]. Holquin calls it "a method developed primarily by the renowned linguist Eugene Nida" [V, 2026], without citation.
- **The evidence.**
  - *Textual likeness.* CLP's "message", "audience", "meaning of the communication" and "purpose of the communication" (7, 8, 14) are Nida's frame. Its "equivalents in modern English" (13) is the only "equivalent" in it [V].
  - *Absence.* No "dynamic", "receptor", "Nida" [V].
  - *Milieu.* In 1968 the Secretariat for Promoting Christian Unity and the United Bible Societies (Nida's institution) issued "Guiding Principles for Interconfessional Cooperation in Translating the Bible" [V, the title as the 1987 text names it]. The revision of 1987, signed by Willebrands, speaks of "functional equivalents of the source-language text" and "the receptor language" [V]. The 1968 text and Nida's part in it [M].
  - *Drafting.* Who drafted CLP was not found. Bugnini's *Reform of the Liturgy* [M].
- Lang adds that LA "is in many ways an elaboration of Paul VI's address of 1965" [V]. MP quotes the same address (section 4). Both sides claim Paul VI.

**For the design.** CLP makes the mode relative to a hearing. The message is the host's sense, found "by the scientific methods of textual study" (CLP 9) [V], so it is an exegetical claim. The hearer is a named community. Fidelity is then whether this hearer receives that message. This is (c), resting on (b): the mode is a function of a claimed message and a declared hearing.

### After it: the approval of sacramental forms (CDF, 1974)

- CDF, "Declaration on the meaning to be given to the approval of the translations of the Sacramental Formulas", approved by Paul VI in audience, 25 January 1974; Šeper and Hamer [V, vatican.va; *L'Osservatore Romano*, English, 7 November 1974, p. 14]. The AAS reference [M].
- The new forms were to be translated "as to express the original meaning according to the characteristic genius of the various languages" [V].
- Translations of essential formulas "must tender faithfully the original sense of the Latin typical text" [V, sic].
- The Apostolic See approves a version "when it deems that it expresses exactly the sense intended by the Church" [V].
- And it decrees "that the sense of the translation must be understood according to the mind of the Church expressed fey the original Latin text" [V, sic].

**For the design.** Two things at once. An approval of a sacramental form is here also a judgement of its sense, so it is a mode claim by the approver. And the declaration lays down how the vernacular is to be heard: by the Latin. That is a hearing rule, not a fact about a hearing. Under it, "for all" as approved was declared to express "exactly the sense" of *pro multis*, and to be heard by the Latin [the inference for the English of 1973 is this record's; that the 1973 form was so approved is M].

## 2. The Council and Scripture

### *Divino afflante Spiritu* (Pius XII, 30 September 1943)

- Exegetes are to "acquire daily a greater facility in biblical as well as in other oriental languages" (§16) [V].
- The original text, "written by the inspired author himself," "has more authority and greater weight than any even the very best translation" (§16) [V].
- The Vulgate's authenticity "is not specified primarily as critical, but rather as juridical" (§21) [V]. It is "free from any error whatsoever in matters of faith and morals" [V].
- Bishops may "make translations into the vulgar tongue, even directly from the original texts themselves" (§22) [V].
- The exegete is to "discern and define clearly that sense of the biblical words which is called literal" (§23) [V]. "the supreme rule of interpretation is to discover and define what the writer intended to express" (§34) [V].

**For the design.** Two tiers of text: the original, with "more authority", and an authentic version whose authenticity is "juridical". That is the pair rule in a sentence: an approval makes a text lawful to use, and does not make it the original. Fidelity is to the author's intended sense.

### *Sacrosanctum Concilium* (4 December 1963)

- "Linguae latinae usus, salvo particulari iure, in Ritibus latinis servetur" (36 §1) [V].
- The vernacular may be extended, "imprimis autem in lectionibus et admonitionibus, in nonnullis orationibus et cantibus" (36 §2) [V].
- The territorial authority decides "de usu et modo linguae vernaculae", "actis ab Apostolica Sede probatis seu confirmatis" (36 §3) [V].
- "Conversio textus latini in linguam vernaculam in Liturgia adhibenda, a competenti auctoritate ecclesiastica territoriali, de qua supra, approbari debet" (36 §4) [V]. The English: "Translations from the Latin text into the mother tongue" [V].
- 54: a "congruus locus" in Masses with the people; "Provideatur tamen ut christifideles etiam lingua latina partes Ordinarii Missae … possint simul dicere vel cantare" [V].
- 63 b: particular rituals, "etiam quoad linguam, accommodata", used "actis ab Apostolica Sede recognitis" [V].

**For the design.** The Council says who approves and from what text ("textus latini"). It says nothing on how to translate. Its words are governance. Taylor reads 36 §4 as leaving translation to the conferences: "Such a requirement is, in fact, not to be found in the relevant document of the Second Vatican Council" [S: Pray Tell 2014, quoting his letter to *The Tablet*]. That is a reading of 36 §§3–4 and belongs to the canon layer.

### *Dei Verbum* 22 (18 November 1965)

- "Christifidelibus aditus ad Sacram Scripturam late pateat oportet" [V].
- The Church "ut aptae ac rectae exarentur in varias linguas versiones, praesertim ex primigenis Sacrorum Librorum textibus" [V]. English: "suitable and correct translations", "especially from the original texts" [V].
- With the separated brethren, if authority approves, "ab omnibus christianis adhiberi poterunt" [V].

The two adjectives divide the field: *aptae* (suited to the hearer) and *rectae* (right to the text). CLP 32 repeats them [V].

### Paul VI to the translators (10 November 1965)

The address is in Latin [V, vatican.va].

- "Etsi sermo vulgaris, cui nunc locus est in Sacra Liturgia, omnium, etiam parvulorum et rudium, captui debet esse accommodatus" [V].
- The same sentence asks that it be "dignus … rebus celsissimis, quae eo significantur", and "diversus a cotidiana loquendi consuetudine, quae in viis et foris viget" [V]. It is to touch the heart: "ut animi sensus tangat et corda Dei amore inflammet" [V].
- He quotes Jerome: "Si ad verbum interpretor, absurdum resonat; si ob necessitatem aliquid in ordine et in sermone mutavero, ab interpretis videbor officio recessisse" (preface to Eusebius's *Chronicle*, PL 27, 35) [V].
- "Nunc autem versiones factae sunt partes ipsorum rituum, factae sunt vox Ecclesiae" [V].
- He hopes that the versions keep "non dissimilis nitor orationis et sententiarum gravitas" [V].

**For the design.** Paul VI holds the two together that later documents divide: the hearer's grasp ("parvulorum et rudium") and a register apart from the street ("in viis et foris"). And he says a version becomes part of the rite. A rendering, once approved, is a text of the rite, not a gloss on one. That is why a mode cannot be read off a version's use: the version is now itself a host for its hearers.

## 3. *Varietates legitimae*, *Liturgiam authenticam*, the *Ratio*, Vox Clara and the Missal of 2011

### *Varietates legitimae* (CDWDS, 25 January 1994)

The text was read in a printed reprint (open.unive.it), whose header has "March 29, 1994" and whose signature block has "Jan. 25, 1994" [V]. vatican.va refused the fetch.

- "The first significant measure of inculturation is the translation of liturgical books into the language of the people" (n. 53) [V].
- "Different literary genres are to be respected, and the content of the texts of the Latin typical edition" "is to be preserved" (n. 53) [V].
- "at the same time the translations must be understandable to participants (cf. above No. 39)," [V]
- "translators must be attentive to the relationship between the text and the liturgical action," [V]
- "aware of the needs of oral communication and sensitive to the literary qualities of the living language of the people" (n. 53) [V].
- "certain words in current Latin use (memoria, sacramentum) took on a new meaning in the Christian faith" [V]. Some Christian words pass between tongues: "ecclesia, evangelium, baptisma, eucharistia" [V].
- "The process of inculturation should maintain the substantial unity of the Roman rite" (n. 36) [V].
- Its notes to n. 53 cite SC 36, 54, 63 and *Vicesimus quintus annus* 20; CLP is not named [V].

**For the design.** VL splits the text into content and expression: content is kept from the Latin (a fact about the pair), expression is fitted to the hearer (a hearing). It is the mediating document, and LA keeps it in force (LA 8) [V].

### *Liturgiam authenticam* (CDWDS, 28 March 2001)

Dates and n. 20's "integrally and in the most exact manner" are in `linguae-sources.md` [V].

- **Its aim and its critique.** Translations are to be "free from all ideological influence" (n. 3) [V]. "The omissions or errors which affect certain existing vernacular translations" have held back inculturation (n. 6) [V]. Translations "in various localities stand in need of improvement through correction or through a new draft" (n. 6) [V]. It prepares "a new era of liturgical renewal" (n. 7) [V].
- **The replacement.** Its norms "are to be substituted for all norms previously published on the matter," "with the exception of the Instruction Varietates legitimae" (n. 8) [V].
- **The unit.** Translation "is not so much a work of creative innovation as it is of rendering the original texts faithfully and accurately" (n. 20) [V]. "without omissions or additions in terms of their content, and without paraphrases or glosses" (n. 20) [V]. "it is always the text of the Latin editio typica itself that is to be translated" (n. 23) [V]. "The translation should not restrict the full sense of the original text within narrower limits" (n. 32) [V]. "the ordering of words, and various forms of parallelism, is to be maintained as completely as possible" (n. 57) [V]. Paraphrases are not to be substituted to ease musical setting (n. 60) [V].
- **Words with doctrine.** Some words "convey, in reality, a properly theological content" (n. 55) [V]. Some expressions are kept by "a translation that is as literal as possible", among them "the words of the people's response Et cum spiritu tuo" and "mea culpa" (n. 56) [V]. Such terms are "not be explained or interpreted by more abstract or general vernacular expressions" (n. 43) [V]. "every effort must be made to preserve this system of vocabulary" (n. 50) [V]. "To be avoided in translations is any psychologizing tendency" (n. 54) [V]. The Creed keeps "the use of the first person singular" (n. 65) [V].
- **The hearer.** The content is to be "evident and comprehensible even to the faithful who lack any special intellectual formation" (n. 25) [V]. So the language is "easily understandable" yet "preserves these texts' dignity, beauty, and doctrinal precision" (n. 25) [V]. "The Sacred Liturgy engages not only man's intellect, but the whole person" (n. 28) [V]. "It is the task of the homily and of catechesis to set forth the meaning of the liturgical texts" (n. 29) [V].
- **The register.** Words are used "which differ somewhat from usual and everyday speech" (n. 27) [V]. "it should cause no surprise that such language differs somewhat from ordinary speech" (n. 47) [V]. This "will facilitate the development of a sacral vernacular" (n. 47) [V]. "Any adaptation to the characteristics or the nature of the various vernacular languages is to be sober and discreet" (n. 20) [V].
- **Scripture.** Biblical words used in catechesis and devotion are "maintained" (n. 40) [V]. "it is advantageous to be guided by the Nova Vulgata wherever there is a need to choose" (n. 41) [V].
- **The process.** The recognitio "is not a mere formality, but is rather an exercise of the power of governance" (n. 80) [V]. Eucharistic Prayers should not be "notably" changed in later revisions "Without real necessity" (n. 64) [V].

**For the design.** LA puts fidelity in the pair: the Latin typical text and the guest, word by word, clause by clause and in order. The hearer is not the measure. What the hearer lacks is supplied by the homily and catechesis (n. 29), and by forming a "sacral vernacular" over time (n. 47). So the hearing is moved, and the text is held. Under LA a mode is close to (a): computed on two texts, given a lexicon. But n. 32's "full sense" presupposes the host's readings, and those are exegesis, so even LA's mode rests on claims.

### Vox Clara and the Missal of 2011

- Vox Clara "was established by the Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments on July 19, 2001" "to advise that Dicastery in its responsibilities related to the translation of liturgical texts in the English language"; chaired by Archbishop George Pell [V, release of 19 November 2002].
- Benedict XVI, letter of 9 November 2005 on the Vox Clara Committee: texts "accurately rendered in accordance with the norms of the Instruction Liturgiam Authenticam" [V]. Its addressee [M; the fetch named Arinze].
- The people's parts [V, Archdiocese of Indianapolis handout, from the USCCB text]:

| --- | --- | --- |
| Et cum spiritu tuo [V, LA 56] | "And also with you." | "And with your spirit." |
| consubstantialem Patri [V, the Latin word per USCCB] | "one in Being with the Father." | "consubstantial with the Father;" |
| "Domine, non sum dignus ut intres sub tectum meum: sed tantum dic verbo, et sanabitur anima mea" [V, a Latin Mass booklet] | "Lord, I am not worthy to receive you, but only say the word and I shall be healed." | "Lord, I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof, but only say the word and my soul shall be healed." |

"And with your spirit" "reflects the important distinction between the roles of priest and people" [V]; the priest "acts by virtue of the Spirit he has received in his ordination" [V].

## 4. *Magnum principium* (2017) and after

### The motu proprio (3 September 2017)

- The end: "finis est salutis verbum fidelibus annuntiare in oboedientia fidei atque precem Ecclesiae ad Dominum exprimere" [V]. Compare CLP 6: "to proclaim the message of salvation to believers" and to express the prayer of the Church [V; the comparison is an observation].
- "fidelitas non semper iudicari potest ex singulis verbis, immo vero in contextu ex toto communicationis actu" [V]. Compare CLP 6: "cannot be judged on the basis of individual words"; "the total context of this specific act of communication" [V; observation].
- The sense is rendered "plene et fideliter", "servata indole cuiusque linguae" [V, in `linguae-sources.md`]. Compare CDF 1974: "according to the characteristic genius of the various languages" [V; observation].
- "translationes, utpote partes ipsorum rituum, una cum Latina lingua Ecclesiae divina mysteria celebrantis vox fierent" [V]. Compare Paul VI, 1965, above [V; observation].
- The English: "attention must be paid to the benefit and good of the faithful" [V]; some "principles handed on since the time of the Council should be more clearly reaffirmed and put into practice" [V].
- MP names neither CLP nor LA [V, by absence in the English].

**Do commentators link MP to CLP?** None fetched for this record does so in terms. Ferrone ties CLP to 1973 and LA to "formal equivalence" [V]. The link above is textual and is this record's.

### The decree of 22 October 2021

CDWDS, signed by Arthur Roche, Prefect, and Vittorio Francesco Viola, Secretary [V, Press Office bulletin 211022b]. The Latin title *Postquam Summus Pontifex* [S: Vatican News].

- "The adverb faithfully implies a threefold fidelity: firstly to the original text," then "to the particular language into which it is translated" and "to the comprehension of the text by the addressees" (n. 20) [V].
- "Faithfulness above all to the original text, i.e. in Latin" (n. 21) [V]. The Latin "always serves as a reference point in case of doubt as to the correct meaning" [V].
- "Faithfulness then to the language into which the translation is made, since each language has its own characteristics" (n. 22) [V].
- "Finally, fidelity to comprehension of the text on the part of the addressees and to their 'spiritual needs'" (n. 23) [V].
- "the fundamental criterion is the participation of the people in the liturgical celebrations" (n. 13) [V].
- The confirmatio checks "the integrity of the texts with respect to the typical liturgical books and their correspondence with them" (n. 45) [V].
- "Sacramental formulas are approved by the Holy Father alone" (n. 25) [V]. They are "submitted to the Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments with a word-for-word translation" and approved after the CDF's "nihil obstat" (note 35) [V].
- It applies "in a particular way to the Instructions Varietates legitimæ and Liturgiam authenticam" [V, introduction]. So LA is still read, within MP.

**For the design.** MP and the decree make fidelity a conjunction of three relations: to the text (the pair), to the target tongue (an encoding), and to the addressees (a hearing). The Latin decides "in case of doubt", which is a hearing rule like 1974's. For the sacramental forms, a word-for-word version is still asked, and the Pope alone approves.

### Later

- The Italian Missal (third edition, 2020): "per tutti" kept; the Our Father has "Do not abandon us to temptation" [S: Crux, CNS]; the Gloria has "people, beloved by the Lord" [S, the same]; obligatory "as of Easter, April 4, 2021" [S]. So CLP 15's *tentatio* returns in 2020. Who approved the formula over the chalice, and when [M].
- From 2022 to October 2026, no new norm on how to translate was found [search only; not exhaustive].

## 5. Critics and defenders

The facts of the texts are not in dispute between them; where fidelity lives is.

### Of CLP and the English of 1973

- **Beall** (Marquette; *Adoremus*, 15 October 1996, from a paper of 1995): CLP is "the official charter of liturgical translators today" and "gives official sanction to the method of 'dynamic equivalence'" [V]. ICEL "has been bitterly criticized by doctrinal conservatives" [V]. "the translators have often chosen to leave the doctrine out" [V].
- **Elliott** (*Antiphon* 2006; *Adoremus* 2007): CLP endorsed the principle; "The old ICEL was faithful to much of that instruction, and even went beyond it" [V]. LA has "freed us from an obsession with communication, instant comprehension, or immediately accessible meaning" [V]. The test: "Do these English words convey the Catholic doctrinal meaning" [V].
- **Lang** (*Adoremus*, September 2010, from an address of January 2010): dynamic equivalence "abstracts the content of the text from its linguistic and cultural form" [V]. In *L'Osservatore Romano* (reprinted 16 May 2012): translation theories "have influenced the translation of the new liturgical books into the vernacular languages" [V]. His *The Voice of the Church at Prayer* (Ignatius, 2012) [M].
- **Esolen** ("Restoring the Words", *First Things*, 1 November 2011) [V]: the Advent collect's *occurrentes* became "increase our strength of will for doing good"; the new has "the resolve to run forth to meet your Christ". He counts thirty cases of *laudatio interrupta* in the 1973 Order of Mass, and finds the old Transfiguration preface "true enough" but "not true enough". The prayers of the Mass "are not gray". His commentary *The Beauty of the Word* (Magnificat, 2012) [V, catalogue].
- **Coleridge** (as reported by *Adoremus*, 15 March 2010): the 1973 text "consistently bleaches out metaphor"; the new is to be "less overtly catechetical"; "Let the texts stand as [they are] and let catechesis draw out from the texts" [S].
- **Moroney** (USCCB, 2011): above [V]. **Weigel** (*First Things*, 28 November 2012): the old English was "stripping the Latin of its distinctive sacral vocabulary and images" [V].

What they locate: fidelity in the text, word, image and allusion (Esolen); doctrine as heard (Elliott, Beall); and the hearer formed by catechesis rather than served by the text (Coleridge, as LA 29).

### Of LA and the English of 2011

- **Trautman** (*America*, 22 October 2001): "Liturgical translation is an art rather than a purely mechanical operation" [V]; "Jerome translated not word for word, but sense for sense" [V]. "The people of God have the right to hear the word of God proclaimed with fidelity to the intent of the inspired author" [V].
- **Jeffery**, *Translating Tradition: A Chant Historian Reads Liturgiam Authenticam* (Liturgical Press, 2005; four articles of 2004 in *Worship*): LA is "the most ignorant statement on liturgy ever issued by a modern Vatican congregation" and should be "summarily withdrawn" (pp. 97–101) [S: Pray Tell 2010]. "Inaccuracies, misrepresentations and contradictions so abound in LA" (p. 22) [S: a reader quoting the book]. His historical arguments in detail were not reached.
- **Baldovin** (*America*, 25 September 2006): CLP was the document "in which dynamic equivalence held sway" [V]. "we shall not move beyond translations like these unless we revisit the Vatican instruction" [V]. He calls Jeffery's book a "devastating critique" [V].
- **Taylor** (chairman of ICEL 1997–2002 [S: Inwood 2023]): the recognitio as a precondition is "not to be found in the relevant document of the Second Vatican Council" [S, above]. His booklet *It's the Eucharist, Thank God* (Decani) [S: Inwood 2023; Osborne 2011]; its year [M].
- **Chupungco**, *Liturgical Inculturation* (Liturgical Press, 1992), pp. 38–39: dynamic equivalence "re-expresses (and hence, reinterprets) a particular doctrinal, liturgical, or theological concept" [S: Asis 2015]. A defender of the method concedes that it interprets.
- **Pecklers**, *Dynamic Equivalence: The Living Language of Christian Worship* (Liturgical Press, 2003) [S: Schellman, *America*, 2004].
- **Ryan** (Seattle): in *America*, December 2009, "What if we just said 'wait'?" [S: Ryan 2012]; more than 23,000 signed online [S, the same]. In 2012: "On almost every page, there are passages so turgid as to be distasteful and, in many cases, downright baffling" [V]. Of the survey on whatifwejustsaidwait.org: "admittedly unscientific", 70 percent negative [V].
- **Cones** (*U.S. Catholic*, 8 August 2011): "consubstantial" was chosen as the English that sounds most like "consubstantialem", and "one in being" is nearer the Greek [V, the fetch's paraphrase]. "The greeting is just that: a greeting" [V].
- **Cameron-Mowat** (*Thinking Faith*, 7 October 2011): the new "Lord, I am not worthy" gives "modified words of the centurion", which "no longer fit the ritual" [V].
- **Ferrone** (*Commonweal*, 30 June 2011): LA required that "every Latin word must be accounted for" [V].

What they locate: fidelity in the sense (Trautman, Chupungco), in the hearing and praying of a congregation (Ryan), in the ritual act (Cameron-Mowat), in the Greek host behind the Latin (Cones), and in the process (Taylor).

**The view that prompted this record**, that CLP is more interesting than LA's "stilted latinese", is one side's. The record gives both. Each side's strongest point is the other's blind spot. CLP's critics show that "the message" can lose what the words carry (Esolen's *occurrentes*). LA's critics show that words kept can fail the hearer (Ryan, Cones).

## Theory

- **Jerome, *Ep.* 57.5.** "non verbum e verbo, sed sensum exprimere de sensu", save in Scripture, "ubi et verborum ordo mysterium est" [V, `linguae-sources.md`; §5 V, NPNF via New Advent]. The apostles and evangelists "sought to give the meaning rather than the words" (§9) [V, NPNF]. Paul VI quotes his other text, the dilemma of the *Chronicle*'s preface (section 2) [V]. Fidelity: the sense, and in Scripture the order of the words.
- **Thomas, *Contra errores Graecorum*, prooemium.** "multa quae bene sonant in lingua Graeca, in Latina fortassis bene non sonant" [V, Corpus Thomisticum]. Three hypostases is said "recte et Catholice" in Greek; "three substances" "non recte sonat" in Latin, "licet hypostasis idem sit apud Graecos quod substantia apud Latinos secundum proprietatem vocabuli" [V]. So it belongs "ad officium boni translatoris" that he "servet sententiam, mutet autem modum loquendi secundum proprietatem linguae in quam transfert" [V]. Fidelity: the *sententia*, with the manner of speech fitted to the target tongue. The case is decisive for this record. Two words are equal by the lexicon and unequal in the hearing.
- **Thomas, III q. 60 a. 7 ad 1.** "Et hic quidem sensus est idem apud omnes, licet non eaedem voces quantum ad sonum" [V, `linguae-sources.md`]. Fidelity: the sense of the form, the same in every tongue.
- **Thomas, III q. 78 a. 3 ad 8**, on the chalice: "Et ideo signanter dicit, pro vobis Iudaeis, et pro multis, scilicet gentilibus" [V, aquinas.cc]. Or "pro vobis manducantibus, et pro multis pro quibus offertur" [V]. Two readings of the host, neither of them "all" against "not all".
- **Schleiermacher**, *Ueber die verschiedenen Methoden des Uebersezens* (1813; *Abhandlungen* of the Berlin Academy, p. 152). Either the translator "läßt den Schriftsteller möglichst in Ruhe, und bewegt den Leser ihm entgegen", or "er läßt den Leser möglichst in Ruhe und bewegt den Schriftsteller ihm entgegen" [V, as printed by docenti.unimc.it, whose "Übertsezer" is sic]. The day and occasion, 24 June 1813 [M]. Fidelity: to the writer (the reader moves) or to the reader (the writer moves). Schleiermacher preferred the first [S: Munday].
- **Venuti**: domestication is "a transparent, fluent, 'invisible' style in order to minimize the foreignness of the TT"; foreignization is "non-fluent, estranging or heterogeneous" [S: Munday 2016, pp. 225–226].
- **Nida**: section 1 above. Fidelity: the receptor's response.

Mapped to the documents: CLP is Nida's and Schleiermacher's second way (the writer moves). LA is Schleiermacher's first (the reader moves: the homily, catechesis, a sacral vernacular formed over time). Jerome and Thomas put fidelity in the sense, with two exceptions that both sides claim. Jerome's Scripture exception is LA's ground for the anaphora and the biblical words. Thomas's *modus loquendi* is CLP's ground for idiom.

## Proposed typing of a mode

All of this section is proposal.

### The three options, against the documents

- **(a) A fact about a pair of texts.** This is LA's theory, and it is computable: which host words have a counterpart, whether order and parallelism are kept, which host readings the guest's lexicon admits. But it is a fact only relative to two claims: the guest's lexicon at its date (`lexicon/4`, which has a claimant), and the host's readings (exegesis: Jeremias, Thomas's two readings, Arinze's (a)). Thomas's hypostasis case defeats it alone. *Hypostasis* and *substantia* are one by the lexicon and diverge in the hearing.
- **(b) A claim with its claimant, at the source's tier.** Arinze ("a faithful translation"), Benedict ("Interpretation"), the CDF's approval of 1974 ("exactly the sense"), and every confirmatio under the decree of 2021 ("correspondence", n. 45) state modes. They must weigh, and none may fix (rule 14's shape). But a claim alone is weighed against nothing. If the mode were only a claim, the checker could not say that Arinze and the 1974 approval disagree, or that they disagree only in their unit.
- **(c) Relative to a hearing.** This is CLP's communication, Thomas's *modus loquendi*, and Francis's third fidelity. The same pair is faithful for one hearer and not for another. Its danger is rule 3. A hearing must not become what a person understood. It is safe only as a horizon with a declared lexicon (`hears_in(G, E)`, with E's lexicons as claims), never as a person's comprehension.

### The proposal: a combination, run under regimes

The mode is computed, never entered. It is computed on the pair (a), relative to declared lexicons and readings, which are claims. It is indexed by hearing (c). Authorities' statements of a mode are claims (b), compared with what is computed. The three documents of law (LA, CLP, MP) are then three regimes, run as switches. Each says which of the computed relations counts as "faithful". The language step computes all of them and prefers none. Which regime governs a book's approval is the canon layer's (c. 838 before and after 1 October 2017).

**New slots** (beside those of `linguae-sources.md`):

- `reading(F1, R, C)`: a reading of the host form, with its claimant. Readings are claims; none is computed.
- `admits(F, R, E)`: under a lexicon of encoding E (`lexicon/4`), F admits R. Rival lexicons are computed both ways.
- `hears_in(G, E)` as before, where G is a horizon, never a person.
- `message(F1, R, C)`: a reading named as the message (CLP 6, 9). A claim, used only by the CLP regime.
- `mode_claim(C, F2, F1, M, U)`: a mode stated by C, with its unit U, one of `word`, `passage`, `act`, `sense`, `recipients`.
- `hearing_rule(C, F2, F1)`: C lays down that F2 is to be heard by F1 (CDF 1974; the decree of 2021, n. 21). `catechesis_rule(C, F2, R)`: C sends the reading R to catechesis (LA 29; CLP 31; Arinze 2006).
- `hears_under(G, C)`: the horizon G hears under C's rule. Declared, never inferred.

**Derived** (pair-level, then per hearing):

- `closes(F2, F1, R)`: `reading(F1, R, _)`, and F2 does not admit R under its encoding.
- `opens(F2, F1, R)`: F2 admits R, which is not a reading of F1.
- `interpretation(F2, F1, R)`: F2 keeps R and closes another reading of F1, as in `linguae-sources.md`.
- `closes_for(G, F2, F1, R)`, `opens_for(G, F2, F1, R)`, `interpretation_for(G, F2, F1, R)`: the same through G's lexicon. Under `hears_under(G, C)` with `hearing_rule(C, F2, F1)`, G admits for F2 exactly F1's readings. The rule is reported, not hidden: `heard_by_rule(G, F2, C)`.
- `contrary_for(G, F2, R, Q)`: a reading R admitted for G that answers a doctrinal question Q against its sources. It joins the authority graph only as a premise (`premise/3`, rule 1).

**Regimes** (switch programs):

- `regime_la`: `faithful(F2, F1)` if no `closes/3` (LA 32), every host word accounted, order kept where the guest allows (LA 57). Each `opens_for/4` and `contrary_for/4` is reported as `for_catechesis(F2, R)` (LA 29), not as a fault of the text.
- `regime_clp`: `faithful_for(G, F2, F1)` if G admits a `message/3` reading and no `contrary_for/4` (CLP 16). It fails on an insertion in a biblical or anaphoral text (CLP 31, 33), and whether a choice is an insertion is itself a disputed reading.
- `regime_mp`: `faithful_for(G, F2, F1)` if three hold. The text: no `closes/3`, the Latin deciding "in case of doubt" (decree, n. 21). The tongue: `idiomatic(F2, E, C)`, a claim. The addressees: as `regime_clp`. Sacramental formulas also need a word-for-word version and the Pope's approval (decree, n. 25, note 35). That is governance, so a canon slot.

**Reports and violations:**

- `info(mode_claim_differs(C, F2, F1))`: a claimed mode differs from the mode computed in the claim's unit.
- `info(units_differ(C1, C2, F2, F1))`: two claims on one pair, in different units.
- `violation(mode_fixed_by_claim(C, F2, F1))`: a mode claim treated as fixing, as `self_elevation` treats a tier.
- `violation(hearing_without_lexicon(G))`; `violation(hearing_of_a_person(G))` (rule 3).
- The pair stays: `law_answers(q(carries(T, F)))` is refused. An approval fixes lawful use. Under the 1974 declaration and the decree's n. 45, it is also a mode claim at the approver's tier, and weighs as one.

### "For all" and *pro multis*, under each theory

Host readings, each a claim. `r_open`: many, how many unsaid [M, the Latin and Greek]. `r_all`: the Semitic "many" as all, after Isaiah 53 [Arinze (a), V; Jeremias M; Vanhoye on *rabim*, S]. `r_gentiles` and `r_offered`: Thomas's two [V]. Arinze adds a constraint, not a reading: salvation "is not brought about in some mechanistic way" [V].

| Rendering | Pair level (`closes/3`) | LA regime | CLP regime | MP regime | Claims |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| en 1973 "for all" | closes `r_open`, `r_offered`; `interpretation(…, r_all)` | not faithful (LA 32) | faithful for an English hearing if the message is `r_all` (ICEL's exegesis); not, if "for all" is an explanation (CLP 31) | text fails in doubt (n. 21); tongue and addressees pass; branches | approval of the 1970s as "exactly the sense" (1974 shape; inference M); Arinze: "explanation"; Benedict: "Auslegung", "sehr wohl begründet" |
| en 2011 "for many" | keeps all four | faithful | if English "many" is heard as "not all" (a lexicon claim, M), then `opens_for(G, …, r_not_all)` and `contrary_for` "Christ died for all" [Arinze, V]; not faithful for that G | text passes; addressees branch on the lexicon | Arinze: "a faithful translation" |
| it 2020 "per tutti" | as en 1973 | not faithful | as en 1973 | confirmed under MP [S]; the confirmatio is a mode claim in the unit of the whole | `units_differ` with Benedict 2012 |
| fr "pour la multitude" | keeps `r_open` and the collective of `r_all` [S: Vanhoye] | faithful, if the lexicon claim holds | faithful | passes | none found |

**The finding.** Both English renderings carry a risk in the hearing, and the two regimes remove it from opposite sides. "For many" may be heard as "not all", against a dogma Arinze states. "For all" may be heard as efficacy without willing, against Arinze's (e). (Both hearings are this record's reading of the letter's two cautions.) LA keeps the text and sends the hearing to catechesis: Arinze's "in the next one or two years" [V]. CLP keeps the hearing ("here and now") and fits the text to it. So `interpretation/3` is a fact relative to declared readings. Whether that interpretation is a fault is regime-relative. Whether the hearer meets it at all is hearing-relative.

### Where the two theories diverge: "Et cum spiritu tuo"

- Host readings, each a claim. `r_person`: "your spirit" as you, the whole person ("It is also possible to translate spirit here as denoting the whole person") [V, Arichea and Hatton, UBS *Handbook on 2 Timothy*, 1995]. `r_inner`: "a person's inner being or self" [V, the same]. `r_charism`: "they do not refer to his soul, but to the grace of the Holy Spirit" [S: Theodore of Mopsuestia, quoted by Davoren 2011], as Moroney's "by virtue of the Spirit he has received in his ordination" [V]. That "your spirit" is a Semitism for "you" [M].
- **Pair level.** "And also with you" admits `r_person` alone: `interpretation(en_1973, la_ecst, r_person)`. "And with your spirit" keeps all three.
- **LA regime.** 2011 is faithful, by name (LA 56); 1973 restricts "the full sense" (LA 32). The English hearer's "spirit" (a mood; the UBS handbook warns against a word that could suggest a ghost [V]) goes to catechesis (LA 29).
- **CLP regime.** The message of a returned greeting is `r_person` (Cones: "just that: a greeting" [V]). The 1973 text is then faithful for an English hearing of its date, and 2011 opens readings the host lacks: `opens_for(G_en, en_2011, la_ecst, r_mood)`. If the message is declared `r_charism` (Moroney, Theodore), CLP too reports `interpretation_for` against 1973.
- **The result.** The pair is the same, and the reports are opposite. Only the claimed message and the declared hearing differ. That is the strongest evidence here against typing a mode as a bare fact.

### Two shorter cases

- **"consubstantial".** The host is itself a rendering: `made_from(la_creed, gr_creed)` [M for the Greek *homoousion*; the Latin V, USCCB]. LA 23 makes the Latin the host; Cones takes the Greek. CLP 19 allows a term "created by use", so CLP can admit "consubstantial" too. Thomas's *hypostasis* and *substantia* show why the host's choice matters. Proposal: a rendering of a rendering declares which parent is its host, and both are run.
- **"under my roof".** The Missal's Latin renders Mt 8:8 for the communicant ("anima mea" for "puer meus") [V]. The 1973 text keeps the act and loses the allusion: `lost_in(…, intertext)`. The 2011 text keeps the allusion. CLP 26 ("the nature of the ritual act") and Cameron-Mowat ("no longer fit the ritual") put fidelity in the act. LA 40 puts it in the biblical words.

### What challenges this typing

1. **Readings are the hidden premise.** Every mode, even LA's, rests on the host's readings, and those are exegesis with claimants. The checker types them; it does not choose them.
2. **A hearing can become an interior.** CLP's "actual community" invites computing what people understood. Rule 3 forbids it. Only a horizon's public lexicon may be entered, and a survey (Ryan's 70 percent) is a claim about a lexicon, not a hearing.
3. **Approvals speak twice.** By 1974 and by the decree's n. 45, an approval is governance and also a judgement of correspondence. The pair rule must let the second weigh as a claim while refusing to let the first answer it.
4. **The unit is part of the claim.** Benedict's "Interpretation" and an approval's "exactly the sense" can both be true, in different units. Without `U`, they look contradictory.
5. **A version becomes a host.** Paul VI and MP make an approved version "part of the rite". The 2011 English is now a host to its hearers, and its own readings ("spirit") need typing.
6. **Both sides claim the same fathers.** Jerome, Thomas and Paul VI are cited by both. The record should enter each citation as a reading of its source, not as the source's verdict.
