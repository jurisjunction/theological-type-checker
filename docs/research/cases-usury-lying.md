## Corrections to the brief, kept because they shape the encoding

1. **c. 1543 of the 1917 Code answers both of SD2's questions, not one.** Its first clause restates *Vix pervenit*: "nihil lucri, ratione ipsius contractus, percipi potest" [V, AAS 9/II (1917), OCR on press.vatican.va]. Its permission is of "lucrum legale", "nisi constet ipsum esse immoderatum", and of a greater gain "si iustus ac proportionatus titulus suffragetur" [V]. SD2 enters c. 1543 on the second question only (`icon_history.l`, `usury_two_questions`) [V, read].
2. **The legal rate is itself a title in c. 1543.** The canon permits the legal rate "per se", without naming any further title. A higher rate needs "iustus ac proportionatus titulus" [V]. So "interest by an extrinsic title", SD2's second question, fits the canon only if the law's own title counts as extrinsic.
3. **The replies found are of 1830–1871, not 1822–1838.** The *Catholic Encyclopedia* lists replies of the Holy Office of 18 August 1830, 31 August 1831, 17 January 1838, 26 March 1840 and 28 February 1871, and one of the Penitentiary of 11 February 1832 [S: Vermeersch, "Usury"]. A reply of 1822 and the phrase "non esse inquietandos" were not found in any text fetched [M]. The English "not to be disturbed", of 1830 under Pius VIII, is [S: NPNF² 14, "Excursus on Usury"].
4. **Smith's word is "increase", not "aggravate".** The prohibition of interest "has been found from experience to increase the evil of usury" (*WN* II.4) [V].
5. **Lateran V does say "define".** Of the *montes pietatis*: "we declare and define, with the approval of the sacred council" [V, session 10, 4 May 1515, English]. That bears on its tier (rule 14, below).
6. **Locke concedes that money is barren.** "But Money is a barren thing, and produces nothing, but by Compact transfers that Profit" [V]. He argues for interest from distribution and compact, not against sterility.
7. **"l_cc1 and l_cc2" are in no file.** `linguae-sources.md` says that "the lying case of this study already types 1992 and 1997 as two sources (`cases-usury-lying.md`, l_cc1 and l_cc2)". No rule file, test or document of the repository has those names [V, searched]. Lying, below.

## Usury: the texts

### Scripture

- **Ex 22:25** (Hebrew 22:24 [M]): "si pecuniam mutuam dederis populo meo pauperi qui habitat tecum, non urgebis eum quasi exactor, nec usuris opprimes" [V, as Thomas quotes it, II-II q. 78 a. 1 s.c.]. The poor man and "my people".
- **Lev 25:35–37**, for the brother who has become poor: "ne accipias usuras ab eo, nec amplius quam dedisti"; LXX "τόκον" (record: [V], `modernity-mammon.md`). The ban stands inside the jubilee chapter (record: [V]).
- **Deut 23:19–20**: "Non fœnerabis fratri tuo ad usuram pecuniam, nec fruges, nec quamlibet aliam rem: sed alieno. Fratri autem tuo absque usura id quo indiget, commodabis" [V, Clementine Vulgate, catholicbible.online]. Douay-Rheims: "But to the stranger" [V]. The motive is "ut benedicat tibi Dominus Deus tuus" [V].
- **Lk 6:35**: "δανίζετε μηδὲν ἀπελπίζοντες" [V, Greek of the Berean Interlinear, studybible.info]. Thomas reads it "date mutuum, nihil inde sperantes" [V, q. 78 a. 1 obj. 4]. The Greek participle can be read "despairing of nothing" as well as "hoping for nothing back" [M]. That is a tongue note for TTC-LINGUAE: the Latin's "nihil inde sperantes" carries the reading that bears on interest.
- **Ps 15 (14):5** and **Ezek 18:8, 13, 17**, the other loci the tradition cites [M].
- **The word.** The Vulgate's verb is *foenerari*, from *foenus*. The 1983 Code bids administrators pay "foenus ... statuto tempore" (c. 1284 §2 5°) [V, vatican.va Latin]. Deut 23:19's word now names a debt that the Church's own administrators owe.

### Aristotle

- **Politics I.10 (1258a–b)**, in Ellis's translation (1912): "usury is most reasonably detested, as it is increasing our fortune by money itself"; "and not employing it for the purpose it was originally intended, namely exchange"; "the name (TOKOS), which means the breeding of money"; "usury is money bred of money"; "of all forms of money-making it is most against nature" [V, ellopos.net]. The trade of exchange is "justly censurable", the household's acquisition "necessary and commendable" [V]. The Greek was not reached [M: ὀβολοστατική, τόκος, παρὰ φύσιν].
- **NE V.5 (1133a)**: money's name, *nomisma*, is from *nomos*, since its value is "not from nature, but from law (νόμος)", and can be changed; money "makes all things commensurable" [V, Toronto Metropolitan University Pressbooks, translator not named]. So for Aristotle money is not a natural thing: its end and its measure are by convention.
- **Pol. I.9 (1257b)**, acquisition without limit, the measure taken as its own end, was not fetched [M].

### Thomas, II-II q. 78

From aquinas.cc (Latin with the Dominican Fathers' English) and ccel.org [V]:

- **Sed contra**: Ex 22:25, as above [V].
- **The object.** "accipere usuram pro pecunia mutuata est secundum se iniustum, quia venditur id quod non est" [V]. Some things' use "est ipsarum rerum consumptio, sicut vinum consumimus" [V]. To sell the use apart from the thing is to sell what does not exist [V], or the same thing twice [M].
- **Money's end.** Money "principaliter est inventa ad commutationes faciendas"; its proper use is its consumption or alienation; the Philosopher is cited (*Ethic.* V; *Polit.* I) [V]. The conclusion: "secundum se est illicitum pro usu pecuniae mutuatae accipere pretium, quod dicitur usura" [V].
- **Ad 2 (Deut 23:20).** Usury from foreigners was "permissum ad maius malum vitandum" [V].
- **Ad 3 (civil law).** "usuras lex humana concessit, non quasi existimans eas esse secundum iustitiam" [V]. Human law's tolerance is not justice. That is rule 1 in Thomas.
- **Ad 4 (Lk 6:35).** "dare mutuum non semper tenetur homo": to lend is of counsel, but "it is a matter of precept not to seek profit by lending" [V].
- **A. 2 ad 1.** The lender may covenant for "compensation for the loss he incurs", but not for the profit he might have made, since "he must not sell that which he has not yet" [V]. So Thomas admits what was later called *damnum emergens* and refuses *lucrum cessans* by pact.
- **A. 2 ad 5.** Money entrusted to a merchant or craftsman as a partnership: the lender "does not transfer the ownership of his money to them, for it remains his", and so "may lawfully demand ... part of the profits" [V]. The difference is the contract's object, ownership and risk, not the sum.

### The councils

- **Lateran II (1139), c. 13**, in Schroeder's translation (1937): "We condemn that detestable, disgraceful, and insatiable rapacity of usurers"; it "has been outlawed by divine and human laws in the Old and New Testaments"; "we deprive them of all ecclesiastical consolation"; "unless they repent let them be deprived of Christian burial" [V, Medieval Sourcebook, Fordham].
- **Lateran III (1179), c. 25**: notorious usurers are not to be admitted "to communion of the altar", nor to "christian burial if they die in this sin" [V, clerus.org, translator not named]. Whoever buries them is to give back what he received and be suspended [V]. The clause refusing their offerings was not in the English fetched [M].
- **Vienne (1311–12), *Ex gravi*** (Clem. 5.5 un. [S: Vermeersch]; DH 906 [M]), decree 29 in Tanner's numbering [V, EWTN's copy]. Officials who make statutes allowing usury, or knowingly order it paid, incur excommunication. They have "three months" to delete such statutes [V]. Of one "presuming to affirm pertinaciously that the practice of usury is not sinful": "we decree that he is to be punished as a heretic" [V]. Ordinaries and inquisitors proceed "as they would against those suspected of heresy" [V]. The Latin, "velut haereticum puniendum", is [M].
- **Lateran V, session 10 (4 May 1515), on the *montes pietatis*** (*Inter multiplices* [M for the incipit]) [V, intratext English]:
  - The definition: "the real meaning of usury: when, from its use, a thing which produces nothing"; "gain and profit without any work, any expense or any risk" [V]. The Latin, "ex usu rei, quae non germinat, nullo labore, nullo sumptu nulloque periculo", is [M].
  - The *montes* may take "a moderate sum for their expenses and by way of compensation", "provided that no profit is made therefrom" [V].
  - Such a charge "should not be considered as usurious"; "we declare and define, with the approval of the sacred council" [V]. Those who preach or write otherwise "incur the punishment of immediate excommunication" [V].
  - The council records the dispute it closes: "Some of these masters and doctors say that the credit organisations are unlawful" [V].
  - So Lateran V puts the extrinsic titles (work, expense, risk) inside the definition of usury, as what usury lacks. Usury is gain from the loan as such.

### Benedict XIV, *Vix pervenit* (1745)

From newadvent.org's English and vatican.va's Italian [V]. The Latin was not reached [M].

- To the "Patriarchs, Archbishops, Bishops and Ordinary Clergy of Italy" [V]; "Dato a Roma, presso Santa Maria Maggiore, il 1° novembre 1745" [V, Italian].
- **The object.** usury "has its proper place and origin in a loan contract"; the sin is that "the creditor desires more than he has given"; the law of loans "consists necessarily in the equality of what is given and returned" [V]. Italian: "eguaglianza fra il prestato e il restituito" [V].
- **No excuse.** Not that the gain is "moderate or small", nor that "the borrower is rich", nor that the money "is spent usefully" [V].
- **The titles.** "titles—which are not at all intrinsic to the contract—may run parallel with it", and from them "entirely just and legitimate reasons arise to demand something over and above" [V]. Italian: titles "non del tutto connaturati ed intrinseci, in generale, alla stessa natura del prestito" [V].
- **Other contracts.** By "contracts differing entirely from loans" money may be invested, and "honest gain may be made" [V]. No one is to presume that titles or other contracts are always present ("some will falsely and rashly persuade themselves") [V].
- **Its warrants.** "most emphatically condemned in the Sacred Scriptures"; Mt 5:42, "Do not refuse to lend to him who asks you"; "the authority of Church councils and the Fathers" [V]. It names neither Lateran V nor *De synodo dioecesana* [V, by absence].
- **Its reach.** Vermeersch: "addressed only to the bishops of Italy, and therefore not an infallible Decree"; the Holy Office in 1836 "declared that this Encyclical applied to the whole Church", but that "could not give to a document an infallible character which it did not otherwise possess" [V, *Catholic Encyclopedia*, "Usury"]. The date of that declaration, 29 July 1836, is [S: Wikipedia, citing the *Catholic Encyclopedia* and Turner 1999].

### The Holy Office, 1830–1871

- The replies, by date, are as in correction 3 [S: Vermeersch]. From them, "The Holy See admits practically the lawfulness of interest on loans, even for ecclesiastical property", "though it has not promulgated any doctrinal decree on the subject" [V, Vermeersch's words].
- In 1830 the Holy Office, with Pius VIII's approval, decided that those who held interest permitted by the State justifiable were "not to be disturbed" [S: NPNF² 14, "Excursus on Usury", whose author the page does not name]. Wikipedia reports that the reply of 1830 distinguished the doctrine from the practice, and it flags its own source (White 1897) [S].
- Not reached: the Latin of any reply; the condition usually quoted with them, "dummodo parati sint stare mandatis S. Sedis"; the Bishop of Rennes as the one who asked in 1830 [M, every item].
- Vermeersch, "Interest": the change in what is permitted "is due to economical circumstances"; "the mere possession of money means a certain value"; "one can commonly employ one's money fruitfully"; "These special reasons, which authorise the charging of interest, are called extrinsic titles" [V, *Catholic Encyclopedia*, newadvent.org]. On his reading, the change lies in a fact about money, not in the principle.

### The 1917 Code and the 1983 Code

- **CIC 1917 c. 1543**, Titulus XXIX "De contractibus": "Si res fungibilis ita alicui detur ut eius fiat et postea tantundem in eodem genere restituatur, nihil lucri, ratione ipsius contractus, percipi potest; sed in praestatione rei fungibilis non est per se illicitum de lucro legali pacisci, nisi constet ipsum esse immoderatum, aut etiam de lucro maiore, si iustus ac proportionatus titulus suffragetur" [V, AAS 9/II].
- **Abrogated.** The base's other canons of 1917 are marked abrogated with that Code by CIC 1983 c. 6 §1 n. 1 [V, `canon_law.l`]. SD2's `cic1917_c1543` has no withdrawal [V, `icon_history.l`].
- **CIC 1983 c. 1284 §2 5°.** Administrators are "foenus vel mutui vel hypothecae causa solvendum, statuto tempore solvere"; 6° bids them employ money "quae de expensis supersit et utiliter collocari possit" [V, vatican.va]. Caridi reads the 1983 Code as otherwise silent on usury [V, canonlawmadeeasy.com, 4 Sept. 2014].

### The extrinsic titles

- **Their substance**, in Vermeersch: interest may compensate "for the risk of losing his capital, or for positive loss, such as the privation of the profit which he might otherwise have made"; and a "conventional penalty" may be exacted "in case of any delay in payment arising from the fault of the borrower" [V, "Usury"]. The article does not use the Latin names [V, by absence].
- **The Latin names** *damnum emergens*, *lucrum cessans*, *periculum sortis*, *poena conventionalis* are the manuals' [M]. Of the texts fetched, only Buchmann names *damnum emergens* and *lucrum cessans* [V, *Church Life Journal*, 14 July 2021].
- **Who admitted what.** Thomas admits loss and refuses lost profit by pact (q. 78 a. 2 ad 1) [V]. Hostiensis "is the first to introduce the justification of lucrum cessans" (*Lectura*, early 1260s), which "Duns Scotus accepted" [S: HET website, "Hostiensis"]. The *Summa aurea* (1253) lists twelve exceptions [S: same]. Gregory IX's *Naviganti* (X 5.19.19), which held the sea loan usurious, is [M].
- **What they are, for the checker.** Each title is a circumstance of the lender: a loss suffered, a gain forgone, a risk run, a delay. Noonan: the change moved "from focus on the loan in itself to focus on the lender" [V, *TS* 54 (1993)]. Whether a title is present is a fact about a lender and a loan, and in the canon layer a disputed fact (rule 2). It is not a cause any horizon holds.

Buchmann reads its point as "to explain the prohibition's descent into irrelevance" [V, his words]. Its chapters on the titles, the triple contract and the *montes* are [M].

## The case as TTC-MODERNITY would type it

### One question or two

Two, and the sources draw the line themselves, so SD2 holds in kind:

- *Vix pervenit*: titles "not at all intrinsic to the contract" may "run parallel with it" [V].
- Lateran V: usury is gain "without any work, any expense or any risk" [V]. Where there is work, expense or risk, there is a title, and the gain is not from the loan as such.
- c. 1543: "nihil lucri, ratione ipsius contractus" against "lucrum legale" and "titulus" [V].
- Noonan: the focus moved from the loan to the lender [V].
- Vermeersch: the change "is due to economical circumstances" [V].

The first question, profit from the loan as such, is doctrinal, and no source of tier 1 to 3 in this record supports its contrary. The second is a permission of law that turns on facts about lenders. Entered as one question, the 1917 Code answers doctrine and is refused (SD2) [V, `suite.py`].

The modern figures do not answer the first question by the titles. They answer it "no" by describing the loan's object otherwise. Smith: the borrower wants "the money's worth", stock that yields profit [V]. Bentham: the daric buys ewes [V]. Calvin: the farm bought with money bears [V]. Thomas: the use of money is its consumption [V]. The dispute is about the object inside a cause both sides can hold. TTC-MODERNITY's CAVEATS say the VM cannot show such a dispute, and that where it arises "the question is split" [V, read]. SD2's two questions are that split.

### What SD2 needs (proposals; nothing changed)

1. **c. 1543's first clause as a premise.** Enter `premise` from the canon to `q(profit_from_loan_as_such)` yes, so the law is seen to presuppose the principle it does not answer (rule 1). Today c. 1543 stands only on the second question.
2. **Name the law's title.** Either read "an extrinsic title" so that it includes the civil law's title, and say so, or split the second question into the legal rate (which is what the replies concern) and a greater rate by a proportionate title.
3. **Withdraw c. 1543 in 1983**, as the base does for the other canons of 1917. Then enter c. 1284 §2 5° as what the 1983 law presupposes: the Church's administrators pay "foenus". Without it, the second question has no source in force after 1983.
4. **The replies as directives.** A candidate that cites "not to be disturbed" as a principle fixing the first question "no" should be refused (`directive_as_principle`, as SD4).
5. **A third question, for the moderns only:** whether the law should cap interest. Smith wants a legal rate a little above the lowest market rate [V]. Bentham wants none, and defines usury by the law or by custom [V]. In his usage "usury" no longer names gain from the loan as such. That is a word redefined, which a trace cannot see (TTC-MODERNITY CAVEATS) [V, read]. Without the third question the case cannot tell Smith from Bentham: both support "no" on the first.

### How the figures separate, by cause or part

- **The end of money.** Thomas and Aristotle: exchange [V]. NE V.5 puts money's being in *nomos* [V], so (final, nature) is wrong for it. The ten parts give only the makers' end (will) or human nature's (human); `art` gives its own. Calvin, Smith and Bentham never deny that money is for exchange. They deny that the loan's object is the coin.
- **The loan's object.** Thomas's consumptible and *Vix pervenit*'s equality, (formal, will), against Smith's "money's worth" and Calvin's farm [V]. The split runs through `object_as_form`: lost at Ockham, the Lockeans, the will readings, Hobbes's four and Kant's three. The moderns' redescription of the object is a dispute inside the cause, which the second question absorbs.
- **The extrinsic titles.** Facts about the lender, not parts. They separate the two questions, not the horizons.
- **Money's sterility.** Aristotle's *tokos*, Lateran V's "produces nothing" [V]; refused by Calvin ("What does the sea beget?") and by Bentham ("organs for generating") [V]; conceded by Locke [V]. As (formal, nature) it is idle; it belongs to `art`.
- **The measure as end.** Aristotle: usury increases "our fortune by money itself" [V]; Marx: "M-C-M is reduced to the two extremes without a mean" [V]. This is the structure `modernity-mammon.md` types as T1 `measure_terminal` (record: [V]), a relation in a rule, contract or argument, never an intention (rule 3). In the causes vocabulary, the argument would rest on (final, human), the bound that the household's good sets on acquisition (Pol. I.9 [M]).

## What the Graeber and McCarraher mapping gives the case, and what it still owes

- **Exchange that settles (E).** The mapping's sketch, `settles(A,X,Y) :- exchange(A,X,Y,G1,G2), equal(G1,G2)` (record: [V], `modernity-mammon.md`), is *Vix pervenit*'s "equality of what is given and returned" [V] and Thomas's "equality of thing and thing" (record: [V]). Usury types as a mutuum that does not settle: more returned than given, with no title. Hobbes takes `equal/2` away and puts the contractors' content in its place: "the just value is that which they be contented to give" [V]. So E carries the case's central dispute. It is equality by the thing against equality by the appetite: `object_as_form` against `ends_desired`. **Owed:** E is not encoded (roadmap) [V, read]. Without it the Grammar refuses the just mutuum itself (record: [V], "The Grammar refuses exchange").
- **Release (R).** The usury ban of Lev 25:35–37 stands in the jubilee chapter (record: [V]). Lk 6:35 is, for Thomas, a counsel to lend and a precept not to seek profit [V]. R would type the gratuitous loan and the release against usury's growth. **Owed:** `releases/3`, and the refusal of a release typed as a payment.
- **A price on a person.** The pledge and debt-bondage loci (Ex 22:26; Lev 25:39; Neh 5) [M]. The *montes* took pledges of goods for the poor [M]. T5 `person_priced` would type a person pledged for a debt. **Owed:** A10 widened from a ranking to a price.
- **The measure as end.** T1 types usury in Aristotle's and Marx's description [V]. Thomas's q. 77 a. 4, gain lawful when ordered to a necessary end (record: [V]), places the titles outside T1, since compensation restores equality and makes no gain. T1 is typable now, as a structure. It has not been entered.
- **The creature as last end** is done (TTC-LOCI, `loci_mammon`) [V, `loci-preregistration.md`, SB2 held]. The case does not need it: usury is a sin of the exchange, not of the last end.
- **McCarraher gives the case nothing direct.** No fetched text shows his treatment of usury, apart from a reviewer's summary (record: [V], `modernity-mammon.md`). **Graeber** lists "Medieval laws against usury" beside clean slates and jubilees (record: [V in `graeber-debt.md`], as cited there). - **Still owed of the mapping overall** (roadmap) [V, read]: exchange as a closing; release; a price on a person; an obligation kept apart from the bond; a total gift to a thing. The `art` domain is owed with them.
