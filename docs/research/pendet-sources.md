# "Hang" as end and as summary, the lifted term, and the primary analogate: the evidence record for TTC-PENDET

This is the verification record for TTC-PENDET: a "lifted" relation (a term outside an order that every node of it is referred to, and that is no node of it); a case on Mt 22:40, "hang", read as end and as summary; and a "measure" under which, in an analogy of attribution, the primary analogate stays first. It was compiled on 25 October 2026. It is an evidence record, so it keeps prose (rule 10). Nothing here is encoded; the bearings on the encoding are proposals.

**How sure each item is:**
- **[V]** The wording was checked against a text fetched in this pass.
- **[S]** It was checked through a named secondary source that quotes or reports the primary.
- **[M]** It comes from memory or a standard citation and was not re-checked. It is unverified until someone checks it in print.

[V] means the fetch tool returned the words verbatim from the named page. It corrected the requested wording several times (noted), which is some evidence of fidelity. Direct downloads (curl) were refused by the egress policy for every host tried (drbo.org, vatican.va, sourceforge, archive.org, newadvent.org, gutenberg.org), so every [V] here comes through the fetch tool, and only from pages a search returned. ## Matthew 22:40

- **Vulgate (Clementine).** "In his duobus mandatis universa lex pendet, et prophetæ." [V, latinprayer.org/en/biblia/matthaeus/22/, which names its text as "the Sixto-Clementine Vulgate"]. The same page: v. 39 "Secundum autem simile est huic : Diliges proximum tuum, sicut teipsum" [V]. The requested wording is right, with a comma before "et prophetae". The Nova Vulgata reading was not fetched [M: the same words].
- **Greek.** "ἐν ταύταις ταῖς δυσὶν ἐντολαῖς ὅλος ὁ νόμος κρέμαται καὶ οἱ προφῆται" [V, biblehub.com/text/matthew/22-40.htm, Nestle 1904; the page gives Westcott–Hort 1881 and Tischendorf 8 as the same]. κρέμαται: Strong's 2910, "V-PIM/P-3S", present indicative middle or passive, third singular; lexicon "To hang, hang up, suspend; mid: To be hanging, hang" [V].
- **The variant.** The Byzantine Majority Text (2005), the Textus Receptus (1550, 1894) and the Greek Orthodox text put the plural κρέμανται after προφῆται [V, same page, as reported]. So the critical text and the Vulgate have a singular verb whose subject is the law, with the prophets added; the KJV's plural "hang" follows the Received Text.
- **KJV.** "On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets." [V, biblestudytools.com/kjv/matthew/22-40.html; also biblehub, same page as the Greek].
- **Thomas's Latin of the verse varies.** "in his duobus mandatis pendet omnis lex et prophetae" (I-II q. 99 a. 1 ad 2); "in his duobus mandatis tota lex pendet et prophetae" (II-II q. 44 a. 3 s.c.) [V, liriocatolico.com.br, below]. The Clementine has "universa". A rule that matches the verse by its Latin words should not key on one of the three.

The verse headings: "39. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself." "40. On these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets." [V]

- **Augustine, on "hang".** Attribution "Aug., Quaest. Ev., i, 33": "'Hang,' that is, refer thither as their end." [V]
- **Raban, the two tables.** Attribution "Raban.": "For to these two commandments belongs the whole decalogue;" the first table to the love of God, and "those of the second to the love of our neighbour." [V]
- **Pseudo-Chrysostom, the statue.** Attribution "Pseudo-Chrys.": "But who loves man is as who loves God;" for man is God's image, "wherein God is loved, as a King is honoured in his statue." [V; the dialogue quotes the whole sentence, 26 words, and it matches]. The same Father: "For this cause this commandment is said to be like the first." [V]. That the text is the *Opus imperfectum in Matthaeum* is [M].
- **Hilary, alike.** Attribution "Hilary": "Or otherwise; That the second command is like the first signifies that the obligation and merit of both are alike;" [V]. It goes on to give the reason, that there is "no love of God without Christ, or of Christ without God" [V]. So Hilary's two alike terms are God and Christ, not God and the neighbour.
- **Origen, the reward.** Attribution "Origen": it opens "Or, because" and speaks of him who has fulfilled what is written of the love of God and neighbour, who "is worthy to receive from God the great reward, that he should be enabled to understand the Law and the Prophets." [V]. The "Or" makes it another reading of verse 40, beside Augustine's.
- **The summary reading inside the chain.** Attribution "Aug., de Trin., viii, 7": "not without reason does Scripture put one for both;" then, for the neighbour, "All the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself." [V; Gal 5:14]. The ground: "it is the selfsame affection by which we love God, and by which we love our neighbour," [V]. Romans 13:10 appears in the chain only as "see Rom 13:10" in an attribution line (de Doctr. Christ., i, 30), not in a text [V].
- **The order.** As returned, the block for vv. 37–40 runs: Pseudo-Chrys.; Aug., de Doctr. Christ., i, 22; Gloss., interlin.; Origen; two unattributed; Aug., de Doctr. Christ., i, 30; Aug., de Trin., viii, 6; Aug., de Doctr. Christ., i, 22; Pseudo-Chrys. (the statue); Hilary; Aug., Quaest. Ev., i, 33; Raban.; Origen ("Or, because"); Aug., de Trin., viii, 7; Aug., de Doctr. Christ., i, 26, 30; one unattributed [V, the order as the fetch tool listed it; where v. 40's comment ends was not shown]. So on verse 40 itself the chain is Augustine, Raban, Origen; Pseudo-Chrysostom and Hilary stand under verse 39.

- **Greek: ἀνακεφαλαιοῦται.** "καὶ εἴ τις ἑτέρα ἐντολή, ἐν τῷ λόγῳ τούτῳ ἀνακεφαλαιοῦται, [ἐν τῷ]" "Ἀγαπήσεις τὸν πλησίον σου ὡς σεαυτόν." The Greek πλήρωμα οὖν νόμου ἡ ἀγάπη is [M].
- **English.** KJV: "and if there be any other commandment," "it is briefly comprehended in this saying, namely, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself." [V, kjvstudy.org/book/Romans/chapter/13/verse/9]. Douay-Rheims: "it is comprised in this word" [V, biblehub catholic].

## Thomas

Latin from liriocatolico.com.br and aquinas.cc; English from New Advent, CCEL or drbo.org (Dominican Fathers, not named on the pages) [V, unless marked].

### The law is one by its end (I-II q. 99 a. 1)

- **The question.** "Whether the Old Law contains only one precept" [V, New Advent search title, newadvent.org/summa/2099.htm].
- **The corpus.** "omnia praecepta legis veteris sunt unum secundum ordinem ad unum finem, sunt tamen multa" [V, liriocatolico]. English: "all the precepts of the Old Law are one in respect of their relation to one end" [V]. This is where "secundum ordinem ad unum" is: in the unity of the precepts, not in the treatise on names (below).
- **Ad 2.** "Ad secundum dicendum quod, sicut apostolus dicit, I ad Tim. I, finis praecepti caritas est" [V]. Then "Et ideo tota lex impletur in hoc uno mandato, diliges proximum tuum sicut teipsum" [V]; "in dilectione enim proximi includitur etiam Dei dilectio, quando proximus diligitur propter Deum" [V]; and "in his duobus mandatis pendet omnis lex et prophetae" [V]. English: "Wherefore the whole Law is comprised in this one commandment" [V].
- **The shape.** The objection is Rom 13:9 (the summary, "instauratur"); the reply grounds it in 1 Tim 1:5 (the end) and then cites Mt 22:40. In this article Thomas does not set summary against end: the law is summed in one commandment because charity is its end.

### Charity, the end of the precept (II-II q. 44)

- **A. 1, the question.** "Whether any precept should be given about charity?" [V, New Advent, newadvent.org/summa/3044.htm].
- **A. 1, corpus.** "finis praecepti est caritas de corde puro et conscientia bona et fide non ficta." [V, liriocatolico.com.br/suma_teologica/_nova/view/ST.II-II.Q44/]. "Per se quidem debitum est in unoquoque negotio id quod est finis" [V]; English: "it is the end that is due for its own sake" [V, New Advent]. "Et ideo maximum praeceptum est de caritate" [V].
- **A. 1 ad 3.** "omnia praecepta Decalogi ordinantur ad dilectionem Dei et proximi" [V]; "praecepta caritatis non fuerunt connumeranda inter praecepta Decalogi, sed in omnibus includuntur" [V]. Raban's two tables, in Thomas's terms: ordered to, and included in, not counted among.
- **A. 1 does not cite Mt 22:40** [V, New Advent, by absence]. *Correction:* the verse is in a. 3's sed contra: "in his duobus mandatis tota lex pendet et prophetae" [V, liriocatolico]; English "On these two commandments dependeth the whole Law and the prophets" [V, New Advent].
- **A. 3, corpus.** "convenienter de caritate duo praecepta sufficiunt" [V]; the ground, as paraphrased: the good is either an end or for the end, so one precept for God as end, one for the neighbour for God's sake [V, paraphrase as returned].
- **"The end is not subject to measure."** Not in II-II q. 44 a. 1 [V, by absence on New Advent]. That Thomas says it of the love of God in II-II q. 27 a. 6 is [M].

### The mode of charity (I-II q. 100 a. 10)

- **The question.** "Whether the mode of charity falls under the precept of the Divine law?" [V, ccel.org/ccel/aquinas/summa.FS_Q100_A10.html].
- **It bears, a little.** The act of charity "as an act by itself" falls under the precept; "the second opinion is true in saying that the mode of charity does not fall under the precept" [V]. Charity is "the end of the commandment," citing 1 Tim 1:5 [V]. Neither ad 1 nor ad 2 cites Mt 22:40 [V, as reported]. It gives the case a distinction: charity as one precept among others, and charity as the mode of all the others.

### Analogy (I q. 13 a. 5 and a. 6)

- **A. 5, two modes.** "vel quia multa habent proportionem ad unum, sicut sanum dicitur de medicina et urina," and "vel ex eo quod unum habet proportionem ad alterum" [V, aquinas.cc/la/en/~ST.I.Q13.A5]. The second continues: "sicut sanum dicitur de medicina et animali, inquantum medicina est causa sanitatis quae est in animali." [V].
- **A. 5, which mode for God.** "Et hoc modo aliqua dicuntur de Deo et creaturis analogice, et non aequivoce pure, neque univoce." [V]. "Hoc modo" follows the second mode, one to another. God and creatures are not named as two things referred to a third.
- **A. 5, the order.** "secundum quod est aliquis ordo creaturae ad Deum, ut ad principium et causam" [V]. *Correction:* not "secundum aliquem ordinem". "Et iste modus communitatis medius est inter puram aequivocationem et simplicem univocationem." [V]. And "est una ratio, sicut est in univocis; nec totaliter diversa, sicut in aequivocis" [V, the clause as returned; its negation precedes it]. English: "is said according to the relation of a creature to God as its principle and cause" [V, newadvent.org/summa/1013.htm].
- **A. 5 ad 1.** "sicut in praedicationibus omnia univoca reducuntur ad unum primum, non univocum, sed analogicum, quod est ens." [V, aquinas.cc/la/en/~ST.I.Q13.A5.Rep1].
- **A. 6, one in the definition of all.** "in omnibus nominibus quae de pluribus analogice dicuntur, necesse est quod omnia dicantur per respectum ad unum" [V, aquinas.cc, ~ST.I.Q13.A5.Rep1, which runs on into a. 6]; that one "oportet quod ponatur in definitione omnium" [V]. English: "all are predicated because they have reference to some one thing" [V, drbo.org/summa/question/01306.htm].
- **A. 6, two orders.** "quantum ad rem significatam per nomen, per prius dicuntur de Deo quam de creaturis" [V]. *Correction:* the sentence opens "Unde, secundum hoc, dicendum est quod", not "Sic ergo". "quantum ad impositionem nominis, per prius a nobis imponuntur creaturis, quas prius cognoscimus" [V]. English: "as regards what the name signifies, these names are applied primarily to God rather than to creatures" [V, drbo]; "as regards the imposition of the names, they are primarily applied by us to creatures which we know first" [V, drbo].
- **A. 6, metaphors.** Metaphorical names "per prius de creaturis dicuntur quam de Deo" [V]. English: "all names applied metaphorically to God, are applied to creatures primarily rather than to God" [V, drbo].
- **A. 6, not causally only.** "Sed supra ostensum est quod huiusmodi nomina non solum dicuntur de Deo causaliter, sed etiam essentialiter." [V]. "God is good" signifies "non solum ... quod ipse sit causa sapientiae vel bonitatis" [V, the phrase as returned]. English: "these names are applied to God not as the cause only, but also essentially" [V].
- **"secundum ordinem ad unum".** The phrase is in neither a. 5 nor a. 6 [V, by absence on aquinas.cc]. A. 6 has "per respectum ad unum"; a. 5 has "ad unum primum" and "aliquis ordo creaturae ad Deum".
- **SCG I c. 34 agrees.** The first mode is not used of God and creatures: "oporteret enim aliquid Deo ponere prius" [V, liriocatolico.com.br/suma_contra_gentios/view/SCG1.C34/]; the second is "secundum quod duorum attenditur ordo vel respectus" [V]. "res nominum de Deo et rebus aliis dictorum per prius est in Deo secundum suum modum" "sed ratio nominis per posterius" [V]; "ordo nominis sequitur ordinem cognitionis" [V].

### Measure, outside the genus and inside it

- **I q. 3 a. 5 ad 2.** God is "not a measure proportionate to anything," yet is "the measure of all things," because "everything has being only according as it resembles Him" [V, English, ccel.org/ccel/aquinas/summa.FP_Q3_A5.html]. The objection: "nothing can be measured save by something of its own genus" [V]. The Latin is [M].
- **I-II q. 90 a. 1.** Law is "a rule and measure of acts"; "that which is the principle in any genus, is the rule and measure of that genus," for example "unity in the genus of numbers, and the first movement in the genus of movements" [V, English, ccel.org/ccel/aquinas/summa/FS/FS090.html]. The Latin is [M].
- So Thomas has two measures: the first of a genus, which is a member of what it measures, and God, who measures what he is not of. The step's lifted term is the second.

### The school's term

- **"Analogy of attribution" is Cajetan's term, not Thomas's** (I q. 13 a. 5–6 use neither "attributio" nor "attributionis" in the passages returned; [V, by absence, partial]). Cajetan, *De nominum analogia*, c. 2 §8: "Analoga autem secundum attributionem sunt, quorum nomen commune est" [V, documentacatholicaomnia.eu PDF, edition not named]; §10: "ita quod primum analogatorum tantum est tale formaliter, cætera autem denominantur talia extrinsece." [V]; §14: "primum analogatum ponitur in definitione cæterorum" [V]. §21: "Abusiva tamen locutio haec est" [V]. C. 3 heading: "QUOD SOLA PROPRIE ANALOGIA VOCETUR" [V]; §29: "Scimus quidem secundum hanc analogiam rerum intrinsecas entitates, bonitates, veritates etc." [V].
- So in Cajetan's attribution only the first analogate has the form; in Thomas's a. 6 the names are said of God "essentialiter" and are imposed first on creatures, which have them too. To enter God and creatures as "attribution" with the primary analogate alone formally such is Cajetan's reading, and one that a. 6 resists. It is a claim with a claimant.

## Bearings on the encoding (proposals)

- **"Hang" has more than two readings in the chain, and they are not rivals in Thomas.** On verse 40 the Catena gives the end (Augustine), belonging by tables (Raban), and the reward of understanding (Origen, "Or"). Summary enters only through Augustine's *de Trinitate* viii, 7 (Gal 5:14, one for both), and it rests on "the selfsame affection". In I-II q. 99 a. 1, summary (Rom 13:9) is the objection, and the reply grounds it in the end (1 Tim 1:5). So enter summary as following from end, or as compatible with it. Do not enter end and summary as exclusive alleles.
- **Summary is of the second table into one word** (Rom 13:9), not of the law into two. If the case types summary as a reading of Mt 22:40, mark it as an extension, with its claimant.
- **Rom 13:9's Latin word depends on the edition.** Clementine and Thomas: "instauratur"; Nova Vulgata: "recapitulatur"; Greek ἀνακεφαλαιοῦται. Cite the edition with the word.
- **The lifted term for God is the second mode of a. 5** (one to another), never a third term above God and creatures (SCG I.34: nothing prior to God). A lifted relation that puts God and creatures as nodes under one term is the excluded mode, and should fail.
- **"The primary analogate stays first" holds in one order only.** First of God "quantum ad rem significatam"; first of creatures "quantum ad impositionem nominis", and for metaphors. The measure must name its order, or it will contradict a. 6.
- **"Attribution" is Cajetan's**, with extrinsic secondaries. A. 6 says "essentialiter". Enter "God–creature names by attribution" as a school's reading with its claimant, not as Thomas's.
- **Two measures.** The first in a genus is a node of what it measures (I-II q. 90 a. 1); God measures and is "not a measure proportionate to anything" (I q. 3 a. 5 ad 2). The lifted relation is the second; keep it apart from an order's first node.
- **"secundum ordinem ad unum" belongs to I-II q. 99 a. 1** (the precepts, "ad unum finem"), not to I q. 13. Cite it there.
