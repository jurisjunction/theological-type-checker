# The four causes and rights, from Thomas to Locke: the evidence record for TTC-MODERNITY

This is the research record behind the predictions, compiled on 23 October 2026. It is an evidence record, so it keeps prose (rule 10).

**How sure each item is:**
- **[V]** The wording was checked against an online primary text or translation.
- **[S]** It was checked through a named secondary source that quotes the primary.
- **[M]** It comes from memory or a standard citation and was not re-checked. It is unverified until someone checks it in print.

Quotations are under 25 words.

## Corrections to the brief, kept because they shape the encoding

1. **"Metaphorical motion" is not Ockham's mark.** It is the common scholastic account of how an end causes. Suárez says "everyone" holds it and attributes it to Thomas (DM 23.4 nn. 3, 5) [V]. Scotus: "Philosophus cum loquitur de fine, dicit quod movet metaphorice" [S: Majcherek 2017, n. 29]. The schools divide on two other questions: (i) whether an end must be *cognized* to cause, and (ii) whether natural agents have *proper* final causality.
2. **Ockham's texts are in two places.** The nature of final causation is Quodlibet IV qq. 1–2 (OTh IX 293–309) [S: Majcherek]. That natural agents' ends cannot be demonstrated is Quodlibet II q. 2 (OTh IX 115–116) [S: Pasnau 2001]. Culleton 2011 cites the same pages as "Quodl. IV q. 2", which looks like a mislabel.
3. **ST II-II q. 66 a. 2 ad 1** reads "secundum ius naturale non est distinctio possessionum, sed magis secundum humanum condictum, quod pertinet ad ius positivum" [V].
4. **Bacon, *Novum Organum*.** II.2 has "Eam autem legem … formarum nomine intelligimus"; "eadem res est forma calidi … et lex calidi" is at II.17 [V].
5. **Locke.** Real and nominal essence are defined at *Essay* III.iii.15; III.vi applies them to substances [S: SEP].
6. **Suárez, *De legibus* I.2.** Editions number the paragraph on "facultas moralis" as n. 4 or n. 5 [S].
7. **Scotus on property.** Wolter's Ord. IV d. 15 q. 2 is cited by others as d. 15 q. 4 (Vivès XVIII 258–263) [S].
8. **Descartes.** His only explicit citation of Suárez is in the Fourth Replies (AT VII 235) [S].

## The figures

### Thomas Aquinas (the baseline)

**Final causes.**
- "Every agent, of necessity, acts for an end" (ST I-II q. 1 a. 2) [V, Dominican Fathers].
- Non-rational things tend to an end "as being moved by another"; "as an arrow tends to a determinate end through being moved by the archer" [V].
- Natural necessity is "a kind of impression from God, directing them to their end" (I q. 103 a. 1 ad 3) [V].
- SCG III.2–3 [V]. *In II Phys.* lect. 12–14 [V, titles only]. "natura nihil est aliud quam ratio cuiusdam artis…" (lect. 14 n. 268) [M].

**Formal causes.** Substantial forms in every natural thing; the soul is the form of the body (I q. 76 a. 1) [M].

**Domain.** Every domain. Rational agents direct themselves to their end; non-rational agents are directed by another (I-II q. 1 a. 2 ad 3) [V].

**Is the end moved into the subject? No.** Self-preservation is only the first and lowest inclination, shared with every substance: "prout scilicet quaelibet substantia appetit conservationem sui esse" (I-II q. 94 a. 2) [V].

**Property.**
- Man has natural dominion over external things as to their use, not their nature (II-II q. 66 a. 1) [V].
- "non debet homo habere res exteriores ut proprias, sed ut communes" (q. 66 a. 2) [V].
- Division "iuri naturali superadditur per adinventionem rationis humanae" (ad 1) [V].
- A field considered absolutely "contains no reason why it should belong to one man more than to another". Considered as to cultivation and unmolested use, it suits one owner; this is the ius gentium (q. 57 a. 3) [V].

### John Duns Scotus

**Final causes.**
- He shows little interest in arguing for natural teleology [S: Majcherek 2017, p. 46].
- The end causes as intended, "secundum esse in anima" [S].
- "Omne naturale agens, praecise consideratum, ex necessitate et aeque ageret, si ad nullum finem ageret" [S: n. 38]. The primary locus is *De primo principio* 4, Theorem 4.4, first proof [V, Ward 2024, §55]. A natural agent "acts by necessity" and "would act just as it does even if it were … acting for the sake of no end at all". So where it acts for an end, it depends on an agent that loves the end. Natural ends are not denied but made extrinsic, in the First's knowing and willing; Thomas makes them intrinsic, an inclination "impressed" by God (I q. 103 a. 1 ad 3). Located in review, 23 October 2026. Edition: John Duns Scotus, *Treatise on the First Principle*, tr. Thomas M. Ward (Hackett, 2024), on Kluxen's Latin (4th ed., WBG 2009).
- He divides nature, which is determined of itself, from will, which is not (Quaest. in Metaph. IX q. 15) [M locus].

**Univocity.** Ord. I d. 3 p. 1 qq. 1–2 n. 26 (Vatican III, 18) [S].

**Property.**
- Common possession belonged to innocence by natural law; after the Fall "revocato isto praecepto legis naturae de habendo omnia communia" (Vivès XVIII 259) [S: Culleton 2008].
- Division came by positive law, "ex mutuo consensu omnium propter pacificam conservationem" (263) [S].

### William of Ockham

**Final causes.**
- "causalitas finis non est aliud nisi esse amatum et desideratum ab agente efficaciter" (Quodl. IV q. 1, OTh IX 293) [S].
- It cannot be demonstrated that natural agents' ends are known or directed (Quodl. II q. 2, OTh IX 115–116) [S: Pasnau].
- Inanimate things have ends only improperly (*Summula* II c. 6) [S].
- He narrows proper final causality first to living, then to rational beings [S: Majcherek n. 19].

**Caution.** He defended final causes against Chatton, who reduced them to efficient causes [S: Majcherek 2019], and his strength on the point varies by work [S: SEP; M. M. Adams 1998].

**Formal causes.** Substantial forms kept, plural in humans [M]. Universals are not things: "nullum universale sit aliqua substantia extra animam exsistens" (*Summa logicae* I c. 15) [V].

**Rights.**
- *Opus nonaginta dierum* c. 2: ius utendi as "potestas licita utendi re extrinseca" [M; the phrase is attested in Krzynówek-Arndt 2024, S].
- After the Fall, "the power of appropriating temporal things" (*Breviloquium* III.7) [V, Gallagher translation].

### Francisco Suárez

**Final causes.**
- The end causes by metaphorical motion: "solum movet metaphorice attrahendo" (DM 23.4 n. 9) [V].
- He reports Ockham: "causationem finis esse movere efficiens ad agendum" (n. 8) [V].
- Natural agents "non tam dicuntur operari propter finem, quam dirigi in finem a superiori agente" (DM 23.10 n. 5) [V].
- "non sit propria causalitas finalis" (n. 6) [V].
- "intentio autem propria talis finis non est in ipsis naturalibus agentibus, sed in prima causa" (n. 7) [V].
- "illa directio et ordinatio in finem iam est ab ipso primo agente" (DM 24.2 n. 14) [V].

**Formal causes.** "omnes res naturales seu corporeas constare forma substantiali" (DM 15.1 n. 5) [V].

**Rights.**
- Ius is "facultas quaedam moralis, quam unusquisque habet vel circa rem suam vel ad rem sibi debitam" (*De legibus* I.2.4/5) [S: Merlo; the head of the clause is M].
- *De legibus* II.14 asks whether natural law can be changed or dispensed [V, title]. Private property comes by agreement (II.14.13–16) [S: Tully]. The negative/permissive reading of the community of goods is [M].
- Suárez amends Scotus: the precept became inactive of itself rather than being revoked (*De opere* V.7 n. 17) [V].

### Francis Bacon

**Final causes.**
- "Causa finalis tantum abest ut prosit, ut etiam scientias corrumpat, nisi in hominis actionibus" (*NO* II.2) [V].
- Final causes are "more allied to man's own nature" (*NO* I.48) [V, Devey].
- "tanquam virgo Deo consecrata nihil parit" (*De augmentis* III.5) [S; Spedding's wording M].
- Physics takes the material and efficient causes, metaphysics the formal and final (*De augmentis* III.4) [M Latin; the 1605 English is V].

**Formal causes.** Forms become laws (*NO* II.2, II.17) [V]. The four-cause frame is kept as a frame [V].

**Is the end moved into the subject? Yes:** "Knowledge and human power are synonymous" (I.3) [V, Montague].

### René Descartes

**Final causes.**
- The kind of cause sought from ends is useless "in rebus Physicis"; "non … absque temeritate me puto posse investigare fines Dei" (Med. IV, AT VII 55) [V].
- "not the final, but the efficient, causes of created things" (*Principles* I.28) [V].

**Formal causes.**
- Substantial forms are "a philosophical being unknown to me" (to Morin, 1638) [S].
- The arguments for them fit "the form of a clock"; the soul is "vera forma substantialis hominis" (to Regius, Jan. 1642, AT III 505) [S].

**Is the end moved into the subject? Partly:** "maîtres et possesseurs de la nature" (*Discours* VI) [V].

**Suárez as his teacher at La Flèche?** Weakly supported. The only explicit citation is AT VII 235. He names the Coimbrans, Toletus and Rubius (AT III 185) [M] and praises Eustachius (AT III 232) [S]. Ariew argues against reading a Suárezian background in; Courtine and Marion argue for one [M].

### Thomas Hobbes

**Final and formal causes.** The formal cause and the end are both efficient causes; the end "has no place but in such things as have sense and will" (*De corpore* X.7) [M wording, S content].

**Ends.** "there is no such finis ultimus … nor summum bonum"; "a perpetual and restless desire of power after power" (*Leviathan* 11) [V].

**Forms.** He mocks "substantial forms" (*Leviathan* 46) [V] and treats Suárez's chapter on concourse as insignificant speech (*Leviathan* 8) [V].

**Rights.**
- "The right of nature … is the liberty each man hath to use his own power … for the preservation of his own nature" (*Leviathan* 14) [V].
- "no propriety, no dominion, no mine and thine distinct" (*Leviathan* 13) [V].
- Property comes from the sovereign (ch. 24) [M].

### Baruch Spinoza

**Final causes.**
- "omnes causas finales nihil nisi humana esse figmenta" (*Ethics* I App.) [V].
- God "as he does not exist for the sake of an end, so neither does he act for the sake of an end" (IV Praef.) [V, Elwes].
- A final cause is "nothing else but human desire" (IV Praef.; IV Def. 7) [V].

**Formal causes.** "Each thing, as far as it can by its own power, strives to persevere in its being" (III p6); conatus is the essence (III p7) [V/S].

**Self-preservation.** "the first and only foundation of virtue" (IV p22 cor.) [V].

**Right.** Natural right is kept "unimpaired" (Letter 50) [V].

**Dispute.** Garrett and Lin against Carriero on whether all teleology is denied [S: SEP].

### John Locke

**Final causes.** Not denied in nature; the design argument (*Essay* IV.x) [M]. Plural happiness (II.xxi.55) [M].

**Formal causes.** Real essence is "the very being of any thing, whereby it is, what it is" (III.iii.15) [S]. "Forms or Molds" have "very much perplexed the Knowledge of natural Things" (III.iii.17) [S].

**Property** (*Second Treatise*) [V]:
- "the judicious Hooker" (§5).
- "they are his property, whose workmanship they are" (§6); "Every one, as he is bound to preserve himself" (§6).
- "given … to mankind in common" (§25).
- "a property in his own person"; "he hath mixed his labour with" (§27).
- "If such a consent as that was necessary, man had starved" (§28).

**The parents' duty** (*Second Treatise*) [V, added in revision]:
- Parents are "by the law of Nature, under an obligation to preserve, nourish and educate the children they had begotten" (§56).
- They do so "not as their own workmanship, but the workmanship of their own Maker, the Almighty" (§56).
- "God hath made it their business to employ this care on their offspring" (§63); He "hath laid on them an obligation to nourish, preserve, and bring up their offspring" (§66).

### Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (the counter-figure)

- Substantial forms are "so necessary in metaphysics" (*Discourse* §10) [V].
- The scholastics are "not to be wholly despised" (§11) [V].
- "The utility of final causes in Physics" (§19) [V].
- Final and efficient explanation are reconciled (§22) [V].
- Right is "potentia moralis" (*Codex juris gentium*, 1693) [M].

## The documented clashes

A clash is where a figure inherits incompatible positions and resolves them his own way.

- **Suárez.** The Thomist end in nature against the requirement that an end be cognized. He puts proper final causality in cognizers, and nature's ends in God's intention.
- **Descartes.** School hylomorphism against mechanism. He keeps one form, the soul; ends stay in ethics and are barred from physics.
- **Hobbes.** Mechanism against the scholastic ethics of ends. The end becomes desire as efficient motion; there is no finis ultimus; right is cut loose from law.
- **Spinoza.** Descartes's conatus against Hobbes's self-preservation. Conatus becomes the essence of each thing, and natural right is kept "unimpaired".
- **Locke.** Hooker's natural-law ends and Grotius's consent against Hobbes's natural right. Self-preservation becomes a duty to the Maker, and labour appropriates without consent.
- **Grotius.** Thomist common use against Roman dominium. Property arises by agreement, express or tacit.
- **Ockham.** Franciscan use without right against the papal claim that use entails dominion. He separates usus facti from ius utendi.
- **Bacon.** The Aristotelian frame against operative science. He keeps the names and changes their content.
- **Leibniz.** Mechanism against forms and ends. He reconciles them as two kingdoms of explanation.

**Added in revision, after the readings below were designed.** These two are not independent of the predictions that use them (TTC-MODERNITY DG5), and are scored apart from the nine above.

- **Suárez, on natural law.** Gregory of Rimini's natural law, which "even if God did not exist" would still be law, against Ockham's, "found entirely in a divine command or prohibition". He holds it is both: indicative of good and evil, and a true divine law with its own prohibition (*De legibus* II.6 nn. 3–5, 13) [V, Freddoso translation].
- **Leibniz, on goodness.** The view of "certain philosophers" that the eternal truths, "and consequently the principles of goodness", are effects of God's will, against his own: they "follow from his understanding, which does not depend upon his will" (*Discourse* §2) [V, Montgomery]. That Descartes is the target is [M]: Leibniz does not name him there.

## Three candidate compositions

1. **A Suárezian parent, tracing God's intention out of physics.** Suárez has already put nature's ends in God's intention alone; Descartes calls that intention inscrutable, and Bacon calls it sterile.
2. **An Ockhamist parent, tracing natural finality and keeping ends in desire.** Hobbes and Spinoza then reduce desire to efficient causation. Gillespie, Gregory and Milbank favour this root; Cross, Williams and Horan reject its premise.
3. **A Thomist parent, with the lowest inclination promoted.** Self-preservation becomes the only end (Spaemann's inversion). Locke re-anchors it in God's workmanship.

## Readings of figures (added in revision, 23 October 2026)

The review expected more loss before Hobbes than the record's nodes give. Each reading below is entered as a sibling of the record's node, and both are run. A reading is a hypothesis about a figure, so its warrant and the literature against it are kept together.

**Scotus, on the will.**
- The second table of the Decalogue belongs to natural law only in a looser sense: it is highly consonant with the first principles, not deduced from them (Ord. III d. 37 q. un.) [S: a teaching extract, *Philosophical Investigations*, peped.org; Wolter, *Duns Scotus on the Will and Morality*, M]. Property falls under the second table.
- The will is not necessitated toward the last end [M: Ord. I d. 1 p. 2 q. 2].
- Entered as: the ends of human nature no longer give a reason by themselves; God's will does.

**Ockham, on the will.**
- God can command acts that are now evil, and prohibit acts now good, the love of God included (OTh V 352–353) [S: Freddoso].
- "Given the ordination that is now in force", no act is perfectly virtuous unless done in conformity with right reason (OTh VIII 394) [S: Freddoso].
- Against the reading: Freddoso finds the ethics "more Aristotelian than is commonly recognized" [V]; Gatto, *Mediaevalia* 30 (2011), argues the *odium Dei* is bounded by eternal truths [V, abstract]; Adams reads natural law as contingent on God's ordination [S: Freddoso].
- Entered as for Scotus.

**Ockham, on common natures.**
- "nullum universale sit aliqua substantia extra animam exsistens" (*Summa logicae* I c. 15) [V, above].
- That the intellective soul is the form of the body cannot be known evidently by reason or experience; it is held by faith (Quodl. I q. 10) [M].
- Entered as: the soul as the form of the body is not a reason natural reason can use.

**Descartes, on the will.**
- "it is because he willed to create the world in time that it is better this way" (Sixth Replies) [S: Cottingham, through Arroyo; AT VII 432 is M].
- The eternal truths are freely created (to Mersenne, 15 April 1630, AT I 145) [M].
- Entered as for Scotus: goodness is God's will, and the ends of human nature are not a reason of their own.

**Grotius, *etiamsi daremus*.**
- Natural law would have a place "etiamsi daremus, quod sine summo scelere dari nequit, non esse Deum" (DIBP Prolegomena §11) [M].
- Before him, Gregory of Rimini held that the dictate of right reason would be law "even if God did not exist" (*De legibus* II.6 n. 3, reporting him; Biel, Almain and Corduba follow) [V, Freddoso translation].
- Entered as: the ends of human nature bind without God's ends for human action. A reading against Grotius, who keeps God. It occupies the one phase of the RN case that no figure of the record held.

**An argument read two ways.** Leo XIII calls the father's duty "a most sacred law of nature" (RN 13) [V]. Locke grounds the same duty in the Maker's command (§§56, 63, 66, above). Whether a Lockean hearer can follow RN 13 is a question of how the argument is read, not of which horizon he has.

**Henry Adams, the measure.**
- He would use "the century 1150-1250, expressed in Amiens Cathedral and the Works of Thomas Aquinas, as the unit from which he might measure motion", "without assuming anything as true or untrue, except relation" (*Education* XXIX) [V, Gutenberg].
- The two books are the two points: *Mont-Saint-Michel and Chartres*, "a Study of Thirteenth-Century Unity"; the *Education*, "a Study of Twentieth-Century Multiplicity" [V].
- The Virgin "had drawn man's activities to herself more strongly than any other power, natural or supernatural" (XXV) [V, Jerz].
- "both energies acted as interchangeable force on man, and by action on man all known force may be measured" (XXV) [V].
- The dynamo "became a symbol of infinity"; "Before the end, one began to pray to it" (XXV) [V].
- Chs. XXXIII–XXXIV: "A Dynamic Theory of History", "A Law of Acceleration" [V, titles]. "The Rule of Phase Applied to History" (1909; printed 1919 in *The Degradation of the Democratic Dogma*) [M].
- The drawing is how an end causes ("solum movet metaphorice attrahendo", Suárez, DM 23.4 n. 9, above). Measured as interchangeable with the dynamo's force, it is taken as an efficient cause: Hobbes's conversion (*De corpore* X.7).

**Leo's hearers.** RN addresses the bishops and argues against the socialists; George answered it. Their record is `modernity-hearers.md`. A guest horizon in the VM is a horizon, not an audience, so the hearers have nodes of their own.

On rights: Villey makes Ockham and Suárez the parents; Tierney roots them in the twelfth-century canonists. Formal cause splits three ways: renamed (Bacon), deleted except the soul (Descartes), unknowable (Locke).
