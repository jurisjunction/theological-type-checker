# Pius XII anchors A4a, but two claims overreach

> **About this report.** It answers three questions raised while formalizing the theory: an independent warrant for A4a, prior art for the canonical reading, and the grounds of the reservation of orders. It was prepared with AI-assisted research, and several quotations reached it through secondary reproductions. The section "Twelve items need checking before print" lists them. Check every quotation against the original before relying on it, and please open an issue for anything you find wrong. "The paper" below is the original programmatic statement that the Core Edition condenses.

**All three questions have clear answers, and each needs about one sentence changed in the paper.** On Q1, A4a has a solid magisterial warrant from before Donum Vitae. Pius XII's addresses of 29 October 1951 and 19 May 1956 place the requirement for licit generation in the spouses' personal conjugal act and say plainly that being married is not enough. But every one of those texts also keeps the bond necessary and defines it as a right *to* that act. The axiom should therefore say closure belongs to the act "not merely" to the standing bond, rather than "not to" it. On Q2, the novelty claim is **partly anticipated**. Each piece of the "grammar" already exists somewhere: c. 1057 §2 read as mutual donation and acceptance, the bond as a relation owed in justice, the c. 1084 impotence/sterility line explained by the self-gift, and a trinitarian ground for conjugal gift and reception. These appear in Hervada, Viladrich, Errázuriz, Burke, Pastwa, Ouellet and a 2003 Rotal sentence. What survives is the canon-by-canon reading of cc. 1056, 1057, 1084, 1091, 1134 and 1135 together as one trinitarian reception/donation grammar, especially its extension to c. 1091 and c. 1135, for which no precedent turned up, plus the executable formal model. On Q3, every magisterial text that gives reasons grounds the reservation in Christ's free choice of the Twelve. That choice was received as normative tradition, and the Church holds no power over the substance of the sacrament. The texts explicitly label the nuptial/iconic sign argument as fittingness. A "sign-of-mission" accommodation is fully consistent on its no-ranking half. It substitutes fittingness for the ground if it says the reservation rests on what the sign signifies. It stays faithful if it says the reservation belongs to the sacramental sign *by Christ's determination*, with the sign's nuptial meaning as the Church's account of why that determination fits. The largest remaining risk is a set of unread Navarra commentaries, chiefly Bañares on cc. 1057/1084/1091 and Fornés on cc. 1134–1135, that could still contain the systematic reading the paper says is absent.

## Pius XII put closure in the act by 1951 and kept the bond necessary

The paper needs a pre-1987 warrant because it derives Donum Vitae's child's-right formulation from A4a. If A4a were sourced to DV, the derivation would be circular. DV largely removes that danger itself. Its treatment of homologous insemination opens with "The teaching of the Magisterium on this point has already been stated." Footnote 51 then lists the Holy Office (1897), Pius XII (1949, 1951, 1956, 1958) and *Mater et Magistra*. The act requirement at II.B.4–5 rests on HV 12 and 14 and on Pius XII 1956 ([Donum Vitae](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19870222_respect-for-human-life_en.html)). The two child's-right sentences carry **no footnote at all** in the vatican.va English. These are the right to be conceived "within marriage" (II.A.1) and the right "to be the fruit of the specific act of the conjugal love of his parents" (II.B.8). They are DV's own synthesis. So deriving them from an A4a footnoted to earlier texts is a genuine derivation, not a circle.

The oldest text on DV's list cannot carry the weight. The Holy Office answered "An adhiberi possit artificialis mulieris foecundatio?" with a bare "Non licere". It was decided on 17 March 1897 and approved by Leo XIII on 26 March. The "24 March" date in the brief is not supported ([Neri](https://www.andrologiaitaliana.it/upload/content/docs/neri_sci_ius.pdf); [Wróbel 2015](https://ojs.tnkul.pl/index.php/rt/article/download/8591/8270)). The response gives no reasons. Gerald Kelly reported in 1947 that recognized moralists read it as condemning only semen obtained "by an unnatural act." Kelly also advised that, "until the dispute is settled," Catholic doctors could follow the opinion that insemination between spouses was permissible if the semen were licitly obtained ([Kelly, *Linacre Quarterly* 14/1 (1947)](https://epublications.marquette.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1127&context=lnq)). The act requirement was therefore **not settled magisterial teaching before 1949**, and the warrant begins with Pius XII.

The 1949 address to Catholic physicians is the first text to separate a bond requirement from a further requirement *within* marriage. Outside marriage, artificial fecundation is condemned because procreation "ne peut être le fruit que du mariage." Within marriage, "ni le désir, en soi très légitime chez les époux, d'avoir un enfant, ne suffit." The verdict is "il faut absolument l'écarter," with an exception for means meant only "à faciliter l'acte naturel" ([Pius XII, 29.IX.1949](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/fr/speeches/1949/documents/hf_p-xii_spe_19490929_votre-presence.html)). In 1949 the act criterion is implicit in that exception. The stated reasons are the principle that the end does not justify the means and the illicit collection of semen ("actes contre nature"). The phrase "pour l'instant" shows the homologous question was still partly open.

The **1951 address to Italian midwives** is where the act becomes the explicit, personalist locus. "L'atto coniugale, nella sua struttura naturale, è un'azione personale, una cooperazione simultanea e immediata dei coniugi," the expression of the mutual gift that effects the "una carne sola." It is "molto più della unione di due germi, la quale si può effettuare anche artificialmente, vale a dire senza l'azione naturale dei coniugi." Reducing it to "una pura funzione organica per la trasmissione dei germi" would turn the home into "un semplice laboratorio biologico." "Perciò" the 1949 address had "formalmente esclusa dal matrimonio la fecondazione artificiale" ([Pius XII, 29.X.1951](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/it/speeches/1951/documents/hf_p-xii_spe_19511029_ostetriche.html)). DV quotes the "azione personale" sentence at II.B.6, footnote 52 (AAS 43, 850). Two citation details matter for the manuscript: the laboratory image belongs to **1951, not 1956**, and the wording is "pura," not "mera." The same address also ties the bond to the act. The conjugal act is "una cooperazione personale, alla quale gli sposi, nel contrarre il matrimonio, si scambiano il diritto."

The **1956 Naples address on fertility** is the fullest pre-1987 statement and the most important source for A4a. On the personalist side: "Il figlio è il frutto dell'unione coniugale… nell'unità di questo atto umano devono essere inserite le condizioni biologiche della generazione." Twelve years before *Humanae Vitae*, and in the context of artificial fecundation, it states a two-way inseparability: "Giammai è permesso di separare questi diversi aspetti così da escludere positivamente sia il proposito della procreazione, sia il rapporto coniugale" ([Pius XII, 19.V.1956, IT](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/it/speeches/1956/documents/hf_p-xii_spe_19560519_fertilita.html)). DV cites it at II.B.4 fn. 40, and *Dignitas Personae* at 16 fn. 29 (AAS 48, 470). In context, "rapporto coniugale" means the conjugal act, not the standing relationship. On the juridical side, artificial fecundation "dépasse les limites du droit que les époux ont acquis par le contrat matrimonial," because the contract "a pour objet non pas l'« enfant », mais les « actes naturels »" ([Pius XII, 19.V.1956, FR](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/fr/speeches/1956/documents/hf_p-xii_spe_19560519_fertilita.html)). The address also contains the first magisterial condemnation of IVF.

Two later texts complete the line. In 1958 Pius XII said he had in 1949 "réprouvé absolument l'insémination entre personnes non-mariées **et même entre époux**." He gives as reasons that it "n'est pas comprise dans les droits des époux" and is contrary to natural law. He also restates separately that new life can be procreated only "dans un mariage valide" ([Pius XII, 12.IX.1958](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/fr/speeches/1958/documents/hf_p-xii_spe_19580912_ematologia.html)). HV 14, aimed at contraception, rejects the idea that a single act could "merge with procreative acts of past and future to form a single entity" ([Humanae Vitae](https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-vi_enc_25071968_humanae-vitae.html)). DV II.B.5 fn. 48 applies exactly this to IVF: it "must be judged in itself and cannot borrow its definitive moral quality from the totality of conjugal life." Structurally, HV 14 is A4a in contraceptive form. The juridical premise is older than all of these. The 1917 Code's c. 1081 §2 defined consent as giving "ius in corpus… in ordine ad actus per se aptos ad prolis generationem" ([Burke, "Canon 1057 and the Object of Matrimonial Consent"](https://cormacburke.me.ke.strathmore.ac.ke/node/276.html)).

The precise answer to Q1 follows from this. Pre-1987 teaching says the requirement for licit generation lies in **the conjugal act as a personal act of both spouses**, and that the marital bond is **necessary but not sufficient**. It does not say the bond is irrelevant. Its juridical strand actually makes the *limits of the bond's right* the reason homologous insemination is illicit. A critic could read that strand as bond-located. The clean warrant for an act-located axiom is the personalist strand (1951 "azione personale"; 1956 "unità di questo atto umano"). Secondary historians agree on this lineage: Pius XII settled an open question by drawing the line at the act, and DV built on it ([W. E. May, Navarra 1999](https://en.unav.edu/web/humanities-and-medical-ethics-unit/bioethics-material/congreso-internacional-bioetica-1999/nuevas-tecnologias-reproductivas-y-ensenanza-catolica); [Abem 2023](https://peterlang.com/document/1304428)).

The paper's own formalisation already encodes the right relation. In `/home/claude/icon/icon_core.lp`, line 99, the act-mode `circuit` rule requires both `closed(A,X,Y)` *and* `bond(X,Y)`. Only the bond-mode rule drops the act. Stress tests S1 and S2 therefore contrast "act and bond" with "bond alone", not "act" with "bond". Only the prose gloss overstates. The recommended text is:

> **A4a (closure in the act).** Among created persons, the closure that makes two sources one principle of a child belongs to the generating act, the spouses' personal conjugal act, and not merely to the standing marital bond. The bond is necessary but not sufficient. What it confers is a right to the conjugal act, not to the child, and an act of generation does not borrow its closure from the totality of married life.
>
> *Footnote:* Pius XII, Address to Italian Midwives, 29 Oct 1951, AAS 43 (1951) 835–854, at 850; Address to the Second World Congress on Fertility and Sterility, 19 May 1956, AAS 48 (1956) 467–474, at 470–473; Address to the Seventh International Congress of Haematology, 12 Sept 1958, AAS 50 (1958) 732–740, at 733; cf. Address to Catholic Physicians, 29 Sept 1949, AAS 41 (1949) 557–561, at 560; Paul VI, *Humanae Vitae* 14, AAS 60 (1968) 490–491; CIC 1917 c. 1081 §2; CIC 1983 c. 1061 §1. DV II.B.4–6 is cited as confirmation ("has already been stated"), not as source.

One weakness remains for a critic. Apart from 1949, the warrant consists of papal allocutions, and only the 1949 address appears in the English Denzinger consulted ([clerus.org Denzinger](https://www.clerus.org/bibliaclerusonline/EN/dx1.htm)). Someone could argue these weigh less than DV. The best reply is DV's own wording: it presents II.B as a restatement of teaching already given and quotes the 1951 and 1956 texts in its main argument.

## Every element of the canonical grammar has a precedent except the set itself

The claim to have made "the move I have not found anywhere" does not survive as worded. Four strands of the reading are already in print, and a canonist reviewer will know at least the first two.

The first strand is the **gift-and-acceptance root of consent, bond and justice**. Within the Navarra–Santa Croce school this is settled common doctrine. Hervada holds that marriage arises from a "libre donación de sí." In it the spouses become "coposesores mutuos" of each other's virility and femininity, and love becomes "el amor en cuanto es un debitum" ([Hervada, *Rev. Chilena de Derecho* 7 (1980)](https://ojs.uc.cl/index.php/Rchd/article/download/79354/60230/230880); ["La identidad del matrimonio"](https://revistadisena.uc.cl/index.php/Rchd/article/download/79362/60238/230896)). His *Diálogos* describes the pact as a moment "de entregarse a sí mismo… y de recibir al otro" ([Hervada, *Diálogos*](https://iveinarabic.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/350123385_Hervada_Javier_Dialogos_Sobre_El_Amor_Y_El_Matrimonio.pdf)). Viladrich in 1987 described consent as "mutua entrega y aceptación del varón y de la mujer en alianza irrevocable" and marriage as a "comunidad debida" ([Viladrich, *Ius Canonicum* 27 (1987)](https://decrecursos1.javeriana.edu.co/Derecho%20matrimonial%20can%c3%b3nico%20/M3/VILADRICH%20sistema%20matrimonial-min.pdf)). Errázuriz makes masculinity and femininity "veri diritti dell'altro" ([Errázuriz 2021](https://es.pusc.it/sites/default/files/can/conv2021/docs/ERRAZURIZ.pdf)). Franceschi grounds fidelity in "la donación y aceptación de la persona misma en su conyugalidad," the nearest verbatim match to the paper's formula ([Franceschi 2015](https://www.almudi.org/articulos/10246-las-propiedades-esenciales-del-matrimonio)). The 2024 Navarra manual teaches that "los esposos se donan en su conyugalidad… estableciendo entre ellos un vínculo, anclado en su dimensión de justicia" ([review of Bañares & González Alonso](https://revistas.upsa.es/index.php/derechocanonico/article/download/1638/1235/4565)). In English, Burke reads c. 1057 §2 as *traditio/acceptatio*. He names acceptance as a separate pole ("not only an unreserved spousal gift, but also an unreserved spousal acceptance") ([Burke, *Angelicum* 75 (1998)](https://www.cormacburke.me.ke.strathmore.ac.ke/node/380.html)). He also treats exclusivity and permanence (c. 1056) as conditions of the gift: "one either gives oneself permanently, or one does not give one's self at all" ([Burke, *Forum* 9 (1998)](https://cormacburke.me.ke.strathmore.ac.ke/node/1181.html)). **The chain c. 1056 → c. 1057 → c. 1134 → c. 1135 is therefore not new.**

The second strand is a **gift-based rationale for c. 1084**. Burke writes that "Physical incapacity for such relations invalidates matrimonial consent" while "Sterility neither invalidates nor prohibits marriage," and derives both from the self-gift of procreative sexuality ([Burke, *Studia Canonica* 26 (1992)](https://cormacburke.me.ke.strathmore.ac.ke/node/274.html)). Olivares D'Angelo reads impotence as "una incapacidad de asumir una obligación esencial del matrimonio" within a c. 1057 §2 self-donation framework ([Olivares D'Angelo, *Est. Ecl.* 78 (2003)](https://revistas.comillas.edu/index.php/estudioseclesiasticos/article/download/10786/10167/23404)). The Rota **coram Defilippi, 16 December 2003**, links cc. 1055 §1, 1061 §1 and 1084 §§1 and 3 through *Familiaris Consortio* 11's "total physical self-giving would be a lie" ([Münster excerpt](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/fb2/d-praktischetheologie/kanonischesrecht/service/rota_1084.pdf)). The older explanation, in the 1977 CDF decree and O'Donnell's commentary, is purely act-based ([CDF 1977](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19770513_impotentia_en.html)). The move from act to gift has therefore already been made.

The third strand is a **trinitarian ground for conjugal gift and reception**, which is not new either. Viladrich (2015) writes "don y acogida recíproca." He says the imago Dei in man and woman "está referida al modo de ser Dios Trinidad" and that "Dios Trino está presente en la fundación de cada matrimonio verdadero" ([Viladrich, *Ius Canonicum* 55 (2015)](https://dadun.unav.edu/bitstream/10171/44348/1/01.pdf)). His anthology has a section titled "Ser entre sí don-aceptación-don" ([*La alianza y la unión conyugal* I, ToC](https://www.udep.edu.pe/icf/wp-content/uploads/sites/24/2020/09/La-alianza-y-la-unión-conyugal-Vol.-I-Índice-y-prólogo.pdf)). In 2021 Castilla de Cortázar presented a "dar-aceptar-don… triádico" to the Santa Croce canon-law faculty ([Castilla 2021](https://es.pusc.it/sites/default/files/can/conv2021/docs/BLANCA%20CASTILLA_ES.pdf)). Pastwa states that "the Triune God… is a true Creator of every particular marital bond," reading cc. 1055, 1057 §2, 1063 and 1134 ([Pastwa, *Ecumeny and Law* 1 (2013)](https://bazhum.muzhp.pl/media/texts/ecumeny-and-law/2013-tom-1/ecumeny_and_law-r2013-t1-s93-115.pdf)). Ouellet treats the Spirit as bond and cites c. 1134 ([Ouellet excerpt](https://katolskakyrkan.se/media/2460/ouelletsacrament.pdf)). The DDF note *Una caro* (24 Nov 2025) joins "mutua donazione" to "l'unico Dio che è comunione nelle relazioni trinitarie" ([DDF](https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/it/bollettino/pubblico/2025/11/24/0904.pdf)).

The fourth strand is the **programmatic claim that marriage law is one structure**. John Paul II told the Rota that the essence in c. 1055 "pervades the entire teaching concerning marriage" ([Rota 1997](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/speeches/1997/january/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_19970127_rota-romana.html)) and that *favor matrimonii* "shapes from within all the canonical norms on marriage" ([Rota 2004](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/speeches/2004/january/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_20040129_roman-rota.html)).

What none of these does is bring the strands together canon by canon. Navarra keeps trinitarian language in its "anthropological foundations" chapters and does canon exegesis in juridical-realist terms (debitum, bien jurídico, justicia). Pastwa and Ouellet apply the trinitarian analogy only to the covenant and bond canons. Burke has the canons and the gift without the Trinity, and the Communio line has the Trinity without the canons. Two canons in particular have **no gift or trinitarian reading anywhere in the sources searched**. For c. 1091 the nearest items are a Navarra course that calls conjugality "la relación fontal de todo parentesco" and Viladrich's remark that "la identidad sexuada es un territorio de intimidad prevalente a la consanguinidad." Neither is offered as a reading of the canon ([UNAV course guide](https://asignatura.unav.edu/antropologia-de-la-conyugalidad-dcanonico); [Viladrich 2015, p. 541](https://dadun.unav.edu/bitstream/10171/44348/1/01.pdf)). For c. 1135 nothing was found. Nor was any formalisation of the marriage canons found: no logic programming, no deontic or Hohfeldian model. The nearest items are an Orthodox canon-law ontology that does not cover marriage and a 1993 expert system whose scope is unstated ([OrthoCCL](https://orthocclproject.sde.uom.gr/?p=20); [Matrim, DEXA '93](https://araid.es/en/node/25639)). The paper's claim that the Code *as promulgated* already has this form is contested from both sides. Pastwa says the conciliar renewal "was by no means completed." Torfs quotes Örsy that the Code "defines marriage as covenant and then keeps talking about it as contract" ([Pastwa 2016](https://bazhum.muzhp.pl/media/texts/philosophy-and-canon-law/2016-tom-2/philosophy_and_canon_law-r2016-t2-s203-219.pdf); [Torfs](https://cormacburke.me.ke.strathmore.ac.ke/node/97.html)). John Paul II's unity statements support it.

The paper should also meet four objections head-on. Burke would resist a trinitarian ground for the *sexual* gift: "God is not body; there is no sex in God." He also warns that spouses "can *bind* themselves to one another, but they cannot actually *give* themselves" ([Burke 2010](https://cormacburke.me.ke.strathmore.ac.ke/node/87.html)). Hervada and Lombardía restrict the bond to "virilidad y feminidad," so that marriage "no es una unión plena y total, sino parcial" ([Franceschi 2014](https://revistas-colaboracion.juridicas.unam.mx/index.php/ars-iuris/article/download/30067/27142)). Augustine and Aquinas rejected the family as an image of the Trinity, as a reviewer of Ouellet notes ([Brown review](https://catholicbooksreview.org/2006/ouellet.htm)). The paper's own tests T6a and T6b formally confirm that no family-to-Trinity mapping exists. The paper can use that result to show its analogy concerns reception/donation *positions*, not a family-as-Trinity image. Finally, because Hervada's debitum and Errázuriz's "veri diritti dell'altro" already yield reciprocal rights, the paper must show what the trinitarian ground adds to c. 1135 beyond equality of debt. The recommended novelty sentence is:

> That the spouses give and accept themselves in their conjugality (c. 1057 §2), that the bond so formed is a relation owed in justice from which equal rights and duties flow (cc. 1134–1135), that its essential properties are conditions of the gift (c. 1056), and that impotence invalidates while sterility does not because the one prevents the gift and the other only its fruit (c. 1084), are established positions (Hervada; Viladrich; Errázuriz; Bañares; Burke; Rota c. Defilippi, 16 Dec 2003). A trinitarian ground for conjugal gift and reception has also been proposed (Viladrich 2015; Pastwa 2013, 2016; Ouellet). What I have not found is a reading that takes cc. 1056, 1057, 1084, 1091, 1134 and 1135 together, canon by canon, as instances of one reception/donation grammar with that ground, and in particular one that brings the consanguinity impediment (c. 1091) and the equality of spousal rights (c. 1135) under it. Nor have I found a formal model in which the outcomes of c. 1084 and c. 1057 §1 are derived rather than stipulated. Whether the Code as promulgated already has this form is disputed (Pastwa; Örsy as quoted by Torfs; contrast John Paul II, Rota 1997 and 2004), and I offer it as a reading of the text, not a claim about legislative intent.

## The magisterium grounds the reservation in Christ's choice and reads the sign as fittingness

Every magisterial text that states a reason gives the same ground. *Inter Insigniores* (1976) rests the reservation on fidelity "to the example of the Lord," on the "normative character" of an "unbroken tradition," and on Pius XII's principle that "The Church has no power over the substance of the sacraments, that is to say, over what Christ the Lord… determined should be maintained in the sacramental sign" (Intro, nn. 1–4) ([Inter Insigniores](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19761015_inter-insigniores_en.html)). *Ordinatio Sacerdotalis* quotes Paul VI's "very fundamental reasons" and his phrase "The real reason is that… Christ established things in this way." It closes with "the Church has no authority whatsoever to confer priestly ordination on women" (nn. 1–2, 4). Its text does not contain "in persona Christi", "Bridegroom" or "sign" ([OS reproduction](https://archive.wf-f.org/OrdSac.html); [vatican.va](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_letters/1994/documents/hf_jp-ii_apl_19940522_ordinatio-sacerdotalis.html)). The Catechism says the Church "recognizes herself to be bound by this choice made by the Lord himself. For this reason, the ordination of women is not possible," citing CIC c. 1024 ([CCC 1577](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4X.HTM)). The 1995 Responsum calls the teaching "founded on the written Word of God," preserved in Tradition and "set forth infallibly by the ordinary and universal Magisterium" ([CDF Responsum](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19951028_dubium-ordinatio-sac_en.html)). Ladaria (2018) places the impossibility in the "substance of the sacrament" (DH 1728) ([Ladaria](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/ladaria-ferrer/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20180529_caratteredefinitivo-ordinatiosacerdotalis_en.html)).

The texts are equally consistent in labelling the iconic/nuptial argument as something other than the ground. *Inter Insigniores* 5 introduces it "to illustrate this norm by showing the profound fittingness," and adds: "It is not a question here of bringing forward a demonstrative argument, but of clarifying this teaching by the analogy of faith." OS 2 calls these "other theological reasons which illustrate the appropriateness of the divine provision." The 1995 reflection calls them reasons "ex convenientia" which "have their undoubted value, and yet… are not conceived or employed as if they were strictly logical proofs" ([CDF reflection](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_19951028_commento-dubium-ordinatio-sac_en.html)). The 1977 commentary is sharper still: the theological reflection "does not involve the magisterium," because "infallibility affects the doctrinal affirmation, not the arguments intended to explain it" ([commentary, womenpriests.org reproduction](https://womenpriests.org/church/intercom-inter-insigniores-official-commentary/)). The commentary's official status is disputed, however. A CDF consultor, Ligier, called it the "unofficial Comment" ([Ligier via EWTN](https://origin.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/question-of-admitting-women-to-the-ministerial-priesthood-10122)).

| Text | Stated ground | How it labels the sign argument | No-ranking language |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Inter Insigniores* (1976) | Christ's example; normative tradition; no power over "the substance of the sacraments" | "fittingness"; "not… a demonstrative argument" | "not… any personal superiority… in the order of values, but only… functions and service" (5); priesthood "does not form part of the rights of the individual" (6) |
| *Mulieris Dignitatem* 26 (1988) | Christ "acted in a completely free and sovereign manner" | "This explanation confirms" II ([MD](https://www.intratext.com/IXT/ENG0593/_PR.HTM)) | Hierarchy "totally ordered to the holiness of Christ's members" (MD 27, via CL 51) |
| *Christifideles Laici* 51 (1988) | "found in the expressed will of Christ" | "can be understood from the rapport between Christ, the Spouse, and his Bride" ([CL 51](https://www.clerus.org/bibliaclerusonline/en/dp3.htm)) | "the area of function, not of dignity and holiness" |
| *Ordinatio Sacerdotalis* (1994) | "Christ established things in this way"; "no authority whatsoever" | Mentioned only as illustrating "appropriateness" | "cannot mean that women are of lesser dignity" (3) |
| CDF Responsum and reflection (1995) | Scripture, Tradition, ordinary and universal Magisterium | "ex convenientia… not… strictly logical proofs" | "Diversity of mission in no way compromises equality of personal dignity" |
| *Evangelii Gaudium* 104 (2013) | Not argued: "not a question open to discussion" | Reservation "as a sign of Christ the Spouse who gives himself in the Eucharist" ([EG](https://www.vatican.va/evangelii-gaudium/en/files/assets/basic-html/page83.html)) | "function, not… dignity or holiness" |

Measured against this, the accommodation is **consistent on the no-ranking half** and close to verbatim. The 1995 sentence "Diversity of mission in no way compromises equality of personal dignity" is almost the paper's own "sign-of-mission, not ranking of persons." The texts add a clause the paper should adopt: the priesthood is not an individual right (*Inter Insigniores* 6; CCC 1578, "No one has a right to receive the sacrament of Holy Orders"). The accommodation **substitutes fittingness for the ground** if it says the reservation is *grounded in* what the sacramental sign signifies, meaning natural resemblance or Christ the Bridegroom represented. That reverses the order the texts state, and it has a practical cost. It ties the doctrine's standing to an argument the magisterium has declined to stake itself on. A successful rebuttal of the iconic argument would then topple the paper's account while leaving the magisterium's untouched. Such rebuttals exist: Ferrara argues that Aquinas's argument concerns eminence and that "None of this has to do… with a 'natural resemblance'" ([Ferrara, *TS* 56 (1995)](https://theologicalstudies.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/56.1.5.pdf)), and Egan presses the Bride-symmetry objection ([Egan, *Commonweal* 2008](https://www.commonwealmagazine.org/why-not)). Defenders and critics agree on how the texts are built. Butler: "These 'fundamental reasons' have real priority over the 'theological arguments'" ([Butler 2007](https://www.laityfamilylife.va/content/dam/laityfamilylife/Documenti/donna/teologia/english/womens-ordination-still-an-issue.pdf)). Dulles: the symbolic argument "does not prove that Christ could not have called women" but shows his decision "was not arbitrary" ([Dulles 1996](https://www.americamagazine.org/faith/2019/05/24/avery-dulles-women-and-priesthood-1996)). The CTSA: the magisterium offers "arguments for fittingness as an explanation and corroboration" ([CTSA 1997](https://natcath.org/NCR_Online/documents/ctsa1.htm)).

The accommodation has real textual cover if it is reworded rather than dropped. The ground is itself stated in sacramental terms: what Christ "determined should be maintained in the sacramental sign" (*Inter Insigniores* 4), and the "substance of the sacrament" (Ladaria). EG 104 calls the reservation "a sign of Christ the Spouse." The 1995 reflection links Christ's will to "the ontological and anthropological truth of the creation of the two sexes," which makes the sign the intelligibility of the will rather than a rival to it. The paper should not swing to calling the sign argument mere ornament either. *Inter Insigniores* 5 says the role "must be taken by a man," and Ladaria calls maleness "indispensable." Ligier and, from the pro-teaching side, Monica Migliorino Miller treat it as integral to the doctrine ([Miller, *First Things* 2007](https://firstthings.com/women-and-the-catholic-priesth/)). The recommended text is:

> The reservation of priestly ordination to men (c. 1024) rests on Christ's free determination of what belongs to the sacramental sign of Orders. That determination is received in constant tradition, the Church has no power over it, and it is taught as to be held definitively (*Inter Insigniores* 4; *Ordinatio Sacerdotalis* 4; CDF, Responsum, 1995; Ladaria 2018). The Church reads its meaning by fittingness and the analogy of faith as the sign of Christ the Bridegroom acting *in persona Christi* (*Inter Insigniores* 5; MD 26; CL 51; EG 104). It marks a difference of mission and function, not of personal dignity, and confers no right on anyone (*Inter Insigniores* 5–6; OS 3; CCC 1578).

This wording has a consequence for the formal model, which currently contains no Orders module. When one is added, the reservation should enter as a **datum**, a positive determination, and not as a **theorem** derived from the pole axioms. Deriving it would make the grammar's fittingness the ground in exactly the way the texts forbid. The model can then show that the datum is consistent with, and fitting to, the grammar, for example by passing the existing no-ranking prohibitions X2, X6 and X10. The paper should also avoid calling OS itself infallible. The CDF calls it "an act of the ordinary Papal Magisterium, in itself not infallible," which "witnesses to the infallibility" of the doctrine. Dulles notes that only "firm assent," not divine faith, was required, and Gaillardetz disputes the infallibility claim ([Gaillardetz 1996](https://gaillardetz.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/infallibility_and_the_ordination_of_women.pdf)).

## Conclusion

All three problems share one shape. The magisterial sources state a layered relation, and the paper's prose flattens it into a binary contrast. The bond is necessary but not sufficient, not irrelevant. The sign gives the intelligibility of a determination, not its ground. Prior canonists supply the pieces, and the paper's contribution is the assembly. The formal apparatus suits these layered relations better than the prose does: `icon_core.lp` already requires act *and* bond. The same discipline, applied to Orders, means entering the reservation as a datum the grammar illuminates rather than a theorem it produces.

The narrowed claims are also stronger ones. Reframed, the paper stops competing with Hervada, Viladrich and Burke on ground they hold. It rests its originality on three things a canonist reviewer can check and will find unoccupied: c. 1091 and c. 1135 read as instances of the grammar, the canon-by-canon assembly, and derived rather than stipulated retrodictions of c. 1084 and c. 1057 §1. The Q3 rewording makes the paper's account of the reservation robust to any rebuttal of the iconic argument, a robustness the magisterium already has.
