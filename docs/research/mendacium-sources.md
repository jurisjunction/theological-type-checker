# TTC-MENDACIUM(7): sources

## NAME

**mendacium-sources** — the evidence record for TTC-MENDACIUM: Augustine on the words given to make the mind known, Thomas on the lie evil by its genus, Kant's four arguments against lying, Grotius's right of the one addressed, Constant's duty owed only to a right, Hobbes's fraud in war, and the Catechism's article on lying.

## DESCRIPTION

Source verification only, compiled 10 November 2026. It designs no rules and is an evidence record, so it keeps prose (rule 10). Each item gives the claim, the locus, a short quotation exactly as the fetch or search tool returned it, the URL, a status and anything still owed. Quotations of the primary texts are under 40 words; of secondary works, a phrase.

Every check went through the fetch tool or the search tool. Hosts were not asked for URLs that had not come back from a search, so some items rest on a second copy of the text or on a secondary source. Where that happens the item says so. TTC-MODERNITY's evidence record for lying (`cases-usury-lying.md`), which `linguae-sources.md` cites, was never committed; nothing here rests on it.

## STATUS

- **verified**: the wording and locus were checked against a page fetched or returned in this pass.
- **verified (secondary)**: checked against a named secondary source that quotes or reports the text.
- **owed**: not confirmed online. The item names the print source that would settle it.
- **Owed:** inside a verified item marks a sub-point (a Latin original, a line number) that was not reached.

## A. AUGUSTINE

**A1. *Enchiridion* 7.22: words were instituted to make thoughts known.**
- Latin, as the Electronic *Manipulus florum* gives it from CCSL 46 (ed. Evans, p. 62, ll. 73–77): "Et utique uerba propterea sunt instituta non per quae inuicem se homines fallant sed per quae in alterius quisque notitiam cogitationes suas perferat." / "Verbis ergo uti ad fallaciam, non ad quod sunt instituta, peccatum est." (https://manipulus-project.wlu.ca/MFfontes/FrausF.pdf)
- English (J. F. Shaw, NPNF): "Now it is evident that speech was given to man, not that men might therewith deceive one another, but that one man might make known his thoughts to another." (https://logoslibrary.org/augustine/enchiridion/022.html)
- The parity, same chapter: "For it is possible to do this by theft also, as when we steal from a rich man who never feels the loss, to give to a poor man". Of those who lie only to save a man: "it is not the deceit, but their good intention, that is justly praised" (logoslibrary, as above).
- The chapter ends on Mt 5:37, said to "the heirs of the new covenant": "Let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil." (https://bkv.unifr.ch/works/cpl-295/versions/the-enchiridion/divisions/30)
- Date: written after the death of Jerome, "which occurred Sept. 30, 420" (NPNF's introductory notice, https://www.clerus.org/bibliaclerusonline/en/40.htm).

Status: **verified**. Shaw renders *instituta* as "given". Owed: the CCSL page itself, and the Latin of the parity and of the close.

**A2. *De doctrina christiana* II.2.3: the given sign, and its only cause.**
- Latin, through the *Stanford Encyclopedia*: "Nec ulla causa est nobis significandi, id est signi dandi, nisi ad … traiciendum in alterius animum id quod animo gerit qui signum dat" (De doctr. chr. II 3; https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2010/entries/semiotics-medieval/).
- English (NPNF): "Conventional signs, on the other hand, are those which living beings mutually exchange for the purpose of showing, as well as they can, the feelings of their minds, or their perceptions, or their thoughts." The next sentence: "Nor is there any reason for giving a sign except the desire of drawing forth and conveying into another's mind what the giver of the sign has in his own mind." (https://bkv.unifr.ch/works/cpl-263-1/versions/contents-of-christian-doctrine/divisions/48)
- II.1.2: "Now some signs are natural, others conventional." (https://bkv.unifr.ch/works/cpl-263-1/versions/contents-of-christian-doctrine/divisions/47)

Status: **verified (secondary)** for the Latin, through the SEP, which elides a phrase; **verified** for the English. NPNF's "conventional" renders *data*, given. Owed: the full Latin of II.2.3.

**A3. *De mendacio* and *Contra mendacium*.**
Thomas reports the definition from *Contra mendacium*: "nemo dubitet mentiri eum qui falsum enuntiat causa fallendi" (II-II q. 110 a. 1 s.c.; https://www.logicmuseum.com/wiki/Authors/Thomas_Aquinas/Summa_Theologiae/Part_IIb/Q110), and Augustine's eight kinds of lie, from *Contra mendacium* xiv (a. 2 obj. 2; https://ccel.org/a/aquinas/summa/SS/SS110.html). The Catechism cites *De mendacio* 4, 5 for its definition (G1).
Status: **verified (secondary)**, through Thomas and the Catechism. Owed: both treatises, and Augustine on the midwives and Rahab, which the case does not enter.

## B. THOMAS

**B1. ST II-II q. 110 a. 3: the lie evil by its genus, words naturally signs of thoughts.**
- Opening of the corpus: "Respondeo dicendum quod illud quod est secundum se malum ex genere, nullo modo potest esse bonum et licitum," (logicmuseum, as A3).
- The rest, as Casey quotes it: "Mendacium autem est malum ex genere. Est enim actus cadens super indebitam materiam: cum enim voces sint signa naturaliter intellectum, innaturale est et indebitum quod aliquis voce significet id quod non habet in mente." (G. Casey, "A Problem of Unity in St Thomas's Account of Human Action", https://es.scribd.com/document/7980381/A-Problem-of-Unity-in-St-Thomas-Account-of-Human-Action)
- English (Dominican Fathers): "Now a lie is evil in respect of its genus, since it is an action bearing on undue matter." (https://ccel.org/ccel/aquinas/summa.SS_Q110_A3.html)

Status: **verified** for the opening and the English; **verified (secondary)** for the rest. Casey's copy reads "intellectum" (and "nullo mode" earlier); the Leonine "intellectuum" is [M]. Owed: the Leonine page.

**B2. a. 3 ad 4: not even to deliver another from death.**
- "Ad quartum dicendum quod mendacium non solum habet rationem peccati ex damno quod infert proximo" (logicmuseum, as A3).
- English: "Therefore it is not lawful to tell a lie in order to deliver another from any danger whatever." (ccel, as B1). The fetch tool returned the comparison between as a summary, not a quotation: as one may not steal to give alms, one may not lie to protect another; the truth may be hidden prudently.
- Objection 4, which the reply answers: "Therefore a man may lawfully lie, to save another from committing murder, or another from being killed." (https://drbo.org/summa/question/34303.htm)

Status: **verified** for the opening, the conclusion and the objection. Owed: the reply's middle, Latin and English, with the comparison to theft as Thomas words it, and its citation of Augustine on hiding the truth.

**B3. a. 1: the species of the lie from formal falsity.**
"Sed tamen ratio mendacii sumitur a formali falsitate, ex hoc scilicet quod aliquis habet voluntatem falsum enuntiandi." The corpus opens: "actus moralis ex duobus speciem sortitur, scilicet ex obiecto, et ex fine." (logicmuseum, as A3)
Status: **verified**.

**B4. a. 3 ad 2: the midwives.**
"The midwives were rewarded, not for their lie, but for their fear of God, and for their good-will" (ccel, as B1). Objection 3 lists Abraham, Jacob and Judith (drbo, as B2).
Status: **verified**. Not entered (CAVEATS).

**B5. *In Peri hermeneias* I lect. 4: names signify by institution.**
"secundum placitum, idest secundum institutionem humanam a beneplacito hominis procedentem. Et per hoc differt nomen a vocibus significantibus naturaliter, sicut sunt gemitus infirmorum" (n. 6; https://www.logicmuseum.com/wiki/Authors/Thomas_Aquinas/perihermenias/perihermenias_I/L4). TTC-LINGUAE's record has the same lecture, n. 4: words signify "ex institutione humana".
Status: **verified**. The tension with B1's "naturaliter" is noted in CAVEATS; it is not entered.

## C. KANT

**C1. *Groundwork* 4:422: the false promise as a universal law.**
"Denn die Allgemeinheit eines Gesetzes, dass jeder, nachdem er in Not zu sein glaubt, versprechen könne, was ihm einfällt, mit dem Vorsatz, es nicht zu halten, würde das Versprechen und den Zweck, den man damit haben mag, selbst unmöglich machen" (https://austria-forum.org/web-books/en/metaphysik00de1785isds/000032, the 1785 edition, its p. 32).
Status: **verified**. The first section's lying promise (4:402–403) is reached only through a teaching page that locates it ("Kant 1997, 15/4:402-03", https://myweb.ecu.edu/mccartyr/GW/lyingpromise.asp); its words are owed.

**C2. *Groundwork* 4:429–430: humanity never merely as a means.**
- The formula, in a published translation: "So act that you use the humanity, whether in your own person or in the person of any other, always at the same time as an end, never merely as a means. (G, 4: 429)" (https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/product/4DDF60DF29EB55D3DF64633B8BBC9C96/core-reader)
- The false promise under it: "he whom I want to use for my purposes by such a promise cannot possibly agree to my way of behaving toward him" (Kant 1997, 38/4:429-30; https://myweb.ecu.edu/mccartyr/GW/FormulaOfHumanity.asp).
- That humanity is an end "not as an end to be effected" is TTC-MODERNITY's warrant for `kant_humanity`, a reading against its author (4:437 [M]).

Status: **verified (secondary)**, in English. Owed: the German of 4:429–430.

**C3. *Tugendlehre* §9 (6:429–430): the lie as the throwing away of human dignity, and against the natural end of speech.**
- "Die größte Verletzung der Pflicht des Menschen gegen sich selbst, bloß als moralisches Wesen betrachtet (die Menschheit in seiner Person), ist das Widerspiel der Wahrhaftigkeit: die Lüge" (https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/immanuel-kant/books/ethische-elementarlehre/chapter/4/).
- "Die Lüge ist Wegwerfung und gleichsam Vernichtung seiner Menschenwürde." (https://www.fachverband-ethik.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Baden-Wu%CC%88rttemberg/dateien/unterrichtsmaterialien/Reader-Kant.pdf, which breaks the line after "seiner").
- "aber die Mitteilung seiner Gedanken an jemanden durch Worte, die doch das Gegenteil von dem (absichtlich) enthalten, was der Sprechende dabei denkt, ist ein der natürlichen Zweckmäßigkeit seines Vermögens der Mitteilung seiner Gedanken gerade entgegengesetzter Zweck, mithin Verzichttuung auf seine Persönlichkeit" (zeno.org, *Die Metaphysik der Sitten*, Tugendlehre, Erstes Buch, Zweites Hauptstück, §9).
- Next: man as a moral being cannot use himself as a physical being "nicht als bloßes Mittel (Sprachmaschine) brauchen, das an den inneren Zweck (der Gedankenmitteilung) nicht gebunden wäre" (zeno.org, as above).
- The structure of §9 is disputed: Bacin and Schönecker propose two transpositions; on their count, Kant introduces the concept of the lie at 429.01–13 (*Kant-Studien* 101, 2010, 247–252; https://www.uni-siegen.de/files/default/media/documents/2026-01/2010_zwei_konjekturvorschlaege_zur_tugendlehre.pdf). A reply by Brandt followed, and their rejoinder (*Kant-Studien* 102, 2011, 113–119).

Status: **verified**, modernised spelling (the Academy's "Mittheilung", "Verzichtthuung" [M]). Owed: the Academy edition's lines for each sentence.

**C4. "Über ein vermeintes Recht aus Menschenliebe zu lügen" (1797): a formal duty to everyone; the source of right made useless.**
- "Wahrhaftigkeit in Aussagen, die man nicht umgehen kann, ist formale Pflicht des Menschen gegen Jeden, es mag ihm oder einem Andern daraus auch noch so großer Nachteil erwachsen" (https://silo.tips/download/kant-im-ethikunterricht); located at "VRML, AA 08: 426.14–16" (https://philarchive.org/archive/PROPTS-2).
- "ich mache, so viel an mir ist, daß Aussagen (Deklarationen) überhaupt keinen Glauben finden, mithin auch alle Rechte, die auf Verträgen gegründet werden, wegfallen und ihre Kraft einbüßen; welches ein Unrecht ist, das der Menschheit überhaupt zugefügt wird." (https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/immanuel-kant/books/kleinere-schriften/chapter/3)
- "Denn sie schadet jederzeit einem Anderen, wenn gleich nicht einem andern Menschen, doch der Menschheit überhaupt, indem sie die Rechtsquelle unbrauchbar macht." (projekt-gutenberg, as above)
- Against the jurists' definition: the lie "bedarf nicht des Zusatzes, daß sie einem Anderen schaden müsse; wie die Juristen es zu ihrer Definition verlangen" (silo.tips, as above); the jurists' formula, "mendacium est falsiloquium in praeiudicium alterius" (https://wikipedia.com/wiki/On_a_supposed_right_to_lie).
- Against Constant's principle, which Kant quotes: "Die Wahrheit zu sagen ist eine Pflicht, aber nur gegen denjenigen, welcher ein Recht auf die Wahrheit hat"; its formula is unclear, "indem Wahrheit kein Besitztum ist" (projekt-gutenberg, as above).
- First printed in *Berlinische Blätter* 1 (1797), 301–314 (Wikipedia, as above).

Status: **verified**. Owed: the Academy edition's lines for all but the first.

**C5. Readings of Kant on lying.**
- That Kant's lie is a technical term, a statement "contrary to duty", and that much of the literature misreads it through the Groundwork's lying promise (https://rcientificas.uninorte.edu.co/index.php/eidos/article/download/3671/2387/12438) [S].
- Akimoto (2017) argues that Kant does not forbid every false statement: he distinguishes the lie, contrary to duty, from the untruth that is not (https://www.peterlang.com/view/9783631698631/chapter08.xhtml) [S].
- Varden (2010) argues that Kant's ground in the 1797 essay is not that the one lied to is wronged (as Wikipedia reports her, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_a_Supposed_Right_to_Tell_Lies_from_Benevolent_Motives) [S].
- Brieskorn reads 6:429 as a conclusion from a natural endowment to an unconditional ought, a remnant ("Übrigbleibsel") of classical natural law in Kant's wording, and sets it beside ST II–II (https://www.kas.de/documents/252038/253252/Naturrecht_brieskorn.pdf) [S].
- Liessmann: Kant rejects the outward lie "mit ähnlichen Argumenten, wie sie schon Augustinus gebrauchte" (https://efphilo.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/07/liessmann-der-wille-zum-schein.pdf) [S].

Status: **verified (secondary)**: each item reports its author, not the text. None is entered.

## D. GROTIUS

**D1. *De iure belli ac pacis* III.1.11: the form of the illicit lie.**
- The chapter's summary: "Mendacii qua illicitum est formam consistere in repugnantia cum iure alterius, quod explicatur." (https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/groo001bjad01_01/groo001bjad01_01_0035.php, the edition of 1939)
- The general notion: "Ad communem ergo mendacii notionem requiritur ut quod dicitur, scribitur, notatur, innuitur, intelligi aliter nequeat quam in eum sensum qui a mente proferentis discrepet." (DBNL, as above)
- The form of the illicit lie, none "praeter repugnantiam cum iure existente ac manente eius ad quem sermo aut nota dirigitur" (DBNL, as above).
- The right: "Id autem nihil est aliud quam iudicandi libertas, quam homines colloquentes his quibus colloquuntur debere quasi pacto quodam tacito intelliguntur." (DBNL, as above)

Status: **verified**. The fetch tool returned the body in segments, joined here where they run on; the summary sentence came back from a search of the same page. Owed: the paragraph numbers within XI.

**D2. III.1.12–16: where there is no lie.**
To an infant or a madman (XII); where the one addressed is not deceived, though a third is (XIII); where he will not mind the hurt to his judgment (XIV); by a superior right (XV); and XVI: "Forte et cum vitam innocentis aut par aliquid tueri aliter non possumus." Its body: "Quintum esse potest quoties vita innocentis aut par aliquid aliter servari" … "et alter ab improbi facinoris perfectione aliter averti non potest" (DBNL, as D1).
Status: **verified**, in segments; the words between the two of XVI's body (a "non potest" [M]) were not returned. XVII–XVIII (to enemies; not to promises) are in the summary and not entered.

**D3. Property by pact.** Grotius's property arises by agreement, express or tacit (DIBP II.2.2), and TTC-MODERNITY types his argument there by the will's act (`modernity-sources.md`, Grotius; the case `modernity_property`). The right of the one addressed, owed "quasi pacto quodam tacito", is typed the same way.

## E. CONSTANT

**E1. *Des réactions politiques* (an V, 1797), ch. VIII, "Des principes".**
- "Le principe moral, par exemple, que dire la vérité est un devoir, s'il était pris d'une manière absolue et isolée, rendrait toute société impossible." (https://philosophie.nathan.fr/9782091727981/asset/129_ch4_texte_kant.pdf)
- "L'idée de devoir est inséparable de celle de droits : un devoir est ce qui, dans un être, correspond aux droits d'un autre. Là où il n'y a pas de droits, il n'y a pas de devoirs." (Nathan, as above)
- "Dire la vérité n'est donc un devoir qu'envers ceux qui ont droit à la vérité. Or nul homme n'a droit à la vérité qui" (Nathan, as above, cut there); Kant's German gives the rest: "Kein Mensch aber hat Recht auf eine Wahrheit, die Anderen schadet." (C4)
- Date: a facsimile catalogued "[S.l.], [s.n.], an V (1797)" (https://www.helveticarchives.admin.ch/detail.aspx?ID=1761683); the *Œuvres complètes* (1998) list it under "V (1797)" (https://philosophy.hse.ru/article/view/9809). The Nathan textbook dates it 1796.

Status: **verified**. Owed: the first edition's page, and the French of the last clause.

## F. HOBBES

**F1. *Leviathan* 13 (1651): force and fraud in war.**
- "Where there is no common Power, there is no Law: where no Law, no Injustice. Force, and Fraud, are in warre the two Cardinall vertues." (https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Leviathan_(1651)/Chapter_13)
- The end men pursue there: "in the way to their End, (which is principally their owne conservation, and sometimes their delectation only,)" (Wikisource, as above).

Status: **verified**.

**F2. *Leviathan* 4: speech an invention; the lie an abuse.**
- "But the most noble and profitable invention of all other was that of speech" (https://liberalarts.austincc.edu/philosophy-religion-humanities/?p=756, modernised spelling).
- The third abuse: "Thirdly, when by words they declare that to be their will which is not." (as above)

Status: **verified**, modernised. Not entered: the abuse of speech is no prohibition in war (F1), and the case asks whether every lie is wrong.

## G. THE CATECHISM

**G1. CCC 2482: the definition, and the Lord's denunciation.**
"A lie consists in speaking a falsehood with the intention of deceiving." / "The Lord denounces lying as the work of the devil" (https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8K.HTM). The definition cites Augustine, *De mendacio* 4, 5: PL 40, 491 (https://usccb.org/sites/default/files/flipbooks/catechism/597, the second edition, where the note is 281).
Status: **verified**, in both editions.

**G2. CCC 2483: the right to know.** The first edition's clause and the Latin of 1997 without it are TTC-LINGUAE's (`linguae-sources.md`, "Case: CCC 2483"; the case `linguae_ccc2483`). vatican.va's English still has "someone who has the right to know the truth" (vatican.va, as G1).
Status: **verified** there; not re-fetched here beyond the English.

**G3. CCC 2485: by its very nature.**
"By its very nature, lying is to be condemned." / "It is a profanation of speech, whereas the purpose of speech is to communicate known truth to others." (vatican.va, as G1). The second edition has the same words (https://www.aquinascollege.edu/wp-content/uploads/From-CCC-02-24-2020-For-Truth-and-Love.pdf).
Status: **verified**, in both editions.

**G4. CCC 2486: destructive of society.**
"Since it violates the virtue of truthfulness, a lie does real violence to another." / "Lying is destructive of society; it undermines trust among men and tears apart the fabric of social relationships." (vatican.va, as G1)
Status: **verified** in the first edition's English; the second edition is reached only in part (https://ascensionpress.com/blogs/articles/is-it-ever-morally-ok-to-lie).

## H. PUFENDORF

**H1. *De officio* I.10: the tacit convention of a language, and the duty not to deceive.**
"natural law prescribes to men this duty, that no one deceive another by language, or by other signs designed to express the thoughts of the mind" (I.10.1); "there must be among the users of the same language a tacit convention, to designate a certain thing by a certain word and no other" (I.10.2; https://www.constitution.org/2-Authors/puf/puf-dut_110.htm, an English translation).
Status: **verified**, in English. Not entered: context for the reading of words as given (A2) and for Grotius's pact (D1).
