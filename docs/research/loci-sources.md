# The deposit on the last end and on latria, Spinoza's God, and the schools on the attributes: the evidence record for TTC-LOCI

This is the verification record for TTC-LOCI: the loci and alleles for the schools' readings of divine simplicity and of essence and esse, and "substitution" (a creature put in God's place, entered as a claim with its claimant) as a fault on the deposit's own rules. It was compiled on 24 October 2026. It is an evidence record, so it keeps prose (rule 10). Nothing here is encoded; the bearings on the encoding are proposals.

**How sure each item is:**
- **[V]** The wording was checked against a text fetched in this pass.
- **[S]** It was checked through a named secondary source that quotes or reports the primary.
- **[M]** It comes from memory or a standard citation and was not re-checked. It is unverified until someone checks it in print.

[V] means the fetch tool returned the words verbatim from the named page; it corrected the requested wording several times (noted), which is some evidence of fidelity. An English page quoted for a Latin text is marked with the translation it names. Quotations are under 25 words.

**Denzinger numbers.** No DH volume was opened. The numbers are checked through the Catechism's footnotes on vatican.va (which cite DS) and one DH range. For these texts DH keeps Schönmetzer's DS numbers [M].

### Nicaea II, the *horos* (787; DH 600–601)

From the critical edition: ACO ser. II, vol. III, Actio VII, pp. 826 (Greek) and 827 (Anastasius's Latin) [V, cte.oeaw.ac.at/edd/Lamberz2016.pdf; the page gives the series, the editor Lamberz is M].

- **Honour, not true latria.** "καὶ ταύταις ἀσπασμὸν καὶ τιμητικὴν προσκύνησιν ἀπονέμειν, οὐ μὴν τὴν κατὰ πίστιν ἡμῶν ἀληθινὴν λατρείαν, ἣ πρέπει μόνῃ τῇ θείᾳ φύσει" (p. 826, ll. 11–12) [V]. Latin: "et ad osculum et ad honoratoriam his adorationem tribuendam, non tamen ad ueram latrian, quae secundum fidem est quaeque solam diuinam naturam decet" (p. 827, ll. 11–13) [V]. English (Price): "the veneration of honour"; "the true worship corresponding to our faith, which pertains to the divine nature alone" [S: J. Clark, *North American Anglican*, quoting Price, *The Acts of the Second Council of Nicaea* (2018)].
- **The honour passes.** "ἡ γὰρ τῆς εἰκόνος τιμὴ ἐπὶ τὸ πρωτότυπον διαβαίνει" (ll. 16–17); "imaginis enim honor ad primitiuum transit" (l. 16) [V]. And: "ὁ προσκυνῶν τὴν εἰκόνα προσκυνεῖ ἐν αὐτῇ τοῦ ἐγγραφομένου τὴν ὑπόστασιν"; "qui adorat imaginem, adorat in ea depicti subsistentiam" [V].
- **Basil is not named in the text.** The edition's apparatus only: "ἡ – διαβαίνει ~ Bas. spir. (CPG 2839) c. 18,45,19–20 Pruche" [V]. So the *horos* quotes Basil without naming him. CCC 2132 n. 70 cites "St. Basil, *De Spiritu Sancto* 18, 45 (PG 32, 149C)" with "Council of Nicaea II: DS 601" and Trent, DS 1821–1825 [V, vatican.va, ENG0015/__P7F.HTM].
- **The numbers.** DS 601 for the honour passing to the prototype [V, CCC 2132 n. 70]. DS 600 for the first part of the *horos* (images to be set up) is [M].
- **The Latin's word.** Anastasius renders *proskynēsis* as *adoratio* ("honoratoriam ... adorationem") and *latreia* as *latria* [V, the two lines above]. So in the Latin tradition "adoration" is the genus, and latria is one kind of it. That is the room Thomas works in.

## Thomas on latria, idolatry and beatitude

Latin from liriocatolico.com.br (Latin with Portuguese and English) [V], and aquinas.cc where noted; English from New Advent, CCEL or drbo (Dominican Fathers, Benziger 1947) [V].

### The image of Christ (III q. 25 a. 3)

- **The question.** "Utrum imago Christi sit adoranda adoratione latriae" [V, search title; liriocatolico].
- **Sed contra.** "Sed contra est quod Damascenus inducit Basilium dicentem, imaginis honor ad prototypum pervenit, idest exemplar" [V]. Thomas and Nicaea rest on the same sentence of Basil.
- **The image as thing.** "imagini Christi inquantum est res quaedam, puta lignum sculptum vel pictum, nulla reverentia exhibetur" [V].
- **One movement.** "motus qui est in imaginem inquantum est imago, est unus et idem cum illo qui est in rem" [V]. English: "the latter movement, which is towards the image as an image, is one and the same as that which is towards the thing" [V, New Advent]. Thomas has it from the Philosopher's "twofold movement of the mind towards an image" (*De memoria* 1) [V].
- **The conclusion.** "Cum igitur Christus adoretur adoratione latriae, consequens est quod eius imago sit adoratione latriae adoranda" [V].
- **No "relative".** Neither "relative" nor "relativus" occurs in q. 25 a. 1 or a. 3, Latin or English [V, by absence on both pages]. "Relative latria" is the school's term, not Thomas's (below).

### Latria due to God alone

- **II-II q. 94 a. 2.** "Sed aliquid speciale debetur summo Deo, qui singulari ratione omnes excellit, et hic est latriae cultus" [V]. This is the clearest text. English: "something special is due to the most high God Who excels all in a singular manner" [V, New Advent].
- **II-II q. 81 a. 1 ad 3.** "a special kind of service is due to Him, which is known as 'latria' in Greek", on the ground of his lordship as creator; the article does not say "alone" [V, CCEL]. The article asks "Whether religion directs man to God alone?" [V].
- **II-II q. 81 a. 3 ad 3.** "The worship of religion is paid to images, not as considered in themselves, nor as things," "but as images leading us to God incarnate" [V, CCEL]. *Correction:* a. 3 asks "Whether religion is one virtue?" [V]; the ad 3 is about images, not latria's reservation.
- **II-II q. 84 a. 1.** "Unde adoratio qua Deus adoratur est religionis actus" [V]. Ad 1: "Deo debetur reverentia propter eius excellentiam"; "alia veneratione quasdam excellentes creaturas, quod pertinet ad duliam" [V]. Adoration is a genus; latria is the adoration of God.

### Idolatry (II-II q. 94)

- **A. 1.** "Et ideo, cuicumque creaturae divinus cultus exhibeatur, superstitiosum est" [V]. English: "it is superstition to give worship to any creature whatsoever" [V, New Advent]. Idolatry is the species of it named there: "Omnia autem haec ad superstitionem idololatriae pertinebant" [V].
- **A. 2.** The sign: "Nam cum exterior cultus sit signum interioris cultus" [V]. The exterior act is typable as an act; the interior faith it signs is not (rule 3).
- **A. 3.** "Et sic peccatum idololatriae est gravissimum" [V].
- **The four titles** (a. 1–4): species of superstition; a sin; the gravest; its cause on the part of man [V, New Advent].
- **Avarice is a likeness only.** II-II q. 118 a. 5 ad 4: the covetous man subjects himself to a creature "by desiring it immoderately for use, not for worship" [V in `mammon.md`, not fetched again].

### Beatitude

- **I-II q. 2 a. 1.** "impossibile est beatitudinem hominis in divitiis consistere" [V; *correction*: "consistere", not "esse"]. Natural riches: "quibus homini subvenitur ad defectus naturales tollendos"; artificial: "quibus secundum se natura non iuvatur, ut denarii" [V].
- **Ad 3.** "appetitus divitiarum artificialium est infinitus, quia deservit concupiscentiae inordinatae"; against it, "summum bonum quanto perfectius possidetur, tanto ipsummet magis amatur" [V].
- **I-II q. 3 a. 8.** "Final and perfect happiness can consist in nothing else than the vision of the Divine Essence"; "for perfect happiness the intellect needs to reach the very Essence of the First Cause" [V, drbo, English only]. The Latin ("ultima et perfecta beatitudo non potest esse nisi in visione divinae essentiae") is [M].
- **Fit with the deposit.** Thomas's I-II q. 3 a. 8 and *Benedictus Deus* name the same object, the divine essence, and Benedict adds "nulla mediante creatura" [V]. The last end has an object that no creature can occupy.

### Thomas's latria for the image and Nicaea's "not true latria"

- **The difference.** Nicaea gives images *timētikē proskynēsis* and refuses them "true latria" [V]. Thomas gives the image of Christ "adoratio latriae" [V]. Read as words, they conflict.
- **The reconciliation in Thomas's own text.** The image "as a thing" receives "nulla reverentia" [V]; the latria is the one movement that terminates in Christ [V]. What Nicaea refuses is latria terminating in the image; what Thomas allows is latria passing through it. Both cite Basil [V]. The CCC reads Nicaea through Thomas's q. 81 a. 3 ad 3 [V].
- **The schools.** St Thomas, with Bonaventure, Albert and Alexander of Hales, held "the worship rendered to the Cross and the image of Christ is that of latria, but with a distinction"; the image is honoured "because of its exemplar" [S: *Catholic Encyclopedia*, "Catholic doctrine on the veneration of the Cross", reposted at earlychristians.org]. The other view, "taught by Bellarmine, by Bossuet, and by Petavius", is "a relative veneration paid to the image ... referring to that which it represents", resting "upon the Second Council of Nicæa"; its adherents wanted "the term 'relative latria', invented by the Thomists" banished as "equivocal and dangerous" [S: same]. Browne's Anglican *Exposition* puts the same split as a dilemma [S: Clark, quoting Browne].
- Cajetan, Vázquez and Suárez on III q. 25 a. 3 were not reached [M that Vázquez held an absolute cult of the image and that Suárez, *De Incarnatione* disp. 54, defends relative latria].

## Spinoza

Latin from the hypertext edition of R. W. Meijer, homepages.uc.edu/~martinj/.../Latin/Ethica.pdf, which holds Pars I only [V; its base edition is not named]. English from Elwes (Gutenberg, pg971 for Part IV) [V] and an unnamed Elwes-like text at marxists.org [V]. Curley was not reached.

- **I p14.** "Praeter Deum nulla dari neque concipi potest substantia" [V].
- **I p15.** "Quicquid est in Deo est, et nihil sine Deo esse neque concipi potest" [V]. English: "Whatsoever is, is in God, and without God nothing can be, or be conceived" [V, marxists.org].
- **I p18.** "Deus est omnium rerum causa immanens, non vero transiens" [V].
- **I p29.** "In rerum natura nullum datur contingens, sed omnia ex necessitate divinae naturae determinata sunt" "ad certo modo existendum et operandum" [V]; the demonstration ends "nullumque datur contingens" [V].
- **I p29 schol.** "per naturam naturantem nobis intelligendum est id quod in se est et per se concipitur"; "Per naturatam autem intelligo id omne quod ex necessitate Dei naturae sive uniuscuiusque Dei attributorum sequitur" [V]. English: "nature viewed as active"; "nature viewed as passive" [V, marxists.org].
- **I p32 cor. 1.** "Hinc sequitur 1. Deum non operari ex libertate voluntatis" [V].
- **I p33.** "Res nullo alio modo, neque alio ordine a Deo produci potuerunt, quam productae sunt" [V].
- **IV praef.** "For the eternal and infinite Being, which we call God or Nature, acts by the same necessity as that whereby it exists"; "The reason or cause why God or Nature exists, and the reason why he acts, are one and the same" [V, Elwes]. The Latin ("Aeternum namque illud, et infinitum Ens, quod Deum, seu Naturam appellamus, eadem, qua existit, necessitate agit"; "Deus, seu Natura") is [M]; the Pars IV Latin was not reached (sacred-texts failed). "Deus, sive Natura" "occurs in the Latin (but not the Dutch) edition" [V, Nadler, SEP "Spinoza", Fall 2002 archive]. The Pars I Appendix does not have it [V, by absence].

### The reception: two readings, and a third

- **Pantheismusstreit.** Jacobi, *Über die Lehre des Spinozas* (1st ed. 1785), his correspondence with Mendelssohn; Lessing "had avowed that he knew no philosophy, in the true sense of that word, save Spinozism"; Jacobi "claimed that Spinoza's doctrine was pure materialism", ending "in absolute atheism" [V, Wikipedia, "Pantheism controversy"]. Goetschel: the publication "significantly shaped the reception of Spinoza's doctrine for centuries" [V, same, reporting]. That Jacobi's book is the source of the *pantheist* label is not said there [V, by absence]; "Hen kai pan" is [M].
- **Reading A, pantheist: God is Nature, naturata included.** Borge (2025) finds in the TTP "compelling evidence supporting the reading of Spinoza as a pantheist": God and nature are "two ways of conceiving one and the same thing" [V, abstract, philarchive SALGON; in Taylor and Wuth (eds.), Edinburgh UP]. The four objections he answers include God active and nature passive, God simple and nature divisible [V, abstract].
- **Reading B, God is *natura naturans*.** Natura naturans is "the active, productive aspect": "Strictly speaking, this is identical with God"; natura naturata is "that which is produced and sustained" by it [V, Nadler, SEP 2002]. The entry says Spinoza "could be read as trying either to divinize nature or to naturalize God" [V]. Okrent: God *qua* naturans immutable, *qua* naturata not [V, *Philosophy & Theology* 11 (1998)]. Curley: either God changes or God "is not a subject of predication" of the modes [S: Okrent]. Curley's own *Spinoza's Metaphysics* (1969) is [M].
- **Reading C, atheist.** Nadler's later book: "Spinoza is not a pantheist" (p. 159); God "numerically identical to the whole of Nature" (reviewer's paraphrase); pantheism "divinizes nature", which Spinoza does not [S: Harrop, *The Philosophers' Magazine*, reviewing Nadler, *Spinoza, Atheist* (Princeton 2026)]. So Nadler has moved from B's wording toward an identity with all of Nature, without worship.

## Adams and his reader

*The Education of Henry Adams*, ch. XXV, "The Dynamo and the Virgin (1900)" [V, Standard Ebooks, chapter-25].

- "As he grew accustomed to the great gallery of machines,"; "he began to feel the forty-foot dynamos as a moral force, much as the early Christians felt the Cross" [V]. The brief's wording is exact.
- "but to Adams the dynamo became a symbol of infinity" [V].
- "Before the end, one began to pray to it; inherited instinct taught the natural expression of man before silent and infinite force" [V]. The brief's wording is exact; the sentence ends there.
- "both energies acted as interchangeable force on man," "and by action on man all known force may be measured" [V].
- **McCarraher, through Nichols.** Industrial technology "was the modern liturgical system of corporate society"; "the sorcery had overwhelmed the sorcerer" (p. 178); the dynamo "an occult mechanism, a graven image of capitalist modernity" (p. 179); "the image and theophany of the Dynamo (an immanent manifestation of Mammon)" (no page) [S: Nichols, *Front Porch Republic*, 9 Dec. 2019, fetched]. The book was not opened, so the page is Nichols's. Nichols calls Adams "a Catholic" [V]; that is the reviewer's error [M: Adams was of Unitarian Boston].
- **What the two give.** Adams's own text has the dynamo prayed to, by instinct, as force, and measured as interchangeable with the Virgin [V]: a conversion of end into force. McCarraher's "graven image" and "theophany" [S] make it an idol: a substitution, and the claimant is McCarraher.

## The schools on the attributes

### Scotus, the baseline (*Ordinatio* I d. 8 p. 1 q. 4)

Latin and English (Simpson) from the Logic Museum, Ordinatio_I/D8/Q4; no critical edition named [V].

- **The question (n. 157).** "Quaero utrum cum simplicitate divina possit stare aliquo modo distinctio perfectionum essentialium" [V]. *Correction:* not "... distinguantur ex natura rei".
- **The answer (n. 191).** "Ad quaestionem respondeo quod inter perfectiones essentiales non est tantum differentia rationis" [V]. *The page's English drops the "non"* ("there is only a difference of reason"); the Latin is the reverse [V]. Do not quote the Logic Museum English for n. 191.
- **N. 192.** "Est ergo ibi distinctio praecedens intellectum omni modo, et est ista, quod sapientia est in re ex natura rei" [V].
- **N. 193.** "est igitur aliqua non identitas formalis sapientiae et bonitatis"; "'includere formaliter' est includere aliquid in ratione sua essentiali" [V].
- **Real identity by infinity (n. 212).** "sicut non esset bonitas realiter infinita nisi esset realiter eadem sapientiae" [V].

### Suárez

Latin from M. Renemann's edition of the *Disputationes metaphysicae* (homepage.ruhr-uni-bochum.de/Michael.Renemann/suarez/, d07, d30, d31; links from the Logic Museum index) [V].

- **DM 7.1, the kinds of distinction.** Title: "Utrum praeter distinctionem realem et rationis sit aliqua alia distinctio in rebus" [V]. The distinction of reason "quae habet fundamentum in re et a multis vocatur rationis ratiocinatae" (n. 4); the example: "quomodo distinguimus in Deo unum attributum ab alio" (n. 4); "Sic distinguimus in Deo iustitiam a misericordia" (n. 5) [V].
- **DM 7.1.13, Scotus.** The formal distinction "communiter tribuitur Scoto, In I, dist. 2, q. 7 ... et dist. 8, q. 4"; some think it "non esse aliam a distinctione rationis ratiocinatae", and so Scotus seems to hold "praesertim quando agit de attributis divinis"; other Scotists find in it a distinction "in Deo ... saltem inter relationes divinas et essentiam" [V].
- **DM 7.1.16, his own view.** "censeo simpliciter verum esse dari in rebus creatis aliquam distinctionem actualem et ex natura rei"; but "Nomen autem distinctionis formalis non ita mihi placet, quia est valde aequivocum": he calls it modal [V]. In the Trinity, of paternity and filiation, a mode of distinction "in obiectivis rationibus relationum" that is not found outside that mystery [V]. DM 7.2.5 leaves the essence–relation question "theologis" and calls the Trinity a mystery "quod sola ac certissima fide tenemus" [V].
- **DM 30.3–4, simplicity.** Sect. III: "An demonstrari possit Deum esse actum purum et ens omnino simplex" [V]. "quidam etiam catholici distinctionem formalem seu ex natura rei inter divinas perfectiones excogitarunt" (n. 4); the reply: it is evident that the most perfect being cannot "ex adunatione plurium perfectionem seu rerum aut partium distinctarum coalescat" (n. 5) [V]. Sect. IV: God is "essentialiter suum esse subsistens" (n. 3) [V].
- **Where DM 30 treats the distinction of the attributes.** Not found. The fetched file holds sections I–IV (perfect, infinite, simple, no substantial composition) and ends in IV [V]; no section title there treats the distinction, and the attributes appear only as above. DM 29.3.1 says "basta la distinción de razón de los atributos" for a priori proof [S: Poncela, *Azafea* 18 (2016)]. The full treatment is in *De Deo uno* (*De divina substantia*), lib. I, cap. 10–13 [M].
- **DM 31.1, essence and esse.** Title: "An esse et essentia entis creati distinguantur in re" [V]. The Thomists' real distinction (n. 3, Capreolus, Cajetan, Aegidius); Scotus's "ex natura rei, seu (ut alii loquuntur) formaliter" (n. 11, "In III, dist. 6, q. 1"); his own: "Tertia sententia ponens solam distinctionem rationis" (n. 12), "Et hanc sententiam sic explicatam existimo esse omnino veram" (n. 13) [V]. "Cum fundamento in re" was not found in DM 31.1 [V, by absence in what was returned].
- **The runtime.** The texts support `attributes_by_reason` (DM 7.1.4–5), a Thomist simplicity that refuses the formal distinction among the perfections and composition of essence and esse in God (DM 30.3.4–5, 30.4.3), and `suarez_esse` (DM 31.1.12–13) [V]. Two cautions: Suárez admits an actual, modal distinction *ex natura rei* in creatures (DM 7.1.16), so he is not a "by reason everywhere" reader; and for the Trinity he allows a distinction of the relations' "obiectivae rationes" found nowhere else [V].

### Ockham (*Ordinatio* I d. 2 q. 1)

No Latin of Ockham was reached. The wording is Kilcullen's English of OTh II [S: J. Kilcullen, "Ockham's Philosophical Theology", British Academy *Dialogus* project, publications.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/pubs/dialogus/PhTh.pdf].

- **The question.** Whether the identity of the divine essence "with the attributal perfections, and of those perfections with one another, is as great as the identity of the divine essence with the divine essence"; Ockham answers yes (OTh II p. 3) [S: Kilcullen].
- **No formal distinction of the attributes.** A formal distinction "should not be posited except where it evidently follows from beliefs handed down in sacred Scripture" or "the determination of the Church"; these "can be saved without positing it" between "the [divine] essence and the [divine] wisdom, therefore I simply deny that such a distinction is possible there, and I deny it universally in creatures" (OTh II pp. 17–18) [S: Kilcullen].
- **One formal distinction in God.** "Because one [viz. a formal distinction in God] is expressed in Scripture and the other [a formal distinction in creatures] is not"; "therefore the one is to be posited and the other denied" [S: Kilcullen]. Kilcullen's text does not name the pair. That it is the essence and the persons: "La esencia y las personas son sólo formalmente distintas" [S: Larre de González, *Scripta* 2 (2009), no note]; "las personas divinas se constituyen y distinguen por las relaciones de origen" (OTh II 157) [S: same].
- **The attributes are terms.** "The attributes are terms. What each of these terms stands for is in no way distinct from God himself" [S: Kilcullen]. An older history: the attributes "are merely different names arbitrarily applied by us to the same identical reality", and "the virtual distinction of the Thomists has no more real foundation for it than the formal distinction of the Scotists" [S: nd.edu Maritain Center etext homp374; author not shown].
- **What he holds, exactly.** In God: real identity of essence and attributes, as great as the essence's with itself; no formal distinction of the attributes, either from the essence or among themselves; one formal distinction, essence and persons (relations), admitted on the authority of faith alone. The attributes differ only as concepts or names, with no distinction on God's side, not even the Thomist foundation. In creatures: no formal distinction at all [S, the four items above]. Whether Ockham calls the attribute-concepts non-synonymous (connotative) is [M].
- **Against Thomas on the relations.** Thomas: "relatio realiter existens in Deo, est idem essentiae secundum rem; et non differt nisi secundum intelligentiae rationem" (I q. 28 a. 2) [V, aquinas.cc]; English "only differs in its mode of intelligibility" [V, New Advent]. And the names: "The many aspects of these names are not empty and vain, for there corresponds to all of them one simple reality"; "These names spoken of God are not synonymous" (I q. 13 a. 4 ad 2 and co.) [V, CCEL, English]. So Thomas has no formal distinction anywhere in God, and a distinction of reason in the attributes with a foundation; Ockham has a formal distinction in God (at the relations) and attributes without that foundation. His allele is the brief's combination, and sharper: Scotist at the persons, more than Thomist at the attributes.

### Hobbes (*Leviathan* 31)

English of 1651, Project Gutenberg 3207, as served by bookrags.com/ebooks/3207 [V].

- **The attributes as honour.** In the attributes we give to God "we are not to consider the signification of Philosophicall Truth; but the signification of Pious Intention", the sentence continuing "to do him the greatest Honour we are able" [V].
- **What he holds.** The attributes declare how we would honour God, not what he is. That is no school's reading of their distinction: neither the Thomist distinction of reason with a foundation in God, nor the Scotist formal distinction.

### Bearings on the encoding

1. **The last end.** *Benedictus Deus* makes the object of beatitude the divine essence, seen "nulla mediante creatura" [V]. Encode the last end by its object, not by its place in an order: a creature entered as the object of the last end is the substitution, whatever ranks it holds.
2. **Latria.** Enter latria by what it terminates in, not what it is directed to. The honour that passes to the prototype terminates in God (Nicaea, Thomas, CCC); latria terminating in a creature as a thing ("res quaedam", "nulla reverentia") is idolatry (II-II q. 94 a. 1, 2). Then Thomas's latria for the image and Nicaea's refusal both pass, and "relative latria" stays a school term with its claimant (the Thomists), not Thomas's.
3. **Spinoza.** I p14, p18, p29, p32 cor. 1 and p33 fire canons 1.3–1.5 (DH 3023–3025) on every reading [V for the texts]. The substitution fires only on reading A (Jacobi's line; Borge): nature (naturata) in God's place. On B it is identity and necessity, not substitution; on C there is no worship, so neither latria nor idolatry types. Enter "Spinoza puts a creature in God's place" as the claimant's claim and run A and B.
4. **Ockham's allele** is a mixed one: a formal distinction in God (essence and persons, on faith) with attributes as names only. It is neither `simplicity_scotist` nor `simplicity_thomist` plus `attributes_by_reason`; the model's mixed-carrier rule would mark it, and it may need its own allele (attributes without foundation). **Suárez's** three are supported, with the modal distinction kept off the divine locus.
