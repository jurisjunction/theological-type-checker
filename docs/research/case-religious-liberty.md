### Leo X, *Exsurge Domine* (1520), prop. 33

- **The proposition.** "That heretics be burned is against the will of the Spirit" [V, EWTN; worldhistory.org]; "Haereticos comburi, est contra voluntatem Spiritus" [S: Wikipedia, its footnote]. DS 1483 [S: Hillerbrand].
- **The censure, in globo.** The notes are given together: "we condemn, reprobate, and reject completely each of these theses or errors as either heretical, scandalous, false, offensive to pious ears or seductive of simple minds, and against Catholic truth" [V, EWTN]. The authority clause: "by the authority of almighty God, the blessed Apostles Peter and Paul, and our own authority". The addressees: "all the faithful of both sexes must regard them as condemned, reprobated, and rejected" [V, EWTN]. Hillerbrand gives the Latin as "respective haereticos aut scandalosos aut falsos" and notes that the bull gave no "explicit verdict" on which sentences were heretical [S: Hillerbrand, "Martin Luther and the Bull *Exsurge Domine*", *Theological Studies* 30 (1969) 108–112; volume and year M]. Luther made the same complaint: "We don't know which are which" [S: Wikipedia, quoting him]. Akin: the propositions were condemned *in globo*, not *in individuo*, and the text does not show which censure applies to which [V, Akin, *Catholic Answers*, 8 Aug. 2022].
- **Whether prop. 33 is Luther's.** Hillerbrand: the bull "forged one sentence out of two disjointed ones in Luther", who cited Jerome as his guide (WA 1, 624) [S: Hillerbrand]. The censure falls on the proposition as the bull words it.
- **Its force.** Cleenewerck holds that the condemnations "technically satisfy the requirements" of an ex cathedra definition [S: Wikipedia]. Akin denies it, by c. 749 §3 [V]. Harrison holds that a censure of unspecified nature is "potentially subject to future clarification or reform" [S: Wikipedia, quoting him; his text not reached].
- **Rule 14.** The text shows apostolic authority (God's, the Apostles', "our own") and the whole Church ("all the faithful") [V]. Whether it defines is the claimants' inference. Even if it defines, an unassigned note fixes no grade for any one proposition, which is the roadmap's reading [V, repo]. The checker's form fits: `ambiguous(exsurge_33)` with one reading per note. Under "heretical" or "false" it supports the contradictory on its question. Under "scandalous", "offensive to pious ears" or "seductive of simple minds" it fixes no value, since those notes censure the assertion's effect, not its truth [inference from the notes' definitions, M]. The repo has no slot for the lesser notes [V, repo: `censures_as_heresy/3` and `censures_in_faith/3`, in `empirica.l`, are the only censures].

### Trent, session VII, canons on baptism, canon 14 (1547)

- "CANON XIV.-If any one saith, that those who have been thus baptized when children, are, when they have grown up, to be asked whether they will ratify what their sponsors promised in their names when they were baptized; and that, in case they answer that they will not, they are to be left to their own will; and are not to be compelled meanwhile to a Christian life by any other penalty, save that they be excluded from the participation of the Eucharist, and of the other sacraments, until they repent; let him be anathema" [V, Hanover College's text; the translator is not named there, Waterworth M]. DS 1627 (Dz 870) [V, clerus.org].
- The Latin clause, as Pink gives it: "suo esse arbitrio relinquendos nec alia interim poena ad christianam vitam cogendos" [S: Pink 2013, p. 97].
- **What it fixes.** The condemned proposition holds that the baptized who refuse to ratify may be compelled by no penalty beyond exclusion from the sacraments. Its contradictory is that some other penalty may compel them. It says nothing of who applies the penalty, or whether the state may. Pink's reading of DH rests on it (below).

### Thomas

- **Unbelievers and heretics (II-II q. 10 a. 8).** Those who have never received the faith "are by no means to be compelled to the faith", because "to believe depends on the will". Heretics and apostates "should be submitted even to bodily compulsion, that they may fulfil what they have promised" [V, New Advent]. Ad 3: "acceptance of the faith is a matter of the will, whereas keeping the faith, when once one has received it, is a matter of obligation" [V]. So Thomas has Trent's line: the baptized are under the Church; the unbaptized are not.
- **Tolerance (a. 11).** Unbelievers' rites "may be tolerated, either on account of some good that ensues therefrom, or because of some evil avoided" [V].
- **The secular arm (q. 11 a. 3).** Heretics "deserve not only to be separated from the Church by excommunication, but also to be severed from the world by death". After the second admonition the Church "delivers him to the secular tribunal to be exterminated thereby from the world by death" [V, New Advent]. This is what *Exsurge* 33 was read against, and the practice that Pink's and Rhonheimer's readings of DH concern.

### Gregory XVI, *Mirari vos* (1832)

- Indifferentism: that "qualibet fidei professione aeternam posse animae salutem comparari, si mores ad recti honestique normam exigantur" [V, vatican.va, Latin text on an English page].
- Liberty of conscience: the opinion that it must be asserted and defended for everyone is "absurda illa ... sententia, seu potius deliramentum" [V, the two phrases]. Augustine: "At quae peior mors animae quam libertas erroris!" [V].
- Separation: those who "Ecclesiam a regno separari mutuamque imperii cum sacerdotio concordiam abrumpi discupiunt" [V].
- The occasion was Lamennais's *L'Avenir*, criticized "without mentioning him by name" [S: Wikipedia]; *Singulari nos* followed on 25 June 1834 [S: Wikipedia].
- **Typing.** The argument is from indifferentism: liberty of conscience follows from holding every profession saving. Its premise is the truth and the end of religion (final, god_ethics), which folds the supernatural end into God's ends (A supernatural domain?).

### Pius IX, *Quanta cura* and the Syllabus (1864)

- **The condemned opinion (n. 3).** Some hold "that is the best condition of civil society" in which no duty is recognized to restrain offenders against the Catholic religion "except so far as public peace may require" [V, EWTN English]. From it they draw an opinion "called by Our Predecessor, Gregory XVI, an 'insanity,'": "liberty of conscience and worship is each man's personal right", which "ought to be legally proclaimed and asserted in every rightly constituted society" [V, EWTN]. The Italian: "la libertà di coscienza e dei culti essere un diritto proprio di ciascun uomo" [V, vatican.va]. They preach "liberty of perdition", after Augustine [V, EWTN]. The Latin "libertatem conscientiae et cultuum esse proprium cuiuscumque hominis ius" is [M]: vatican.va's Latin was not reached, and the Italian page holds only the Italian.
- **The formula (n. 6).** "by our Apostolic authority, we reprobate, proscribe, and condemn all the singular and evil opinions ... and will and command that they be thoroughly held by all children of the Catholic Church ... as reprobated, proscribed and condemned" [V, EWTN]. Italian: "con la Nostra autorità apostolica riproviamo, proscriviamo e condanniamo" [V].
- **The claims about its force.**
  - Joy, *OnePeterFive*, 18 Nov. 2022: "the pope indeed speaks infallibly in *Quanta Cura*" [V]. His grounds are three: the apostolic authority invoked, the matter, and the formula requiring all to hold the opinions condemned [V, paraphrase]. He reports that Billot lists *Quanta cura* among the ex cathedra definitions (*Tractatus de Ecclesia Christi*) [S: Joy], and that Sullivan and Miller deny it [S: Joy].
  - Lefebvre, *Dubia* (preface dated 22 May 1987): the condemnation "semble bien réunir les quatre 'notes' d'un document 'ex cathedra'", and is "très probablement infailliblement" made [V, laportelatine.org].
  - King and Miller, *Public Discourse*, 2 Feb. 2019: "we cannot conclude that Quanta cura contains infallible teaching" [V]; Pius IX attached no traditional censure [V, paraphrase].
  - Newman, 1875, calls "the Encyclical of December, 1864" "a document of dogmatic authority" [V, newmanreader.org, §7]. He does not call it a definition [V, by absence in §7].
  - Most claims that the teaching of the three popes, given repeatedly in the ordinary magisterium, "seems" infallible [V, paraphrase: EWTN, undated]. That is a claim of the ordinary universal magisterium (`oum_claim`), not of a definition, and LG 25's conditions are not shown by repetition in one see's acts [inference].
  - Choupin's *Valeur des décisions doctrinales* (2nd ed., imprimatur 12 July 1912) was opened. The part reached does not reach *Quanta cura* [V, by absence in the first 100,000 characters].
- **Rule 14.** The repo applies *Pastor aeternus*'s conditions to papal acts before 1870 (`ineffabilis_1854` with all five `condition_shown`) [V, repo: `icon_history.l` l. 112–115]. The text of *Quanta cura* shows `apostolic_authority` and `whole_church` [V]. `defines` is shown only by the claimants' reading of the formula: there is no "definimus", and the act condemns opinions. So the entry is `source(quanta_cura,3,truth,1864)` with `claims_tier(joy_2022,quanta_cura,1)`, `claims_tier(lefebvre_1985,quanta_cura,1)` and Billot's. Each gives `lower_speaks_of_higher`. Treated as fixing, each gives `self_elevation`. The branch resting on them is never preferred (c. 749 §3) [V, repo: the rules].
- **The Syllabus.** Its heading: "The Syllabus of the principal errors of our time, which are stigmatized in the Consistorial Allocutions, Encyclicals, and other Apostolical Letters of our Most Holy Lord, Pope Pius IX" [V, Schaff, Latin and English]. The propositions that bear on the case [V, Schaff]:
  - 15: "Liberum cuique homini est eam amplecti ac profiteri religionem, quam rationis lumine quis ductus veram putaverit."
  - 55: "Ecclesia a Statu, Statusque ab Ecclesia sejungendus est."
  - 77: "Ætate hac nostra non amplius expedit, religionem Catholicam haberi tamquam unicam Status religionem, ceteris quibuscumque cultibus exclusis."
  - 78: "Hinc laudabiliter in quibusdam Catholici nominis regionibus lege cautum est, ut hominibus illuc immigrantibus liceat publicum proprii cujusque cultus exercitium habere."
  - 79: it is false that the civil liberty of every worship, with full power to manifest any opinions, conduces "ad populorum mores animosque facilius corrumpendos ac indifferentismi pestem propogandam" [*sic*].
  - 80: "Romanus Pontifex potest ac debet cum progressu, cum liberalismo et cum recenti civilitate sese reconciliare et componere."

  §X is headed "Errores qui ad liberalismum hodiernum referuntur" [V]. Their sources [V, the Indiana University edition]: 15, the allocution *Maxima quidem* (9 June 1862) and *Multiplices inter* (10 June 1851); 55 and 78, *Acerbissimum* (27 Sept. 1852); 77, *Nemo vestrum* (26 July 1855); 79, *Nunquam fore* (15 Dec. 1856); 80, *Jamdudum cernimus* (18 March 1861).
- **Its force.** Newman: "the Syllabus then has no dogmatic force"; "The Syllabus is not an official act, because it is not signed"; it is received "by an act of obedience, not of faith"; "The value of the Syllabus, then, lies in its references"; it was sent "by his Foreign Minister", Antonelli [V, §7]. So each proposition enters at the tier of its source, an allocution or a letter at 3, and the Syllabus is a witness to them, not an act above them.
- **Two of its propositions are directives.** 77 and 78 speak of expediency in "this our age" and of a law "laudably" made in some countries [V]. In CCC 2423's terms, which the repo types (`teaching_type(S, directive)`; `directive_as_principle`, `moral.l`), they are judgments of circumstances [V, repo]. Read as fixing a kind, they are refused, as EV 56's rarity is in SD4 [V, repo]. Benedict XVI's "decisions on contingent matters" (below) is this distinction.

### Leo XIII

- ***Immortale Dei* (1885).**
  - n. 6: "Nature and reason, which command every individual devoutly to worship God in holiness", "bind also the civil community by a like law"; "it is a public crime to act as though there were no God" [V, vatican.va]. Civil society is "not in any way to hinder, but in every manner to render as easy as may be", "the possession of that highest and unchangeable good for which all should seek" [V].
  - n. 10: the Church "is supernatural and spiritual, on account of the end for which it was founded" [V].
  - n. 13: "Each in its kind is supreme, each has fixed limits within which it is contained" [V].
  - n. 26 rejects "that every one is to be free to follow whatever religion he prefers, or none at all" [V]; n. 34 cites *Mirari vos*, Pius IX and the Syllabus [V].
  - n. 36: the Church "deems it unlawful to place the various forms of divine worship on the same footing as the true religion", yet "does not, on that account, condemn those rulers who, for the sake of securing some great good", tolerate them [V]. She takes "earnest heed that no one shall be forced to embrace the Catholic faith against his will", for "Man cannot believe otherwise than of his own will" (Augustine) [V].
- ***Libertas* (1888).**
  - n. 5: liberty "is the faculty of choosing means fitted for the end proposed", and its object is "that good only which is in conformity with reason" [V].
  - n. 6: "the possibility of sinning is not freedom, but slavery" [V].
  - n. 19: liberty of worship is "so opposed to the virtue of religion"; n. 21: "that religion must be professed which alone is true" [V].
  - n. 30: in its true sense, liberty of conscience is the freedom to follow the will of God and obey His commands, "free from every obstacle" [V, phrase].
  - nn. 33–34: tolerance "for the sake of avoiding some greater evil, or of obtaining or preserving some greater good", "for the common good (and this is the only legitimate reason)", "strictly confined to the limits which its justifying cause, the public welfare, requires" [V].
  - n. 42: it is not lawful "to demand, to defend, or to grant unconditional freedom of thought, of speech, or writing, or of worship, as if these were so many rights given by nature to man" [V].

  The word "unconditional" is the textual hook of every continuity reading (below).
- ***Longinqua oceani* (1895), n. 6.** The Church in America, "unopposed by the Constitution and government of your nation", "is free to live and act without hindrance". But "it would be very erroneous to draw the conclusion that in America is to be sought the type of the most desirable status of the Church, or that it would be universally lawful or expedient for State and Church to be, as in America, dissevered and divorced". She "would bring forth more abundant fruits if, in addition to liberty, she enjoyed the favor of the laws and the patronage of the public authority" [V, vatican.va]. The first half denies a universal principle; the second is a judgment of circumstances (a directive) [inference].
- ***Testem benevolentiae* (1899).** The root of the new opinions: the Church should draw near to the age, "Id autem non de vivendi solum disciplina, sed de doctrinis etiam intelligendum esse multi arbitrantur" [V, vatican.va Latin]. Some want "libertatem quamdam in Ecclesiam esse inducendam", on the model of civil liberty [V]. Some exalt natural virtues "praeter modum"; a "passive" virtue "nec est nec esse potest" [V, the phrases]. On the name: if "Americanism" means the qualities of the American people and their institutions, "non est profecto cur ipsum reiiciendum censeamus". If it commends the opinions, the American bishops would be the first "repudiaturi ac damnaturi" [V]. The occasion was the French translation of the *Life* of Isaac Hecker, with Klein's preface [S: Wikipedia; Pallen, *Catholic Encyclopedia* 1912]. "Americanism" is thus the letter's own label, and a conditional one.

### Pius XII, *Ci riesce* (1953)

- First principle: what does not correspond to truth or to the norm of morality "objectively has no right to exist", to be spread or activated [V, EWTN, the Vatican Press Office's English]. Second: failure to impede it "with civil laws and coercive measures can nevertheless be justified" by a higher and more general good [V, phrase]. The duty of repressing moral and religious error "cannot therefore be an ultimate norm of action" [V]. On concordats, toleration "may also mean a simple tolerance" [V].
- In the case: on tolerance as a governance question, Pius XII's first principle answers the question of right, no, and his second the question of policy [inference].

### Vatican II, *Dignitatis humanae* (1965)

- **DH 1.** The one true religion "subsists in the Catholic and Apostolic Church" [V]. The Council "leaves untouched traditional Catholic doctrine on the moral duty of men and societies" toward the true religion and the one Church of Christ [V]. It means "to develop the doctrine of recent popes" [V, phrase].
- **DH 2.** Men are "to be immune from coercion" by individuals, social groups and "any human power" [V, phrases], "within due limits" [V]. The right is grounded in the dignity of the person, "known through the revealed word of God and by reason itself" [V], and is "to be recognized in the constitutional law" [V].
- **DH 3.** "the highest norm of human life is the divine law" [V]. Government "would clearly transgress the limits set to its power", "were it to presume to command or inhibit acts that are religious" [V]. The Latin "Potestas ... civilis, cuius finis proprius est bonum commune temporale curare" is [M]. Pink paraphrases DH 3: the state lacks authority because religion "transcends its temporal ends" [V, Pink 2012, paraphrase].
- **DH 7.** The limits are the basic component of the common welfare, and "they are what is meant by public order" [V]. That they are "in conformity with the objective moral order" is [M]; the unsigned study of 1987 uses the phrase [V].
- **DH 10.** "The act of faith is of its very nature a free act" [V].
- **DH 12.** At times a way of acting appeared "hardly in accord with the spirit of the Gospel or even opposed to it" [V].
- **DH 13.** "The freedom of the Church is the fundamental principle" in relations between the Church and governments [V]. An amendment to read "fundamental condition" was rejected [S: Murray 1966].
- **The tier.** The notification of 16 November 1964: in faith and morals the Council defines as binding only what "it shall openly declare to be binding" [V, IntraText]. DH declares a right and defines nothing, so it is tier 3, as `sspx` enters it (`teaching_tier(dignitatis_humanae,3)`) [V, repo]. SX8 refuses a classification of schism on dissent from it (c. 752, not c. 751) [V, repo: `docs/cases/sspx-2026.md`]. The vote on the section on establishment was 2,034 to 186 on 26 Oct. 1965 [S: Murray 1966]. The final vote, 2,308 to 70, is [M].
- **The text's own word for itself.** "doctrine of religious freedom" (DH 9) is [M]; DH 1's "develop the doctrine" is [V]. So by its own manner DH speaks as teaching, not as policy. Pink's and Harrison's readings of it as policy are readings against that manner (below).

### Benedict XVI, address to the Roman Curia (22 Dec. 2005)

- He sets "a hermeneutic of discontinuity and rupture" against the "hermeneutic of reform", "of renewal in the continuity of the one subject-Church" [V, vatican.va]. ## The figures outside the Church's texts

- **Locke, *A Letter Concerning Toleration* (1689).** The commonwealth is "constituted only for the procuring, preserving, and advancing their own civil interests", which are "life, liberty, health, and indolency of body" and "the possession of outward things". "the care of souls is not committed to the civil magistrate, any more than to other men". "true and saving religion consists in the inward persuasion of the mind", and "such is the nature of the understanding, that it cannot be compelled". A church is "a voluntary society of men". "The care, therefore, of every man's soul belongs unto himself." [V, let.rug.nl; translator not named there, Popple M]. There are two exceptions: those who "deliver themselves up to the protection and service of another prince", and atheists, since "Promises, covenants, and oaths ... can have no hold upon an atheist" [V]. The page does not name Catholics in the first [V, by absence].
- **Madison, *Memorial and Remonstrance* (c. 20 June 1785).** "Religion or the duty which we owe to our Creator and the manner of discharging it, can be directed only by reason and conviction, not by force or violence" (§1). "This duty is precedent, both in order of time and in degree of obligation, to the claims of Civil Society." "in matters of Religion, no mans right is abridged by the institution of Civil Society and ... Religion is wholly exempt from its cognizance". "This right is in its nature an unalienable right", since "the opinions of men, depending only on the evidence contemplated by their own minds cannot follow the dictates of other men". The claim that "the Civil Magistrate is a competent Judge of Religious Truth" is an "arrogant pretension" (§5) [V, founders.archives.gov].
- **Rousseau, *Social Contract* IV.8 (Cole).** The sovereign fixes a "purely civil profession of faith", its articles "not exactly as religious dogmas, but as social sentiments". Whoever does not believe them may be banished, "not for impiety, but as an antisocial being". The positive dogmas begin with "The existence of a mighty, intelligent and beneficent Divinity, possessed of foresight and providence", the life to come, the reward of the just and the punishment of the wicked. The negative dogma is one, intolerance: "whoever dares to say" there is no salvation outside the Church "ought to be driven from the State", unless the State is the Church. The religion of the citizen counts "service done to the State" as "service done to its tutelary god". "The third", the religion of the priest, the Roman, "is so clearly bad". "Christianity preaches only servitude and dependence." [V, Standard Ebooks]. The text calling country "the object of the citizens' adoration" was returned only in paraphrase [V, paraphrase].
- **Hobbes.** That a church is the same thing as a Christian commonwealth under its sovereign (*Lev.* 39) is [M]. Hobbes is a node of the study [V, repo]; *Lev.* 39 is not among its loci [V, repo: THE TEXTS].

### The crux in the texts

*Quanta cura* condemns the view that the civil power need restrain offenders against the Catholic religion only "so far as public peace may require" [V]. DH 7 sets the limit of religious freedom at "public order" [V]. On the one-question reading the two criteria are the same. On the two-question readings, public order is wider than public peace: Murray's "threefold aspect", political, moral, juridical [V]; the 1987 study's "objective moral order" [V]; and, for Harrison, the common good wider still [V, paraphrase]. The second qualifier is *Libertas* 42's "unconditional ... as if these were so many rights given by nature" [V], against DH 2's "within due limits" [V]. The identification of the two criteria, and its denial, are readings [inference]. The texts give both sides their words.

## A supernatural domain?

### What the case's arguments rest on that no part types

- Leo's "that highest and unchangeable good" and the Church "supernatural and spiritual, on account of the end for which it was founded" (ID 6, 10) [V].
- Connell's temporal that is "not identical with the natural" [V], and Fenton's Beatific Vision through the Church [V].
- DH 1's "one Church of Christ" and DH 13's freedom of the Church [V].
- Trent's canon 14, and Pink's "jurisdiction over the baptized" [V].
- *Testem*'s natural against supernatural virtues [V], and McCarraher's sacramental, the row of `modernity-mammon.md` that waits on this record [V, repo].

In the study each folds into (final, god_ethics), "God's ends for human use and action" [V, repo], which Locke holds by his own `add` [V, repo]. So typed now, Leo's argument from the supernatural end reaches Locke, whose conclusion is the contrary. The fold loses exactly the line the case turns on.

### A proposal

- A domain `grace`, with two parts. `ends_of_grace` is (final, grace): the end above nature, beatitude, the salvation of souls. `church_as_society` is (formal, grace): the Church constituted by Christ as a society with its own authority, "Each in its kind ... supreme" (ID 13) [V].
- The second cannot be an efficient cause, though the Church's authority is Christ's act. The library holds efficient and material causes "in every domain by the library itself: no horizon traces them" [V, repo: THE DESIGN; `causae.l`, `holds_cause((efficient;material),D)`]. So an authority typed as efficient could never be traced, and Locke's "voluntary society" could not be told from Leo's "perfect society". The Church's constitution has to be a form.
- `default` would add both, so every existing school keeps them, as with the nine (SU2's pattern) [V, repo]. `locke` would keep `ends_of_grace` and exclude `church_as_society` [V, the Letter]. Hobbes's and Spinoza's nodes would exclude both, by the reading of "every part but" as the parts each inherits [V, repo: SCORING, Readings], unless a record of their texts on the Church says otherwise [M].
- **What it would do.** It would part Locke from the Catholics a second way. It would let Madison's and Rousseau's natural religion be told from the Gospel, if their nodes exclude `ends_of_grace`. It would give mammon's sacramental row a part to trace.
- **What it would not do.** It would not part any Catholic from another, since all hold both parts. It would not type the state's duty toward the supernatural end, which is a relation of the state's end to the Church's: the object inside causes both sides hold, again.

### The verdict on the lost conclusion

- "TTC-MODERNITY has no supernatural domain": held [V, repo].
- "so the case's decisive difference (the Church's supernatural end and the state's duty toward it) cannot yet be typed in the cause library": held in part. The Church's end and constitution cannot be typed now; a domain of two parts would type them. The state's duty toward them cannot be typed by any part, present or proposed. It is a dispute about an object inside causes the disputants share, and so a question for the rebuttal slot and the authority layer, not for the horizons.
- "the Catholic positions all fall in the Thomist group": held, by construction. A supernatural domain would leave it so.

## What it does not settle
