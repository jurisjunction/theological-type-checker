# TTC-RERUM(7): sources

## NAME

**rerum-sources** — the evidence record for TTC-RERUM: the deposit on creation and on faith and reason, Thomas on forms, ideas and being, Aristotle on causes and parts, the later authors, and the chemistry of soap.

## DESCRIPTION

Source verification only, compiled 29 October 2026. It designs no rules and is an evidence record, so it keeps prose (rule 10). Each item gives the claim, the locus, a short quotation exactly as the fetch tool returned it, the URL, a status and anything still owed. Quotations are under 40 words.

Every check went through the fetch tool. Several hosts refused URLs that had not come back from a search (see NOT REACHED), so some items rest on a second copy of the text or a secondary source. Where that happens the item says so.

## STATUS

- **verified**: the wording and locus were checked against a page fetched in this pass.
- **verified with correction**: checked, but the wording or locus in the brief was wrong. The correction is stated.
- **owed**: not confirmed online. The item names the print source that would settle it.
- **Owed:** inside a verified item marks a sub-point (a Latin original, a line number) that was not reached.

## CAVEATS

- **DH and DS numbers.** The pages give DS numbers. That DH keeps the DS numbering for Vatican I and the creeds cited here is [M].
- **The clerus.org English Denzinger.** It agrees with DS through 3036. It numbers the canons *De fide et ratione* 3037–3039 (Dz 1816–1818), against *Mysterium Ecclesiae*'s DS 3043 for canon 3. Do not take those numbers from it.
- **The Observatory PDF of Dei Filius.** Its Latin dates the constitution "die decima octava lulii" against the English's 24 April. That is a fault of the page, not of the text cited.
- **Second-hand quotations.** A quotation reached through a secondary or medieval source (D1 Latin, D2 Latin, D3, D5, D16) is that source's text, not a critical edition's.

## CORRECTIONS

1. **B2, ST I q. 15 a. 2.** "Distinct by reason" ("secundum rationem") is not in the article as returned. Thomas has "plures ideae sunt in mente divina ut intellectae ab ipso", and (ad 4) the relations are "respectus intellecti a Deo", not real relations.
2. **D2, Augustine, De Trin. VI.10.11.** The Latin is "plena omnium rationum viventium" (with "omnium"), reached only through Auriol's quotation. The §11 numbering was not shown.
3. **D5, Scotus.** "Ordinatio II d. 12" is not in the Vatican edition. Cite *Opus Oxoniense* II d. 12 q. 1 (Vivès 12:557), or *Lectura* II d. 12 q. un. What is attested, through Wolter and Gordon, is that matter would have "its own proper existence" and that God could create matter existing without form.
4. **D6, Suárez, DM 13.** The locus is s. 4 (title; n. 13, matter's "entitatem seu actualitatem existentiae distinctam ab existentia formae"). The phrase "actum entitativum" is at s. 5 n. 2, where Suárez reports it as the view of Scotus, Henry and others. Matter existing without form by divine power was not found in ss. 1–5.
5. **D14, Quodlibet III q. 4 a. 1 ad 3.** The Latin is genitive, "magisterii cathedrae pastoralis" and "magisterii cathedrae magistralis" (in "pericula magisterii …"), not "magisterium cathedrae …".
