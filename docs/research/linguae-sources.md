# One word, many encodings: the evidence record for TTC-LINGUAE

This is the research record behind the predictions of TTC-LINGUAE, compiled on 23 October 2026. It is an evidence record, so it keeps prose (rule 10). The step comes before the senses and is scoped to written encodings: a written form is entered as given. Its purpose is translation bridges for the VM of TTC-MODERNITY, which waits on it (CAVEATS, "No tongue is the word").

References to `modernity-hearers.md` and `cases-usury-lying.md` are to evidence records of TTC-MODERNITY, which waits on this step and is committed with its own predictions.

**How sure each item is:**
- **[V]** The wording was checked against a primary text or translation fetched for this record. For a secondary work, [V] means its own wording was fetched.
- **[S]** It was checked through a named secondary source that quotes or reports the primary.
- **[M]** It comes from memory or a standard citation and was not re-checked. It is unverified until someone checks it in print.

Quotations are under 25 words. They came through a fetch tool that returns short extracts, so a quotation is exact as returned, not proof-read against a printed page. A translation's translator is named where the page names one. "Proposal" marks this record's own design, not a source's.

## The word and the encodings

### The inner word, of no tongue (Augustine)

- The word thought in the heart is one "quod nec Graecum est nec Latinum nec linguae alicuius alterius" (*De Trinitate* XV.10.19) [V, BKV Latin]. NPNF: "neither Greek nor Latin, nor of any other tongue" [V].
- Spoken words are signs of it: "cum ipsae voces in sermone nostro earum quas cogitamus signa sint rerum" (XV.10.19) [V].
- "verbum quod foris sonat signum est verbi quod intus lucet, cui magis verbi competit nomen" (XV.11.20) [V]. NPNF: the inner word "has the greater claim to be called a word" [V].
- "Et sicut verbum nostrum fit vox nec mutatur in vocem, ita verbum dei caro quidem factum est, sed absit ut mutaretur in carnem" (XV.11.20) [V].
- Our word is the word "non de deo natae sed a deo factae imaginis dei" (XV.11.20) [V].

For the design: the word assumes a form as it assumes a voice, and is never changed into it. So the word takes no date; the date, the parent and the drift belong to the form (roadmap, "One word, many encodings").

### Sounds as signs, the same for all (Aristotle, Thomas)

- "Spoken words are the symbols of mental experience and written words are the symbols of spoken words" (*De interpretatione* 1) [V, Edghill]. The Greek's σύμβολα and σημεῖα, and the affections "ταὐτὰ πᾶσι" (16a3–8) [M].
- Thomas's lemmas: the signs "non est eadem apud omnes" (*In Peryerm.* I lect. 2 n. 8); the passions "sunt eaedem omnibus" and are "similitudines rerum" (n. 9) [V, Logic Museum].
- "voces significant intellectus conceptiones immediate et eis mediantibus res" (n. 5). They signify "ex institutione humana" (n. 4) [V].
- ST I q. 34 a. 1: "Vox enim significat intellectus conceptum, secundum Philosophum, in libro I Periherm." [V]. "Word" is said "primo et principaliter" of the interior concept; secondarily of the sound "interioris conceptus significativa"; thirdly of "ipsa imaginatio vocis" [V].
- I q. 34 a. 1 ad 3: in the Word "tota Trinitas verbo dicatur, et etiam omnis creatura" [V]. a. 3: the Word "is only expressive of what is in God the Father, but is both expressive and operative of creatures" [V, Dominican Fathers]. The Latin of a. 3 [M].

For the design: the likeness (concept to thing) is natural and the same for all, which is the order of reason. The sign (sound to concept, mark to sound) is by institution, which is an encoding's lexicon: a claim with a claimant and a date. Writing adds a layer: in an alphabet the mark signifies through the sound.

### Sense for sense (Jerome)

- "non verbum e verbo, sed sensum exprimere de sensu", save "absque scripturis sanctis, ubi et verborum ordo mysterium est" (*Ep.* 57, to Pammachius) [V, greek-language.gr; the section, 5, is M].

Two fidelities, named long before *Liturgiam authenticam* and *Magnum principium*: to the sense, and, in Scripture, to the very order of the words. The second gives a bridge a unit below the sentence.

### Babel and Pentecost

- Gen 11:1: "Erat autem terra labii unius, et sermonum eorumdem" [V, Clementine]. 11:7: "confundamus ibi linguam eorum, ut non audiat unusquisque vocem proximi sui" [V]. 11:9: "quia ibi confusum est labium universæ terræ" [V].
- Before Babel, the nations are already divided "unusquisque secundum linguam suam" (Gen 10:5; so 10:20, 31) [V].
- Acts 2:6: "audiebat unusquisque lingua sua illos loquentes" [V]; "ἤκουον εἷς ἕκαστος τῇ ἰδίᾳ διαλέκτῳ λαλούντων αὐτῶν" [V, Nestle 1904]. The crowd "mente confusa est" (συνεχύθη): Babel's verb, and Babel's "unusquisque" now hearing [V; the echo is my observation; the LXX's συγχέωμεν at Gen 11:7 is M].
- Acts 2:8: "linguam nostram in qua nati sumus" [V]. 2:11: "audivimus eos loquentes nostris linguis magnalia Dei" [V].
- Augustine, *Enarr. in Ps.* 54.11: "Through proud men, divided were the tongues; through humble Apostles, united were the tongues" [V, NPNF, numbered there Ps. LV §10]. "even among the diversity of tongues of flesh, one is the tongue in faith of heart" [V]. The Latin [M].
- Augustine, *Enarr. in Ps.* 44, on the queen's robe of many colours: "In the languages there is diversity; gold in the thoughts" [V, NPNF, numbered there Ps. XLV §22]. The Latin [M].
- Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 41.16: "ἐπαινετή μὲν καὶ ἡ παλαιὰ διαίρεσις τῶν φωνῶν", and "ἀξιεπαινετωτέρα δὲ ἡ νῦν θαυματουργουμένη" [V, PG 36.449–452, via Sullivan]. Babel's division was itself praiseworthy; Pentecost's is more so.
- Gregory asks whether each heard "ταῖς ἑαυτῶν διαλέκτοις", a miracle of the hearers, and prefers a miracle of the speakers: "Καθὰ καὶ μᾶλλον τίθεμαι" [V, Greek; the account of his reasons is the site's, S].

For the design: the many are kept, not undone (Augustine's robe; Gregory's "praiseworthy"). Gen 10 makes the division natural before Gen 11 makes it penal. And the Fathers ask where the bridge lives: in the speaker (a translation made) or in the hearer (each hears in his own). The VM needs both (Proposed design).

### Value and division (Saussure)

- French *mouton* "may have the same meaning as the English word sheep; but it does not have the same value" [S: Leithart, quoting an English translation, p. 114, edition unnamed]. English divides the animal (sheep) from the meat (mutton) [S: Calvet, *Universalis*, paraphrase].
- Before a tongue, thought is a "vague, shapeless mass" [S: Leithart, pp. 110–11]. The French (*Cours*, Part II, ch. IV) [M].
- Value is a fact of *langue*, signification of *parole* [S: Calvet].

For the design: value is why a bridge loses. A term's extent in one tongue is not its extent in another. But the thesis that thought is shapeless before a tongue is contrary to *De Trinitate* XV.10.19 and to *Peryerm.* n. 9. The step can take Saussure's value for the lexicon (the order of time) and refuse his thesis for the word (the order of reason), or run him as a school whose runtime constitutes the word by the division. That is a choice to declare.

## Analogy as a mode of translation

### The classical division

- *Categories* 1: equivocals share a name, but "the definition corresponding with the name differs for each" [V, Edghill]. Univocals "have both the name and the definition answering to the name in common" [V]. Derivatives "differ from it in termination" [V].
- ST I q. 13 a. 5: such names are said of God and creatures "secundum analogiam, idest proportionem" [V]. Either "multa habent proportionem ad unum" (healthy, of medicine and urine), or "unum habet proportionem ad alterum" [V]. The ratio is not one "sicut est in univocis; nec totaliter diversa, sicut in aequivocis" [V]. Said of man, "wise" "quodammodo circumscribit et comprehendit rem significatam"; said of God, it does not [V].
- Lateran IV, c. 2 (DH 806): "inter creatorem et creaturam non potest tanta similitudo notari, quin inter eos maior sit dissimilitudo notanda" [V, Fribourg lecture]. The council's own case is one word read two ways. "One" in Jn 17:22 "is understood first for the faithful, as implying a union of charity in grace", then for the persons, "as implying a unity of identity in nature" [V, Fordham, translator unnamed].
- Cajetan, *De nominum analogia* (1498): analogy of inequality, of attribution, of proportionality, proper or metaphorical [M]. Attribution is analogy "in which senses are related by priority and posteriority"; proportionality "relies on the comparison of two relations" [S: Ashworth, NDPR 2010, reviewing Hochschild]. Cajetan held proportionality "the only mode of analogy that is analogy in the strict sense", which his critics read as a departure from Thomas [S: SEP, D'Ettore and Ashworth]. Medieval analogues were "intermediary between chance equivocals and univocals", "deliberate equivocals" [S: SEP].

### What the checker already has

- `bridge(F, Primary, Secondary)`: a form "said first of the primary analogate and of the secondary by participation". `mode(F, Sort, Mode)`: "A bridge with no mode on a side licenses nothing on that side". `licensed/2` follows from both; a bridge with the creature as primary is `projection` [V, `l/kernel_analogy.l`].
- The lint: a rule whose body uses a divine-sorted and a creature-sorted predicate "must go through licensed/2, mode/3 or bridge/3" [V, the same file].

A crossing between two encodings can reuse the shape: a bridge between two forms, a mode on the guest side for each typing it carries, and a refusal for a crossing without one.

### The modes, proposed

Each is the mode of a rendering: a guest form F2 given for a host form F1, each under its encoding at its date.

- **univocal.** F2's ratio is F1's, by the guest's lexicon at its date: the names differ, the definition is one (*Categories* 1, across tongues). It carries every typing of F1. Example: *personne* for *persona* in RN 9.
- **analogical.** The ratio is neither one nor wholly diverse (I q. 13 a. 5). Declared with its kind:
  - *attribution*: F2 names something by its relation to F1's ratio ("personality", to the person);
  - *proportionality*: F2 names a like relation (impress is to wax as form is to matter);
  - *metaphor*: an improper proportionality.

  It carries a typing only where `carries/3` declares it on the guest side, as `mode/3` licenses a side. One kind per bridge; rival bridges are computed both ways.
- **equivocal.** The forms are paired (a cognate, a received rendering), but the ratios differ. Declared, it carries nothing and is reported. A guest reading that rests on a pair with no declared mode is refused, as an unbridged crossing is.
- **gap.** The guest has no term for F1's ratio. Every typing resting on it is lost in the guest and reported; nothing fills it. A periphrasis is a reading of its own.
- **Extent.** Within univocal or analogical, F2 covers more (*wider*) or less (*narrower*) of F1's ratio (Saussure; EuroWordNet's hyperonym and hyponym links). It carries the common part and reports the difference.
- **Readings kept, closed or opened.** A rendering keeps the host's readings, closes some (an interpretation), or opens new ones. "For all" closes *multi* to one reading (Case: "pro multis"). English "you" opens a number that Latin *te* fixes (Case: "We baptize").

A mode is an interpretive fact with a claimant. A mode stated by an authority (Arinze: "a faithful translation") is a claim, computed both ways and never fixing, as rule 14 treats a tier stated by an author.

- In the sentence before, "ad conservandam vitam maximeque ad perficiendam" is "the preservation of life, and for life's well-being" [V, `modernity-hearers.md`, correction 3].

- Finn: Bishop Hedley was "the principal English translator of the encyclical"; "it was Manning who determined the revisions" [S: Finn 2021, citing Leslie 1921]. McNabb later asked why "unjustly" was dropped from "a second translation purporting to be the 'official translation'" (1917) [S: Finn].

### What each encoding divides differently

- **forma.** The Latin hedges its own term: "velut formam quamdam", as it were a certain form. Every vernacular fetched gives a seal-word instead: impress, impronta, empreinte, huella [V]. "Impress" is the English of Aristotle's image of form. Sense receives "the sensible forms of things without the matter", as "a piece of wax takes on the impress of a signet-ring without the iron or gold" (*De anima* II.12, tr. J. A. Smith) [V; Bekker 424a17–21 M]. So the English does not simply drop the form: it gives the form's image. What it may lose is that the person's form, and not his mark, is in the field.
- **persona.** English and Italian give the abstract ("personality", "personalità"). French and Spanish keep the person ("sa personne", "su persona"). The Spanish makes the person the subject: "su persona dejó" [V].
- **perficiendam.** "maximeque ad perficiendam" becomes "for life's well-being": the end kept, the perfecting widened [V, as above].
- **applicat ad sese** becomes "makes his own" [V].

### "personality" at its dates

- Locke, *Essay* II.xxvii.26: "Person is a forensic term, appropriating actions and their merit" [V, York University teaching text, modernised, which omits the section's first sentences]. "This personality extends itself beyond present existence to what is past, only by consciousness" [V]. The early editions' "forensick" [M].
- Webster, 1828: "That which constitutes an individual a distinct person, or that which constitutes individuality" [V, 1828dictionary.com]. Its example adapts Locke's sentence, unattributed [V].
- Webster, 1913 (through GCIDE): "That which constitutes distinction of person"; "individuality" [V]. Coleridge: "Personality is individuality existing in itself, but with a nature as a ground" [V]. GCIDE's added gloss on "the externally evident aspects of the character" is marked as a later editor's [V, "+PJC"].
- Etymonline: late 14th century, "quality or fact of being a person", from Old French *personalité* and Medieval Latin *personalitas* [S]. 1795: "a distinctive essential character of a self-conscious being" [S]. 1889: "person whose character stands out from that of others" [S]. The OED was not reached.
- Illingworth, *Personality Human and Divine* (Bampton Lectures for 1894; Macmillan, October 1894): "the fundamental characteristic of personality is self-consciousness" [V]. Its meaning was "acquired by slow degrees", and "the Trinitarian controversies" are part of that history [V].
- Thomas: personality pertains to a thing's perfection "so far as it pertains to the dignity and perfection of that thing to exist by itself" (ST III q. 2 a. 2 ad 2) [V, Dominican Fathers]. Boethius: "naturae rationalis individua substantia" (*Contra Eutychen* 3; ST I q. 29 a. 1) [M].

So "personality" in the English of 1891 meant first what makes one a person, and two lexicons gave it: the scholastic (*personalitas*, subsisting by itself) and the Lockean (consciousness; "a forensic term"). The sense of a character's traits is later (1795 and 1889 in etymonline; not checked in the OED).

**The juridical side.** None. The Latin is the promulgated text (ASS 23 [M]). The vernaculars are no acts, and no approval governs them.

**For the modernity study.** The switch `rn9_in_english` becomes a hearing, `hears_in(G, en_1891)`, with these bridges. The Latin's own "velut … quamdam" makes the host's typing (formal, human) a metaphor in the host. Enter it as a further reading of rn9, and run both.

**A hypothesis, not documented.** The Latin was made from Italian drafts. If a draft said "impronta della sua personalità", as vatican.va's Italian does, then *forma personae* is the Latin translator's rendering, and the English follows the draft more closely than the Latin. Only Antonazzi 1957 can settle it.

- What stays in 1997: "Ius ad communicationem veritatis absolutum non est" (2488) [V]. "Caritas et veritatis observantia debent responsum determinare omni postulationi informationis vel communicationis" (2489) [V].

### Who changed it

- The Interdicasterial Commission: "Presided over by Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger, this Commission worked diligently to fulfill the mandate it received" [V, *Laetamur magnopere*]. The Latin "thus becomes the definitive text" [V]. "From Castel Gandolfo, August 15, 1997" [V].
- The letter lists no changes; a list of English corrections of 8 September 1997, 2483 among them, is attributed to the CDF by Catholic Culture; Ratzinger, 9 September 1997, counts "circa un centinaio" paragraphs corrected [V in `cases-usury-lying.md`, not re-fetched here].
- The clause "was deleted from the revised Catechism", having proved "more confusing than helpful for a number of the faithful" [S: Catholics United for the Faith, Catholic Exchange, 2007]. The Holy See gave no reason; Akin supposes it was to avoid adopting one theory of lying [S: EWTN News, 2024].

### What each encoding divides differently

- **This is not a translation.** The 1997 text changes the word. The definition loses a condition on the hearer, and the "right" moves to the norms on communicating the truth (2488–2489). The change is in the order of reason (what defines lying), not in a tongue.
- **The English and German on vatican.va are faithful encodings of the 1992 word**, not unfaithful encodings of the 1997 word. Their tongue does not say which edition they encode; only their parent does.
- **The order in time is the reverse of RN's.** The vernacular comes first, and the Latin last, as the definitive text.

### The proposed bridge

- `made_from(ccc_en_1994, ccc_fr_1992)`, univocal clause by clause. The German's parent is not documented [M].
- `revises(ccc_la_1997, ccc_fr_1992)`: a new edition of the source, not a rendering. No `renders/3` joins them.
- Reported: `superseded_encoding(ccc_en_vatican, ccc_la_1997)`, and the same for the German.

All three are proposals.

**The juridical side.** Which text is definitive is governance: *Laetamur magnopere* makes the Latin "the definitive text". Whether a lie needs a hearer with a right to the truth is a question of moral teaching. The deletion defines nothing, and law never answers it (`teaching_from_law`). The lying case of this study already types 1992 and 1997 as two sources (`cases-usury-lying.md`, l_cc1 and l_cc2). The language step adds which encoding, of which edition, a hearer reads.

The English of 2011 entered use in the United States "as of the First Sunday of Advent, November 27, 2011", after a letter of 23 June 2010 from Cardinal Cañizares, Prefect of the CDW [V, USCCB].

### The acts

- **Arinze, CDW, 17 October 2006** (Prot. no. 467/05/L; to the presidents of the conferences; the Congregation writes "At his direction", the Pope's) [V, USCCB]:
  - (a) Mt 26:28 and Mk 14:24 "make specific reference to 'many'", read "in connection with the words of the prophet Isaiah (53:11-12)" [V].
  - (b) "The Roman Rite in Latin has always said pro multis and never pro omnibus in the consecration of the chalice" [V].
  - (c) The Eastern anaphoras "contain the verbal equivalent of the Latin pro multis in their respective languages" [V].
  - (d) "For many is a faithful translation of pro multis"; "for all" is "rather an explanation of the sort that belongs properly to catechesis" [V].
  - (e) Salvation "is not brought about in some mechanistic way, without one's own willing or participation" [V].
  - (f) "effort should be made to be more faithful to the Latin texts of the typical editions" [V].
  - "the formula 'for all' would undoubtedly correspond to a correct interpretation of the Lord's intention" [V]. "It is a dogma of faith that Christ died on the Cross for all men and women" [V].
  - The conferences are to give "the necessary catechesis of the faithful on this matter in the next one or two years" [V].
- **Benedict XVI to Zollitsch, 14 April 2012.** "für alle" was "keine reine Übersetzung, sondern eine Interpretation" [V, vatican.va German]. It "sehr wohl begründet war und bleibt, aber doch schon Auslegung und mehr als Übersetzung ist" [V]. The Holy See decided that "pro multis" be translated "als solches" [V]. He sets Mk 14:24's "für viele" against Luke's and Paul's "für euch" [V]. Dated "Vatikanstadt 14. 4. 2012" [V]. The Polish: "nie było czystym tłumaczeniem, ale interpretacją" [V].
- ***Liturgiam authenticam***, dated 28 March 2001, in force 25 April 2001 [V]. The text "must be translated integrally and in the most exact manner" (n. 20) [V]. Translation is "not so much a work of creative innovation" (n. 20) [V]. It is "the text of the Latin editio typica itself that is to be translated" (n. 23) [V]. Some words "convey, in reality, a properly theological content" (n. 55) [V].
- ***Magnum principium***, 3 September 2017, in force "a die I mensis Octobris anni MMXVII" [V]:
  - c. 838 §2: the Apostolic See is to "aptationes, ad normam iuris a Conferentia Episcoporum approbatas, recognoscere" [V].
  - c. 838 §3: the conferences prepare and approve versions "fideliter et convenienter intra limites definitos accommodatas", and publish the books "post confirmationem Apostolicae Sedis" [V].
  - The preamble: "fidelitas non semper iudicari potest ex singulis verbis", but "in contextu ex toto communicationis actu" [V]. The sense of the original is to be rendered "plene et fideliter", "servata indole cuiusque linguae" [V]. The translations, "utpote partes ipsorum rituum", become with the Latin the Church's "vox" [V].
  - Roche's note: the recognitio "implies the process of recognising" adaptations; the confirmatio "pertains instead to the translations of liturgical texts" [V].
- **Sarah's commentary** (sent to the Pope at the end of September 2017) held the terms "interchangeable with respect to the responsibility of the Holy See" [S: Adoremus, 2017]. **Francis to Sarah, 15 October 2017**: they are not "strictly synonymous or interchangeable"; the confirmatio "no longer supposes a detailed word-by-word examination"; fidelity is "a triple fidelity" [S: Adoremus]. Corrected from `linguae-norms.md`, correction 3: in the Italian some numbers "sono stati abrogati o sono decaduti", with 76 and 80 as examples, and the rest "vanno attentamente ri-compresi, inclusi i nn. 79-84" [V, the Italian as printed by InfoVaticana]. Adoremus's "LA 79–84 have been abrogated" overstates it.
- The checker already encodes the governance side: `#program translation`, with `q(translation_approval)` between `detailed_recognitio` and `confirmatio`; `la2001`; `mp2017`; `sarah_2017` at tier 5; and `francis_sarah_2017` as the legislator's reading [V, `l/icon_history.l`].

### What each encoding divides differently

- Latin *multi* and Greek πολλοί say "many", open on how many. Semitic "the many" may mean all (Isaiah 53) [M; the exegetical thesis, Jeremias, M]. English "many" keeps the openness; "all" closes it.
- So "for all" is not an equivocation: Arinze calls its sense correct, and its content a dogma of faith. It is a rendering that closes a reading the host leaves open. The acts' own word for it is interpretation ("Auslegung").

### The proposed bridge

- `renders(en_2011_for_many, la_pro_multis, univocal)`: the host's readings kept.
- `renders(en_1973_for_all, la_pro_multis, univocal)` with `closes(en_1973_for_all, la_pro_multis, r_all)`: reported as `interpretation/3`.
- `mode_claim(arinze_2006, en_2011_for_many, la_pro_multis, univocal)` and `mode_claim(benedict_2012, de_fuer_alle, la_pro_multis, interpretation)`: claims at their sources' tiers, weighing and never fixing.

All three are proposals.

**The juridical side.** Who approves a translation is law: c. 838 §§2–3 as it read before 1 October 2017 and after. The checker computes it (`q(translation_approval)`). Whether a translation carries the word is not law's, and an approval never answers it: `law_answers(q(carries(missal_en_2011, pro_multis)))` is refused. Whether Christ died for all is a third question, doctrinal, answered by its own sources and not by the Missal's words either way. LA (the most exact rendering of the Latin) and MP (fidelity not always "ex singulis verbis") look for fidelity in different places. That difference is the governance side's to branch on. The language step takes no side, and bridges spans within the reading of the whole.

## Case: "We baptize"

### Texts by encoding and date

| Encoding | Form | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Latin, Florence, Decree for the Armenians (1439 [M]) | "Ego te baptizo in nomine Patris et Filii et Spiritus Sancti" | Latin M; English V |
| The Greek form, as Florence accepts it | "Baptizatur talis servus Christi in nomine Patris…", or "Baptizatur manibus meis talis…" | Latin M; English V |
| Byzantine Catholic, English, from the *Roman Malyj Trebnyk* (1952) | "The servant of God (name) is baptized in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit" | V, Metropolitan Cantor Institute |
| Italian, as the CDF reports its use | "a nome della comunità noi ti battezziamo" | V, the CDF's Note, Italian |
| English, the CDF's question (24 June 2020) | "We baptize you in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" | V |
| Latin, Zachary to Boniface (c. 746) | "Baptizo te in nomine patria. et filia et spiritus Sancti" | V, as printed by romanletters.org |

- Florence: "The form is: I baptize thee in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost" (DH 1314; Dz 696) [V, clerus.org Denzinger]. The sacrament is performed where "the act which is performed through the minister himself, is expressed with the invocation of the Holy Trinity" [V].
- In Robinson's translation: "the principal cause from which Baptism derives its virtue is the Holy Trinity"; "the instrumental cause is the minister who confers the sacrament externally"; and the second form, "By my hand N. is baptized…" [V, Fordham].

### The acts and the doctors

- **CDF, *Responsa*, 24 June 2020** (approved 8 June 2020; published 6 August 2020 [M]): the first answer is "Negative". Asked whether those so baptized "must be baptized in forma absoluta", the CDF answers "Affirmative" [V].
- **The Note**: the change was meant to "sottolineare il valore comunitario del Battesimo" [V]. "Tale assemblea però agisce ministerialmente – non collegialmente" [V]. "The minister is therefore the sign-presence of Him who gathers" [V]. He cannot declare that he acts "on behalf of the parents, godparents, relatives or friends" [V]. Saying "I baptize you…", "he does not speak as a functionary who carries out a role entrusted to him" [V]. It quotes SC 22 §3 [V].
- **Thomas, III q. 66 a. 5.** The form must express the cause: the principal, "the Blessed Trinity", and the instrumental, "the minister who confers the sacrament outwardly" [V, Dominican Fathers]. ad 1: the Greek form, "May the servant of Christ, N…, be baptized", is valid, since "the action performed by the minister is expressed with the invocation of the Trinity" [V].
- **III q. 67 a. 6.** If several say "We baptize thee", "the intention expressed is that several concur in conferring one Baptism", and such an intention "seems to annul the sacrament of Baptism" [V]. ad 3: "Nor could they say: 'We baptize thee': since neither baptizes" [V].
- **III q. 60 a. 7 ad 1.** "Et hic quidem sensus est idem apud omnes, licet non eaedem voces quantum ad sonum" [V]. "cuiuscumque linguae verbis proferatur talis sensus, perficitur sacramentum" [V]. The corpus: "verba se habent per modum formae, res autem sensibiles per modum materiae" [V].
- **ad 3.** A corruption at the start ("in nomine matris") takes the sense away; at the end ("patrias et filias") the words "nihil significent ex virtute impositionis, accipiuntur tamen ut significantia ex accommodatione usus" [V].
- **III q. 60 a. 8.** An addition or omission invalidates where "the essential sense of the words is destroyed", or where one intends "a rite other from that which is recognized by the Church" [V, translator unnamed].
- **Zachary, c. 746.** A priest "who knew no Latin at all" baptized with "patria … filia". If he "intended no error or heresy, but simply through ignorance made a slip in Latin", then "we cannot agree to a repetition of the baptismal rite" [V, Fordham's English, on romanletters.org]. Its Tangl and Denzinger numbers [M].
- **The checker today**: `#program we_baptize` gives `q(valid_form(baptism,we_baptize))`, doctrinal, answered "no" by `cdf_responsum_2020` at tier 3. The canon case `we_baptize_case` has `form_used(bap_we,we_baptize)` and `disputed(form_used(bap_doubt,we_baptize))` [V, `l/icon_history.l`, `l/canon_cases.l`]. A form is an atom there, not a form under an encoding.

### What each encoding divides differently

- By Florence and Thomas the substance is two features: the minister's act expressed, and the Trinity invoked. It is not "the first person singular".
- Latin marks the person in the verb (*baptizo*), so its "Ego" is emphatic [M, grammar]. English needs the pronoun. The Greek passive leaves the agent unsaid and expresses the act ("is baptized"); Florence's second form adds the agent ("manibus meis").
- So the Greek form has a gap on the agent, and the gap is no defect: the act is expressed. "We" fills the agent with another subject, the assembly acting collegially. That is a contrary on a feature of the substance.
- English "you" does not mark number, where Latin *te* does. The English opens a reading the Latin lacks, which the rite closes [proposal].

### The proposed bridge

- Features, typed on the word: `form_feature(F, minister_act, V)` with V one of `expressed`, `unexpressed`, `other_agent`; and `form_feature(F, trinity_invoked, yes|no)`. Each is read from the encoding's grammar, with the grammar as claimant.
- *Ego te baptizo* to "I baptize you": univocal on both features. To the Greek passive: univocal on the Trinity, a gap on the agent, the act expressed. To "We baptize you": equivocal on the agent, `other_agent`.
- A corrupt form ("patrias et filias") is read by accommodation of use. That is a reading of its own, entered with the place of the corruption (at the end the sense is kept; at the start it is lost), as Thomas divides it. It is ill-formed in its encoding and still read as the word.

All three are proposals.

**The juridical side.** Which features belong to the substance is teaching: `classification(Q, form(baptism,F), valid_form)`, and the CDF's response. Absolute baptism follows on the canon side. A recorded doubt whether "I" or "we" was said is computed both ways, and conditional baptism follows (c. 869 §1 [M]; the spoken doubt is the senses step's). Who approves a vernacular book is c. 838. That a vernacular form keeps the substance is checked against the features, never answered by the approval.

## Prior art

- **EuroWordNet** (Vossen, 1997). The Inter-Lingual-Index "starts off as an unstructured list or fund of WordNet1.5 synsets" [V]. "the language-specific wordnets will be stored as independent language-internal systems" [V]. Dutch *hoofd* and *kop*, more specific than "head", are linked to it by eq_hyperonym [V]. In Vossen's later survey (about 2002): EQ_SYNONYM, "a direct match between a synset and an ILI-record"; EQ_NEAR_SYNONYM; HAS_EQ_HYPERONYM and HAS_EQ_HYPONYM, "typically used if there is a gap in English"; and EQ_HAS_HOLONYM, EQ_CAUSES, EQ_ROLE, EQ_CO_ROLE [V].
  - Fit: the hyperonym and hyponym links are Saussure's division as data, and the proposal's extent.
  - Warning: the ILI is flat by design, and structure stays in each language's wordnet. The roadmap puts structure in the order of reason. Any structure laid on an interlingua is some encoding's: in EuroWordNet it would have been WordNet 1.5's, which is English.
- **The Collaborative Interlingual Index** (Bond et al., 2016) and the Open Multilingual Wordnet [M].
- **OntoLex-Lemon's vartrans module** (W3C Community Group, 2016 [M]). `vartrans:Translation` "connects ontolex:LexicalSense instances", typed by `vartrans:category` [V, through Moussallem et al.]. `trcat:culturalEquivalent` is "a translation between two entries that are not semantically but pragmatically equivalent" [V]. The module "was inspired by (Gracia et al., 2014)" [V]. `trcat:directEquivalent` and `trcat:lexicalEquivalent` [M].
  - Fit: a translation as a typed relation between senses, not forms, with a category. It is the nearest prior art to `renders/3`.
- **Annotation graphs** (Bird and Liberman): "a directed graph with fielded records on the arcs and optional time references on the nodes" [V, arXiv cs/0010033]. It is "capable of representing multiple hierarchies" and "committed to the acyclic graph model" [V]. The journal publication, *Speech Communication* 33 (2001) [M].
  - Fit: for a written form the anchors are character offsets in a text. Arcs are forms, terms and spans, labelled by encoding. An alignment of two texts is a set of arcs between their graphs, and a span is read within the reading of its whole (roadmap, "A part is no whole").
- **Identifiers.** BCP 47 (RFC 5646) tags a tongue with its script and region [M]. ISO 639-3 gives dated varieties their own codes: `ang` (Old English), `enm` (Middle English), `eng` [M].
  - Fit: an encoding is a tag with a span of years.
- **Genealogies.** Glottolog classifies languages in trees, setting mixed languages apart [M]. The roadmap's DAG, with contact declared, is the stronger model. Etymology as typed links between words: de Melo's Etymological WordNet (2014) and EtymDB 2.0 (Fourrier and Sagot, 2020) [M].
- **Interlinguas.** UNL's Universal Words [M]; Wierzbicka's semantic primes, a proposed common core of every tongue [M].
  - Fit: each is a claim to the order of reason, entered with its claimant, never taken as that order.
- **Formal and dynamic equivalence.** Nida (1964) [M], behind *Comme le prévoit* (1969) [M], to which *Liturgiam authenticam* answers [M, as a reading].

## Proposed design

### Sorts

- `encoding` (open): a written encoding, a tongue in a script over a span of years (`la_1891`, `en_1891`, `en_2026`, `fr_1992`), with a BCP 47 tag.
- `text` (open): one encoding of one edition of a source (`rn_en_vatican`, `ccc_fr_1992`, `ccc_la_1997`, `missal_en_2011`).
- `form` (open): a written form, as given, at a locus of a text.
- `word` (open): a node of the order of reason. No slot gives it a date or a place in the order of time.
- `tmode` {univocal, analogical, equivocal, gap}; `akind` {attribution, proportionality, metaphor}; `extent` {same, wider, narrower}.

### Slots

- `encoding(E, Tag, From, To)` and `text_of(T, Source, E, Year)`.
- `made_from(T2, T1)`: a translation's parent, in the order of time. Several parents are allowed, as `derives_from/2` allows them for a rite.
- `revises(T2, T1)`: a new edition, so a new word. It is never a rendering.
- `form_in(F, T, Locus, String)`.
- `lexicon(E, String, W, Claimant)`: how an encoding reads a string, by a claimant (a dictionary at its date, a grammar, a corpus). Lexicons that disagree are computed both ways.
- `renders(F2, F1, M)`, with `analogy_kind(F2, F1, K)` and `extent(F2, F1, X)`. On an analogical bridge, `carries(F2, F1, Typing)`.
- `closes(F2, F1, R)` and `opens(F2, F1, R)`: readings of the host a rendering cuts or adds.
- `mode_claim(C, F2, F1, M)`: a mode stated by an author. Computed both ways, never fixing.
- `hears_in(G, E)`: a guest horizon hears in an encoding.
- `form_feature(F, Feature, Value)`: what a form expresses under its encoding's grammar (sacramental forms).

### Reports

- `carried_in(A, E, Typing)`, and `lost_in(A, E, Typing, Why)` with Why one of `gap`, `equivocal`, `no_mode`.
- `interpretation(F2, F1, R)`: a rendering that closes the host to the reading R.
- `superseded_encoding(T, T2)`: T encodes an edition that T2 revises.
- Violations:
  - `equivocation_undeclared(F2, F1)`: a guest reading on a pair with no mode;
  - `word_dated(W)`: a word given a date or a parent in time, as `procession_in_time`;
  - `made_before_parent(T2, T1)` and `translation_cycle(T)`, as `rite_from_later` and `rite_descends_from_itself`;
  - `analogy_without_kind(F2, F1)`;
  - `lexicon_out_of_span(E, String, Year)`.
- The pair: `law_answers(q(carries(T, F)))` is refused. Approval belongs to c. 838; that a text carries the word is not law's.

### How the modernity VM uses it

- With `hears_in(vm_R, E)`, the guest's availability is computed on `carried_in/3` for E, not on the host's typing. `guest_without_reason` and `guest_sole_reason` take the encoding.
- Loss by horizon and loss by encoding are reported apart. A Thomist guest hearing in en_2026 loses rn9's form by encoding alone. A Lockean guest hearing in Latin loses it by horizon alone.
- `rn9_in_english` retires into the en_1891 bridges, with the impress bridge disputed (proportionality or gap), so both run.
- The host's typing of rn9 gains the Latin's "velut … quamdam" as a further reading (metaphor), run beside the literal one.
- New predictions to commit: rn9 heard in French and Spanish (the person kept) against English and Italian ("personality"); the English of 1891 against the English of 2026.

### What challenges the design

1. **The host already speaks by analogy.** "velut formam quamdam" hedges the Latin's own form-term. "An argument is typed as what it says, which no tongue owns" then needs a mode inside the host, or a metaphor is typed as literal.
2. **Gap or analogy is itself disputed.** "Impress" is the English image of form (*De anima* II.12). Whether the English drops the form or gives its image is a reading, and both should run.
3. **A form changes its word with its date.** "personality" was "That which constitutes an individual a distinct person" in 1828, and self-consciousness for Illingworth in 1894; the traits come later. An encoding must be dated (en_1891, en_2026), and two lexicons of one date (scholastic, Lockean) compete.
4. **A change of edition is not a translation.** CCC 2483 changed the word, and vatican.va still serves the old word in English and German. A text names its edition; its tongue does not.
5. **A true rendering can still be no translation.** Arinze and Benedict call "for all" a correct interpretation, not a translation. The four modes relate terms; an interpretation closes readings. The design needs the branch accounting as well as the modes.
6. **The unit of a bridge is disputed in law.** LA asks for the most exact rendering of the typical edition; MP says fidelity is not always judged "ex singulis verbis". The language step should bridge spans within the whole, and leave the dispute to the governance side.
7. **A gap is no defect; a contrary is.** Florence accepts the Greek passive, where no "I" is said; the CDF refuses "we". The substance is in the features, not in a string.
8. **Ill-formed is not unreadable.** Thomas reads "patrias et filias" "ex accommodatione usus", and Zachary would not rebaptize. The roadmap's "ill-formed where the form is not one the encoding admits at that date" needs a reading by accommodation, typed by where the corruption falls.
9. **Saussure is half usable.** His value gives the lexicon its division. His "vague, shapeless mass" before a tongue denies the word of no tongue. Declare which is taken, or run him as a school.
10. **Interlinguas are flat for a reason.** EuroWordNet keeps the ILI unstructured so that no one tongue's structure governs it. The order of reason the checker builds is itself an encoding (roadmap), so each of its nodes and edges needs a claimant.
11. **Where the bridge lives.** Gregory puts Pentecost's miracle in the speakers; Acts says each heard. A made translation is a text, with a parent and an approver. A hearing is a reading through a guest's lexicon. `made_from/2` and `hears_in/2` must stay apart.
12. **A mode: fact or claim?** Typing modes like the empirical kind (no act settles one) would refuse Arinze's reasons as an act answering a fact. Typing them as claims, in rule 14's shape, lets them weigh. This record proposes claims; the choice is open.
