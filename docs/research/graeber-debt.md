## What Graeber claims

### Three moral logics

- **Mauss, first.** "working very much in the Maussian tradition" (GP2011) [V]. Mauss saw several principles at work in every society at once, not reciprocity alone, and economics reduces them all to exchange (GP2011) [V, paraphrase as returned]. G2010's subtitle is "A Maussian Approach" [V].
- **Baseline communism.** Communism is any relation run on "from each according to their abilities, to each according to their needs" (G2010) [V]; "I call this 'baseline communism'" [V]. It is "the foundation of all human sociability" (G2010) [V]; "in a way the basis of all social relations" (GP2011) [V]. It shows where need is extreme, as with the drowning stranger, or the cost is slight, as with a light or a screwdriver asked of a coworker (GP2011) [V, as returned]. It is not "basically about property relations" (G2010) [V].
- **Exchange.** Exchange is between equals and seeks equivalence. It lets the parties "call it even" and so end the relation, and an exact equivalent suggests one "wishes to end the relationship" (G2010) [V]. With vendors "one is often just pretending to have a relationship at all" [V]. Against baseline communism it is a closing: "If communism could be imagined as a kind of permanent mutual debt", exchange settles it (G2010) [V].
- **Hierarchy.** Hierarchy works "by a logic of precedent", "the opposite of reciprocity" (G2010) [V]. Gifts up or down need no return, and a superior's gift may call for another (GP2011) [V, as returned].
- **Debt.** Debt is "a promise that can be quantified by means of money", and so impersonal and transferable, where an obligation is vague, like "I owe you one" (GP2011) [V, as returned]. Exact equivalents arose from violence: compensation for killing, war, plunder, slavery (GP2011) [V, paraphrase]. Morality comes first; the question is how moral relations became quantified debts (GP2011) [V, paraphrase].
- **Violence and equivalence.** Social currencies rearrange relations among persons; "human economies" are those "in which social currencies predominate" (G2012) [V]. Their payments acknowledge what cannot be paid, and only violence makes a person exchangeable, by ripping him from "the web of unique human relations" (G2012) [V].

### Money against the barter myth

- **The claim.** There is "no known example of a human society whose economy is based on barter" (G2009) [V]; "No one has ever found an economy based on barter" (G2011b) [V]. G2011i quotes the sentence "No example of a barter economy, pure and simple, has ever been described" [V]; that it is Caroline Humphrey's (1985) is [M].
- **The order reversed.** The textbook story, from Adam Smith, has barter beget money and money credit (G2011i) [V, paraphrase]. Graeber's order is credit, then money, then barter: "Before there was money, there was debt" (MH2014) [V]; WD reports the same reversal [S]. In the first age money was "virtual credit money", reckoned in silver and often paid in barley (G2009) [V, as returned]; Mesopotamian silver became a unit of account in temple administration (G2011i) [V, as returned].
- **Coinage, not money, for war.** "Coinage, certainly, was not invented to facilitate trade" (G2009) [V]; it was first made to pay soldiers (G2009) [V, paraphrase]. "Coinage is invented to pay soldiers", and markets grew up beside military camps (G2011b) [V].
- **Against it.** WD reports Huato, Selgin and Hummel defending the barter account or charging errors of fact (Menger confused with his son), and DeLong's charge of mistakes [S]. None was fetched in its own wording [M].

### The ages, and the axial age

- **The periodization** (G2009) [V, headings as returned]: I, "Age of the First Agrarian Empires (3500–800 BCE)", virtual credit money; II, "Axial Age (800 BCE — 600 CE)", coinage and bullion, with the world religions and a period of war; III, "The Middle Ages (600 CE — 1500 CE)", "The return to virtual credit-money"; IV, "Age of European Empires (1500–1971)", "The return of precious metals"; V, "Current Era (1971 onwards)", "The empire of debt", from the end of the dollar's convertibility into gold. That the book ends the Middle Ages at 1450 is [M].
- **The axial age.** Jaspers's term, so footnoted (G2009) [V]. Coinage spread with the world religions, in war, and those religions arose partly as reactions against the market's logic (G2009) [V, paraphrase]. WD names Graeber's "military–coinage–slave complex": armies paid in coin, cities looted, captives enslaved, and taxes in coin driving the poor into debt [S]. The phrase is not in any text fetched [M for its wording].
- **The Middle Ages.** Commodity markets and the world religions began to merge; trade ran through networks overseen by religious authority; the Church limited interest and banned debt bondage; Islam forbade interest while honouring commerce; Chinese Buddhism made the pawnshop against local usurers (G2009) [V, paraphrase as returned]. G2011b: Christianity and Islam banned interest in the Middle Ages [V, paraphrase]. What *Debt* ch. 10 says of Ambrose and Basil is reported only by a blog summary [S: Melanie Tan Uy, *Frontalobe*, "Chapter 10 (Part 7)"], with Ambrose's tie of usury to just war taken from Barry Gordon [S, same].

### Clean slates and the jubilee

- **The clean slate.** Each new ruler would "wipe the slate clean, cancel all debts, and declare a general amnesty" (G2009) [V]. Rulers decided that the only way to avert social breakdown was "to declare a clean slate" (GP2011) [V]. The first word for freedom is Sumerian "amarga", "return to mother" (G2009) [V; the book's spelling *amargi* is M].
- **The jubilee.** For G2009 the jubilee is the same custom in Israel, at seven years (correction 3) [V]. GP2011 lists clean slates, jubilees and the medieval laws against usury together, as protections for debtors [V]. In an age of virtual money there must be protections "not just ones to protect creditors" (GP2011) [V, as returned].
- **Hudson, whom Graeber follows.** Clean slates were pragmatic, not utopian; usury was not banned but "its effects were reversed" (H2024) [V]. *Dĕrôr* is cognate with *andurārum*, whose root meaning is "to move freely, as running water" (H2024) [V]. Modern renderings of *amargi*, *andurarum* and *misharum* are "an ideological Rorschach test" (H2024) [V]. Hudson's *… and forgive them their debts* (2018) is [M].
- **Today.** "After the Jubilee" asks for "a grand jubilee for all personal debt up to say, $100,000?" (G2012j) [V]. WD reports that *Debt* recommends a jubilee that makes debts unenforceable, with no bailout [S]. That *Debt*'s last chapter calls for a "Biblical-style Jubilee" in those words is [M].
- **Two structures in one chapter.** Lev 25 holds both: the release of the fiftieth year (25:10) and the ban on usury to the brother, "ne accipias usuras ab eo, nec amplius quam dedisti"; "pecuniam tuam non dabis ei ad usuram" (25:36–37) [V, Vulgate]. Hudson's Mesopotamia had the first without the second [V]. So a release reverses the effects of debt, and a prohibition forbids an act. They are different types, and the usury case must keep them apart (below).

### Redemption (ch. 4)

- **What is fetched.** The publisher: the language of ancient law and religion, "words like 'guilt,' 'sin,' and 'redemption'", comes from ancient debates about debt (MH2014) [V]. GP2011: the great religions took "reckoning, redemption, karmic accounting" from ancient finance, having no other language, and often used it to "turn it into its opposite": debts are not sacred, and forgiving them is [V, as returned].
- **Nietzsche.** For Graeber, Nietzsche starts from Smith's calculating man, so that morality would begin in exchange and justice in punishing the debtor. Graeber finds "zero evidence for any of this", and suspects a provocation to bourgeois readers (GP2011) [V, as returned]. Nietzsche's own text, in Samuel's translation: the moral idea "ought" (*Schuld*) "originates from the very material idea of 'owe'" (*Genealogy* II §4) [V; the German words are M]; and God "paying himself personally out of a pound of his own flesh" (II §21) [V].
- **The chapter itself.** That ch. 4 is "Cruelty and Redemption" [S: the titles of two web summaries]. Its argument from memory: Nietzsche's creditor and his pleasure in cruelty; *redemptio* as buying back what was pawned; the Christian story taking the market's words and then breaking them, since a debt to God cannot be paid [M, every item]. The unpayable debt is verified in G2012, above [V].
- **Aramaic and Hebrew.** "In Aramaic to forgive a sin is to remit" a debt owed to you, and Matthew's Greek follows the Aramaic idiom (Anderson, A2009) [V, the second as returned]. On Hebrew, correction 4.

## The texts the typing reads

- **Aristotle, NE V.5.** "Money, then, acting as a measure, makes goods commensurate and equates them" [V]. It is called *nomisma* "because it exists not by nature but by law (nomos)", and can be changed or made useless [V; the rest as returned]. Money is "as it were our surety" for a future need [V].
- **Aristotle, *Politics* I.10.** "The most hated sort, and with the greatest reason, is usury, which makes a gain out of money itself, and not from the natural object of it. For money was intended to be used in exchange, but not to increase at interest" [V, classicalwisdom.com, translator not named; the wording is Jowett's, M]. In Ellis's version, "of all forms of money-making it is most against nature" [V, ellopos.net]. *Politics* I.8–9, the limit of true riches and wealth-getting "by experience and art", was not reached [M].
- **Thomas, I-II q. 2 a. 1.** Artificial riches are "quibus secundum se natura non iuvatur, ut denarii"; "ars humana eos adinvenit", "for the convenience of exchange" [V, Latin and English, aquinas.cc]. "Divitiae autem artificiales non quaeruntur nisi propter naturales" [V]. Natural riches are sought "propter aliud", to sustain man's nature, and are "propter hominem facta" [V].
- **Thomas, I-II q. 57 a. 4.** Art is the "right reason of things to be made", prudence the "right reason of things to be done" [V, Dominican Fathers, ccel.org]. Making passes into outward matter; doing abides in the agent [V]. The good of things made by art "is not the good of man's appetite, but the good of those things themselves" [V].
- **Thomas, II-II q. 77 aa. 1–2.** The quality of a thing in human use "is measured by the price given for it, for which purpose money was invented" (a. 1, citing *Ethic.* V.5) [V]. Price does not follow a thing's grade of nature, for "a horse fetches a higher price than a slave", "but it depends on their usefulness to man" (a. 2 ad 3, after Augustine) [V].
- **Thomas, II-II q. 78 a. 1** [V, Latin and English, aquinas.cc]:
  - "accipere usuram pro pecunia mutuata est secundum se iniustum".
  - In things "quarum usus est ipsarum rerum consumptio", to sell the use apart is to sell "id quod non est", and so "inaequalitas constituitur, quae iustitiae contrariatur".
  - "Pecunia autem, secundum philosophum, in V Ethic. et in I Polit., principaliter est inventa ad commutationes faciendas"; its "proprius et principalis … usus" is its spending in exchange.
  - "secundum se est illicitum pro usu pecuniae mutuatae accipere pretium, quod dicitur usura".
  - The sed contra is Ex 22:25; Lk 6:35 is objection 4, and the reply reads it as a counsel [V]. Usury from foreigners (Deut 23:20) "non fuit eis concessum quasi licitum" (ad 2) [V].
  - Human laws let usury go unpunished "propter conditiones hominum imperfectorum", "ne impedirentur utilitates multorum" (ad 3) [V]. That is rule 1 in Thomas: the law permits what teaching does not approve.
- **Hobbes.** *Lev.* XV: the just value is "that which they be contented to give", and commutative justice as equality of value is mocked: "As if it were injustice to sell dearer than we buy" [V]. The Introduction: by art is made the Leviathan, "which is but an artificial man", "for whose protection and defence it was intended" [V, Standard Ebooks]. *Lev.* X, "The Value, or WORTH of a man, is as of all other things, his Price", is [M]: the page was not permitted in this pass.
- ***Vix pervenit* §3** (1 Nov. 1745, to the bishops and ordinaries of Italy) [V, newadvent.org]. Usury has its place "in a loan contract", which "demands, by its very nature," that one return "only as much as he has received" [V]. Gain beyond the principal is "illicit and usurious" [V]. Other titles may run beside a loan, "which are not at all intrinsic to the contract" (III) [V]. The Italian on vatican.va: the loan "per sua propria natura chiede soltanto che sia restituito quanto fu prestato" [V]; the titles are "non del tutto connaturati ed intrinseci" to its nature [V]. The Latin, *suapte natura*, is [M].

### The case for

1. **Thomas names art, and keeps it apart from will.** Money is made by art, "ars humana eos adinvenit" (I-II q. 2 a. 1) [V]. Art is reason about things to be made, not things to be done, and its good is "not the good of man's appetite" (I-II q. 57 a. 4) [V]. Typed (final, will), money's end would be the user's desire, the usurer's own domain. The work's end (*finis operis*) and the worker's end (*finis operantis*) would collapse. (The terms are [M]; their locus, *In II Sent.* d. 1 q. 2 a. 1, was not fetched.)
2. **Aristotle grounds money in neither nature nor desire.** It is "not by nature but by law (nomos)" (NE V.5) [V], and usury is wrong because money "was intended to be used in exchange" (*Pol.* I.10) [V]. An instituted end is not a natural end and not a desire.
3. **No domain fits the premise.** Of the seven domains, `nature` is physics, `human` is human nature, `will` is intending and choosing, `self` is self-preservation, and `reason` is Kant's law-giving reason [V, `modernity-preregistration.md`, "The library"]. None names factive reason.

### The case against

1. **The dependency it was meant to meet is gone.** The trace mode needed `art` to tell the counterfeit from the trace. TTC-LOCI tells them apart on the deposit, on all 42 runtimes, with no domain (correction 7) [V].
2. **The thing made is already typed.** RERUM's `makes/3`, `artifact/1`, `cause_of/3` and `end_of_use/2` hold money as a thing, the work's end as `cause_of(money,final,exchange)` and the worker's as `end_of_use(money,gain)` (a proposal, using slots that exist [V]). The distinction the case for asks for is kept at the level of the thing. The library of causes types arguments, not things.
3. **In Thomas the end of art is not terminal.** Artificial riches are sought only for natural ones, and these for man (I-II q. 2 a. 1) [V]. Price follows "usefulness to man" (q. 77 a. 2 ad 3) [V]. Exchange for need "supplies a natural need" (q. 77 a. 4) [V in `modernity-mammon.md`; M here]. So every argument of Thomas's from money's end rests at last on (final, human). The usury argument rests on the object, (formal, will) (correction 6), and *Vix pervenit* on the loan's own nature [V], also the object.
4. **No horizon traces it, so it separates no one.** Every modern node holds that made things have ends. Hobbes makes the state itself by art, "an artificial man", for an intended end (Introduction) [V]. If `ends_of_art` is held everywhere, it is an order parameter of no case (PH3) and only enlarges `horizons_all` from 1,024 to 4,096 (2 to the 12th; arithmetic).
5. **Held at Hobbes, it would misreport.** Typed (final, art), Aristotle's argument against usury would be available at `hobbes`, who holds that value is "measured by the appetite of the contractors" (*Lev.* XV) [V] and mocks the equality of thing and thing [V]. Typed (final, human), it is lost at `hobbes`, which is the right report. To exclude `ends_of_art` at `hobbes` would need a locus, and his Introduction is against it [V]. Hobbes's difference from Thomas on money is already the study's difference between `ends_of_human_nature` and `ends_desired` (this is a reading).
6. **Graeber's dispute is about a fact, not a cause.** He denies the premise that money "principaliter est inventa ad commutationes" (q. 78 a. 1) [V]: "Coinage, certainly, was not invented to facilitate trade" (G2009) [V], and credit came first (MH2014) [V]. That is a dispute inside a premise both sides could hold, and the roadmap already owes "a rebuttal slot, for a dispute about the object inside a cause both sides hold" [V, `roadmap.md`]. Graeber's own case against debt argues from human sociality and the incommensurability of persons (G2010, G2012) [V], (final, human), on a reading. A domain for making types neither.

### Recommendation

Do not add `art` now, neither the domain nor its two parts. Type money as a thing in RERUM's slots, its office as substitute in LOCI's slot (done), and the arguments from or against it in the existing domains: the object (formal, will), human need (final, human), the divine command (final, god_ethics). The usury case need not wait on this decision.

Reopen it on one condition: a case shows an argument whose premise is the end of a made thing (an institution's, a law's, money's) that does not reduce to a human end or to the act's object, together with a figure, on a locus, who holds those two parts and traces that premise. If the encoders want the typing labelled before then, the cheaper form is a domain the library holds in every horizon, as it holds the efficient and material causes, with no part. `horizons_all` would stay at 1,024. Such a domain could change no report, and rule 5 would still need a test that fails without it.

This disagrees with the record of 24 October and with `modernity-mammon.md` lines 136, 258, 352 and 357, which assumed the domain.

## Graeber's place in the DAG

### How hearers and runtimes are declared

- **A runtime** is a school's parts loaded whole, declared in `schools.l` by `runtime R extends P1, P2` with `add` and `exclude` [V, `schools.l`]. An edge must be documented, and a contested or undocumented edge is marked in a comment [V, `schools.l` 102–108].
- **Leo's hearers** are the figures *Rerum novarum* addressed or argued against [V, `modernity-hearers.md` opening]. "Their own parents outside the DAG traced over", each node rests on `default`, `thomist` or `locke` with the exclusions its loci warrant, "a first approximation" [V, `schools.l` 246–247; `modernity-preregistration.md`, "Leo's hearers"]. `marx extends default` and excludes `ends_in_nature`, `forms_in_nature`, `ends_by_god_physics`, `ends_by_god_ethics` and `soul_as_form` [V, `schools.l` 271–273]. `lassalle extends default` and excludes God's ends only, "by absence; weak" [V, 279–281].
- **Secondary readers enter as claimants, not runtimes.** Fortin's reading of RN 9 is `concludes_heard(rn9, lex_en_locke_1891, fortin_1992)` [V, `modernity-preregistration.md`, "The case"]. McCarraher's is `substitutes(mccarraher_2019, …)` [V, `icon_history.l`].

### Graeber's documented relations

- **Mauss: the strongest edge.** "working very much in the Maussian tradition" (GP2011) [V]. "Mauss was also a revolutionary socialist", who ran a consumer cooperative (G2004) [V, the second as returned]. The afterword of 2014 says he "wanted to write the sort of book Mauss might have written" [S: WD]. The three logics are Mauss's several principles (GP2011) [V, paraphrase]. Mauss is not a node.
- **Marx: a hedged edge, and a formula re-read against its author.** He counts himself among "libertarian, practice-oriented Marxists including, arguably, myself" (G2004) [V]. He sets Marxism, a "theoretical or analytical discourse about revolutionary strategy", against anarchism, "an ethical discourse about revolutionary practice" (G2004) [V]. WD has him "reconciling Marx and Mauss" [S]. He takes Marx's formula from the *Critique of the Gotha Programme* as the definition of communism (G2010) [V]. He denies that it is "basically about property relations" (G2010) [V], against the *Manifesto* and Erfurt's social property in the means of production [V in `modernity-hearers.md`, not fetched again: M here]. No reading by Graeber of Marx on a cause in a domain was found.
- **Polanyi: a reviewer's comparison.** He is named in none of G2009, G2010, GP2011, G2011b or G2012 [V, by absence]. Gillian Tett compared the book to Mauss, Polanyi and Keith Hart [S: WD].
- **Nietzsche: a foil.** "zero evidence for any of this" (GP2011) [V].

### Hearer, child runtime, or neither

- **Not a hearer.** A hearer in this study is someone a source addressed or argued against. No source the study enters addressed Graeber: not RN (1891), not *Vix pervenit* (1745), not the 1917 Code. He reads those sources as a historian, which is what Fortin and McCarraher do, and they are claimants.
- **Not yet a child of `marx`.** A child inherits a horizon, and the edge must carry a reading on causes. Graeber's documented readings are of Mauss's principles, of Marx's formula re-read as a relation, and of Nietzsche refused: none is on a cause. No locus fetched shows him holding or tracing any of the ten parts. His sociality, "the foundation of all human sociability" (G2010) [V], could be read as (final, human), which `marx` holds. His person as a "web of unique human relations" (G2012) [V] could be read as Thesis VI, which `marx_social` takes to exclude it [V, `schools.l` 275–277]. That is two readings, not a node. A `graeber` node would compute what `marx` or `lassalle` computes, and would add no order parameter (PH3).
- **Recommendation: neither.** Enter Graeber as a claimant in three places:
  - his rebuttal of the premise on money's origin, in the usury case (below);
  - his reading of the medieval laws against usury as protections for debtors (GP2011) [V];
  - his three logics, as relations in the Grammar of the Icon, following the mapping in `modernity-mammon.md`.

  If the usury case later wants a modern guest in the VM, run `marx` or `lassalle` and say so. If a node is still wanted, the precedent is `smith extends locke` with nothing of its own: `graeber extends marx` with no `add` or `exclude`, marked `-- undocumented edge: graeber <- marx (no reading on a cause; the formula re-read as a relation)`. The record must then say that it computes what `marx` computes.

This disagrees with the record of 24 October, which proposed Graeber as a hearer-style node on `marx`. On the evidence, "hearer" is the wrong kind, and a node on `marx` is idle.

### What it leaves open

1. **Graeber's rebuttal needs a slot.** It denies q. 78 a. 1's premise that money was "invented" for exchange (G2009, MH2014) [V], and denies it as fact. The roadmap's rebuttal slot is owed. Until it exists, the rebuttal cannot be entered without being mistaken for a trace.
2. **Graeber may be reading Aristotle against Aristotle.** Money as a unit of account fits NE V.5's measure "by law" [V] better than it fits *Politics* I.9's origin of money in the exchange of necessaries [M]. That is my reading.
3. **The modern collapse of the barrenness premise was not fetched.** Calvin's letter of 1545 (money buys a field), Locke's *Some Considerations* (1691) on interest as rent, and Bentham's *Defence of Usury* (1787) on liberty of contract are all [M]. 4. **The extrinsic titles.** *Vix pervenit*'s "not at all intrinsic to the contract" [V] (*lucrum cessans*, *damnum emergens*, [M]) may need their own typing, perhaps (efficient, will), for the arguments behind c. 1543.
5. **Releases are not prohibitions.** The jubilee and the clean slate release a debt; they are not arguments on either question. They belong to the Grammar's proposed `releases/3` (`modernity-mammon.md`, sketch) and must not be entered as reasons against usury.
